# Exemplars to design against · `reg:exemplars:reference-register`

This register names the reference systems whose choices the engine borrows or deliberately refuses, and the reference problems whose answers are known, so that every bite proves itself against something outside itself. Each entry states what it exemplifies, what to take, what to refuse, and which bite of the plan it binds.

## Reference systems · `sec:exemplars:reference-systems`

### Verified linear algebra · `sec:exemplars:verified-linear-algebra`

**Example (INTLAB)** · `ex:exemplars:intlab`

Rump's MATLAB toolbox for verified computation is the direct ancestor of the certified solve: an approximate inverse, a validated bound on the norm of the identity minus its product with the matrix, and a Neumann-series enclosure of the inverse norm. Take the certificate shape and its honesty about what a failed certificate does not prove; refuse the interactive-environment ergonomics, which the engine replaces with typed results. Take residual and inverse-norm expressions as obligations for algebraic dimension checks; refuse substituting a standard uncertainty for a deterministic input radius merely because their dimensions match (`def:vision:dimensions`), (`req:vision:algebraic-unit-checks`), (`rule:vision:uncertainty-unit-algebra`). Binds (`rule:vision:conditioning-and-refusal`), (`dec:vision:primary-error-model`), (`tab:vision:error-model-operation-composition`), (`def:vision:certified-solve-contract`), (`dec:vision:inverse-norm-certificate`), (`rule:vision:solves-and-inverses`), (`def:vision:operation-result-and-failure`), (`step:nrz:bite-numerical-feasibility`), (`step:nrz:bite-certified-matrix-core`).

**Example (Arb)** · `ex:exemplars:arb`

Johansson's ball arithmetic library carries every value as a midpoint and a radius and propagates rigorous bounds through arithmetic and matrix functions. Take the center-plus-radius companion as the public value shape and the discipline of a rigorous bound per operation; refuse arbitrary precision as a first capability, which the architecture admits only behind evidence. For unit-bearing values, take the intrinsic deterministic companion shape and require each uncertainty expression’s own dimension, measure and kind; refuse inferring that relative uncertainty, variance, or an affine point’s uncertainty shares the center’s full descriptor (`def:vision:dimensions`), (`def:vision:measures`), (`def:vision:intrinsic-uncertainty-companions`), (`rule:vision:uncertainty-unit-algebra`). Binds (`dec:vision:precision-selection`), (`tab:vision:precision-alternatives`), (`def:vision:admissible-values`), (`dec:vision:primary-error-model`), (`tab:vision:error-model-comparison`), (`tab:vision:error-model-operation-composition`), (`def:vision:error-companion-surface`), (`rule:vision:arithmetic-closure`), (`rule:vision:matrix-function-guarantees`), (`step:nrz:bite-units-and-foundations`), (`step:nrz:bite-advanced-matrix-domains`).

### Error analysis as doctrine · `sec:exemplars:error-analysis-doctrine`

**Example (Higham's Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms)** · `ex:exemplars:higham-accuracy-and-stability`

Nicholas J. Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, second edition, SIAM, 2002. Take the backward-error vocabulary, the distinction between normwise and componentwise analysis, and the dependence of conditioning on scaling as a reference for the engine's bounds. The engine requires each norm, perturbation and relative weight to name its coordinates and physical interpretation (`rule:nrz:shape-norms-and-conditioning`), (`def:nrz:numerical-error-shapes`), (`rule:nrz:shape-certified-solve`). Refuse promoting an algorithmic stability statement, a condition estimate or an unvalidated floating bound to a certificate: certified acceptance still requires conservative numerical evidence for the particular input. Componentwise models remain optional until their tighter bounds justify their cost; the initial public Frobenius envelope is the engine's choice. Binds (`dec:vision:accuracy-policy`), (`dec:vision:factorizations-and-scaling`), (`rule:vision:equilibration`), (`dec:vision:primary-error-model`), (`tab:vision:error-model-comparison`), (`tab:vision:error-model-operation-composition`), (`rule:vision:arithmetic-closure`), (`def:vision:certified-solve-contract`), (`rule:vision:decomposition-objects`), (`rule:vision:solves-and-inverses`), (`rule:vision:spectral-guarantees`), (`rule:vision:matrix-function-guarantees`), (`step:nrz:bite-numerical-feasibility`), (`step:nrz:bite-certified-matrix-core`), (`step:nrz:bite-advanced-matrix-domains`).

**Example (Extra-precise iterative refinement in LAPACK)** · `ex:exemplars:extra-precise-iterative-refinement`

The LAPACK work on iterative refinement with extended-precision residuals and its error bounds shows mixed precision as a demonstrated improvement with a bound, not a hope that iterations restore digits. Take the stopping rules, the componentwise and normwise bound reporting and the refusal to accept an unverified result; refuse the assumption of a particular backend summation order. Binds (`dec:vision:reductions-and-residuals`), (`dec:vision:refinement-and-mixed-precision`), (`step:nrz:bite-certified-matrix-core`), (`step:nrz:bite-accuracy-and-benchmarks`).

**Example (Error-free transformations)** · `ex:exemplars:error-free-transformations`

Ogita, Rump and Oishi's accurate sum and dot product through error-free transformations show compensated reductions whose error is itself bounded. Take the transformations for engine-controlled reductions and residuals; refuse treating compensation as a certificate on its own. Binds (`dec:vision:reductions-and-residuals`), (`step:nrz:bite-numerical-feasibility`), (`step:nrz:bite-units-and-foundations`), (`step:nrz:bite-certified-matrix-core`).

### Dimensioned matrices and multivariate uncertainty · `sec:exemplars:dimensioned-matrices-and-multivariate-uncertainty`

## Reference problems · `sec:exemplars:reference-problems`

**Convention (Problem entry shape)** · `conv:exemplars:problem-entry-shape`

Every reference problem states its physical model, input dimensions and measures, physical counts where applicable, semantic kinds and affine roles, declared uncertainty, expected observables with closed-form or independently computed values, error coverage, and the architectural claims it stresses (`def:vision:dimensions`), (`def:vision:measures`), (`def:vision:counts`), (`rule:vision:semantic-kinds-and-roles`). Structural selectors are declared metadata; each uncertainty expression has its own descriptor and source/assurance contract (`rule:vision:counts-and-metadata`), (`rule:vision:uncertainty-unit-algebra`). A problem needs an independently justified answer and a checkable expression/symbol contract to judge both numerical results and algebraic consistency. Existing unit tables and prose closed forms require the planned migration; their current validation checks shape and does not prove these algebraic obligations (`req:vision:expression-and-symbol-contract`), (`req:vision:unit-data-migration`).

**Problem (Spring-mass truss statics)** · `prob:exemplars:spring-mass-truss`

A planar truss of springs under static loads, with one member far stiffer than the rest. Observables are the node displacements in metres and member forces in newtons from a stiffness matrix in newtons per metre; the answer follows from linear elasticity by hand for small trusses. Stresses typed heterogeneous blocks, the certified solve and refusal under ill-conditioning. Take the quotient relation between force equations, displacement variables and stiffness operators, including typed zero blocks in the mixed force/displacement formulation. Take intrinsic input and computational bounds with their own descriptors: displacement radii use a length difference measure, stiffness radii have force/length dimension, and relative budgets are ratios (`def:vision:dimensions`), (`def:vision:measures`), (`def:vision:intrinsic-uncertainty-companions`), (`rule:vision:uncertainty-unit-algebra`). Refuse one unnormalized physical norm for the mixed vector, and refuse treating the number of nodes or degrees of freedom as a physical count (`rule:vision:heterogeneous-assembly`), (`def:vision:counts`), (`rule:vision:counts-and-metadata`). Specialized numeric closed forms need explicit dimensional symbols in the planned migration (`req:vision:unit-data-migration`). Binds (`rule:vision:heterogeneous-assembly`), (`rule:vision:verification-locations`), (`step:nrz:bite-exemplar-register`), (`step:nrz:bite-units-design`), (`step:nrz:bite-certified-matrix-core`), (`step:nrz:bite-physics-core`), (`step:nrz:bite-accuracy-and-benchmarks`).

**Problem (Resistor network by modified nodal analysis)** · `prob:exemplars:resistor-network`

A resistor network with voltage and current sources under Kirchhoff's laws, assembled by modified nodal analysis into one block system in volts and amperes. Observables are node voltages and branch currents with closed forms for ladder and bridge networks. Stresses the second heterogeneous block case and the quotient-dimension rule of assembly. Take explicit conductance, resistance and dimensionless operator blocks: each block must map its variable kind and dimension to its equation kind and dimension, even when every entry is zero (`def:vision:dimensions`), (`req:vision:algebraic-unit-checks`). Take uncertainty as intrinsic block contents, with voltage and current radii in their respective difference measures and any voltage/current covariance in the product measure; normalized matrix bounds retain the map that gives them meaning (`def:vision:measures`), (`def:vision:intrinsic-uncertainty-companions`), (`rule:vision:uncertainty-unit-algebra`). Refuse concatenating volts and amperes into a single physical radius, or treating a topology selector as a physical count (`rule:vision:heterogeneous-assembly`), (`def:vision:counts`), (`rule:vision:counts-and-metadata`). Binds (`dec:vision:homogeneous-numerical-leaves`), (`rule:vision:heterogeneous-assembly`), (`rule:vision:verification-locations`), (`step:nrz:bite-exemplar-register`), (`step:nrz:bite-units-design`), (`step:nrz:bite-certified-matrix-core`), (`step:nrz:bite-physics-core`), (`step:nrz:bite-accuracy-and-benchmarks`).

**Problem (Coupled oscillators and normal modes)** · `prob:exemplars:coupled-oscillators-normal-modes`

Masses on springs with a symmetric stiffness and mass matrix, including a configuration with a degenerate mode pair. Observables are the mode frequencies in radians per second and the mode shapes; the symmetric chain has closed-form modes. Stresses spectral guarantees, invariant subspaces for repeated eigenvalues and the generalised eigenproblem's dimensions. ## Use in the plan · `sec:exemplars:use-in-the-plan`

**Decision (An exemplar bite precedes numerical feasibility)** · `dec:exemplars:exemplar-bite`

One short design bite renders this register into the repository and seeds the problems tree with the reference problems above, each in the entry shape, before the feasibility stage, because the feasibility experiments need targets with known answers and the later bites need a fixed list to cite.

**Rule (Take and refuse are stated per exemplar)** · `rule:exemplars:take-and-refuse`

Every exemplar entry states what the engine takes and what it refuses, because a reference admired whole imports its compromises along with its insights, and a later bite citing the entry inherits a decision rather than an inspiration. Where the lesson concerns units, the entry identifies dimensions, measures, counts and semantic roles and states the invalid inference it refuses (`def:vision:dimensions`), (`def:vision:measures`), (`def:vision:counts`), (`rule:vision:semantic-kinds-and-roles`). Lessons about uncertainty identify its own descriptor and assurance and how it travels with values (`def:vision:intrinsic-uncertainty-companions`), (`rule:vision:uncertainty-unit-algebra`).

**Rule (Adversarial fixtures gate certification first)** · `rule:exemplars:adversarial-fixtures-gate-first`

The feasibility bite meets the adversarial fixtures before any physics problem, because an engine that produces a confident wrong sign on a known counterexample has nothing to certify yet, and the fixtures are cheap to run and exact to judge.
