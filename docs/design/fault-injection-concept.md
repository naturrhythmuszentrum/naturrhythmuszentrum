# Fault injection and repair evidence · `spec:nrz:fault-injection-concept`

**Caveat (Concept status and authority)** · `cav:nrz:fault-injection-status`

This concept compares ways to obtain failure-path evidence and records the surface decisions in (`sec:nrz:fault-injection-questions`). The recommendation to retain private seams beside public controls is decided; no supported fault-injection feature or shared schedule is introduced. The four proposals remain explicitly **Proposal, not adopted**, with reason and cost, and authorize no implementation. Existing contracts remain the authority for authentication, recoverable handback, no-fail publication and prepaid cleanup (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-authentication`), (`alg:nrz:matrix-loans-handback`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-publication`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-cleanup-tariffs`). The comparison serves confidence in those product contracts (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-options`), (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-product-choice`).

## Inventory and reachability · `sec:nrz:fault-injection-inventory`

**Observation (Inventory boundary)** · `obs:nrz:fault-injection-source-boundary`

The inventory is tied to source commit d4488d5e0bbafad47b3778280fe0de90131a1427 and groups controls by failure/repair transition rather than duplicating every numerical recipe's allocation-prefix test. Paths below are repository-relative. Inspection covers matrix heap, managed loans, native applications, LC pending execution/publication, graph adapter/owner/walk/sink, bindings correspondence and CAS transaction controls; sampling adds public host-failure and interruption-clock evidence. A private test location alone does not make the behavior private: the reachability column distinguishes public inputs, private seams and absent transitions. This distinction follows the evidence boundary in (`req:nrz:tiles-streams-graph-evidence`) and the prohibition on counterfeit ownership in (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-authentication`).

The graph adapter's relevant failure scenarios are late execution failure leaving a cancel-only capability; temporary loss of a candidate occupation, restoration of the exact occupation, then publication retry or cancellation; allocator refusal after preflight while retaining a fan-out use; and allocator refusal while turning checked preparation into pending storage, followed by bounded retry and cancellation. The first two require private state intervention: a reserved outcome is written before traversal, or the occupation is temporarily removed and restored. The latter two use the existing allocation schedule for the allocator-refusal-after-preflight variant. Public byte-limit neighbours also reach fan-out retention refusal and preparation refusal, returning a resource-byte error and preserving the original capability for bounded retry or cancellation. Those neighbours establish consuming-path evidence without selecting the later physical allocation failure. Heap reservation code can map both logical capacity refusal and real or injected allocation refusal to the same Capacity error, so an error variant alone does not identify which boundary was exercised. The public drive control covers these byte-limit neighbours (`[MATRIX-test:integration:lc-omega-graph-drive]`); bounded residence separately covers retained history and admission beside surviving owners (`[MATRIX-test:integration:lc-omega-graph-bounded-residence]`). The library control `graph_native_failure_repair` exercises defensive late failure and exact-occupation restoration (`[MATRIX-test:unit:graph-native-failure-repair]`). These are distinct evidence homes under the F1/F3 rows in (`tab:nrz:tiles-streams-graph-controls`) and the adapter contract (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-lc-graph-adapter`).

**Observation (Public controls and remaining gaps)** · `obs:nrz:fault-injection-public-controls`

Caller-supplied `HeapLimits`, resource policies, bindings/CAS `Limits`, original cumulative `Budget`/`ExecutionAccount`, bounded scan counts, host coverage/payload, unsupported syntax and explicit lifecycle operations already select capacity, Work, domain, payload and stale-source refusals. Exact and one-below tests with prior spend exercise these boundaries without a special fault surface; a private test that consumes ledger Work is not evidence that the corresponding budget condition is intrinsically private. In particular, a real stale source need not be made current again to test candidate preservation and cancellation (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-ledger`), (`def:nrz:matrix-loans-request-outcomes`), (`rule:nrz:cas-design-transactions`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-pins`).

Sampling already accepts a failing host operation at four lifecycle phases; CAS already demonstrates an oversized reservation failure using caller limits and requested size. The graph adapter's public byte-limit neighbours cover retention/preparation refusal followed by retry or cancellation (`[MATRIX-test:integration:lc-omega-graph-drive]`), and retained-history admission is covered by (`[MATRIX-test:integration:lc-omega-graph-bounded-residence]`); later allocation-prefix selection stays private. Defensive late failure and occupation restoration are library evidence (`[MATRIX-test:unit:graph-native-failure-repair]`). These are distinct evidence routes, not substitutes for one another: the public controls exercise consuming recovery, and the private controls distinguish later acquisition failure and defensive corruption (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-paths`), (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-source-boundary`), ``test:unit:sampling-host-failure-finalization``, ``test:crate:ordered-scratch-allocation-refusal-retry-charges-relocation``.

Unhooked fallible reservations are a gap across matrix and bindings as well as CAS. In particular, core evidence reservation after validation is separate from the managed loan publication inventory; absence of a hook is not evidence that allocation is infallible. Covering those prefixes would add hook sites, error propagation scrutiny and cleanup controls across owners (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-paths`), (`alg:nrz:matrix-core-publish`), (`alg:nrz:matrix-loans-handback`).

The inventory does not claim an executable arbitrary-phase crash recovery path, a persistent CAS recovery log, a fallible step inside native no-fail publication, or an external evidence-export transport. Those paths are absent from the contracts inventoried here. Neither kernel tests nor the private graph sink establish them; the sink's evidence is expressly local. The adapter controls do not extend these contracts to arbitrary crash or external-export recovery (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-publication`), (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-graph-evidence-sink`), (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-source-boundary`).

**Argument (Corruption is a separate control class)** · `arg:nrz:fault-injection-corruption`

A test that temporarily replaces a private receipt or generation can establish rejection and rightful-owner preservation without describing a state constructible by a safe caller. Publishing that mutation as a fault API would expand authority precisely where the test asserts it is sealed. The faithful public target is the error-producing transition, if it exists, not a setter for the rejected bytes. Private corruption controls therefore remain useful even if every realizable fault becomes externally schedulable; no recommendation below promotes them to environmental faults (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-authentication`), (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-lc-omega-lifecycle`), (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-events`).

**Observation (Existing private schedule as cost basis)** · `obs:nrz:fault-injection-existing-schedule`

`loan::fault::Injection` already provides a thread-local acquisition countdown through test-only hooks and clears it on drop; production builds have no corresponding hook lookup. It is non-nesting: `after` overwrites the thread's schedule and resets shared counters, while dropping either guard clears rather than restores the earlier schedule. `unwinding_after` deliberately panics through the assertion in `loan::fault::before`. Exposing this guard under a feature would therefore expose panic behavior and preserve non-nesting unless separately changed; the proposed scoped shape is an extension of this existing mechanism, not a free new abstraction. A feature exposing only existing loan hooks requires no new hook sites: its incremental costs are the feature/API configuration, enabled-build checks and a decision to retain or redesign the non-nesting and panic properties. Broad coverage beyond those hooks additionally incurs cross-crate ownership, hook placement at uninstrumented reservations and independent prefix-cleanup controls; no measured runtime figure is inferred from their presence (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-paths`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-abandonment`), (`proposal:nrz:fault-injection-scoped-schedule`).

**Argument (Scoped observation does not authorize scoped mutation)** · `arg:nrz:fault-injection-dispatch`

The shared matrix observer helper installs a no-op dispatcher before a scoped observer and documents a residual registration race; copying its tracing mechanics would make fault selection depend on an observational subsystem with different guarantees. A fault schedule could borrow the lexical scope concept while using independent closed matching and no user callback at a mutation boundary. Conversely, caller-passed capabilities make propagation explicit but can burden every product call for a test-only need. Neither option has a measured idle-cost claim in this concept (`[MATRIX-rule:matrix:scoped-observer-tests]`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-publication`), (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-options`).

## Recommendation and proposals · `sec:nrz:fault-injection-recommendation`

**Argument (Reason for the surface choice)** · `arg:nrz:fault-injection-recommendation`

The decided surface retains private seams together with public limit neighbours and real-dependency controls, without a supported fault-injection feature (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-product-choice`). The landed public drive and bounded-residence controls supply consuming retry/cancel and surviving-history admission evidence (`[MATRIX-test:integration:lc-omega-graph-drive]`), (`[MATRIX-test:integration:lc-omega-graph-bounded-residence]`); `graph_native_failure_repair` supplies defensive late-failure and occupation-restoration evidence (`[MATRIX-test:unit:graph-native-failure-repair]`). Sampling supplies host failures directly, CAS demonstrates a real oversized-reservation error without injection, and the private matrix schedule selects bounded acquisition prefixes (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-public-controls`), (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-existing-schedule`).

A narrow public schedule limited to existing loan hooks needs no new hooks. It costs feature/API and configuration support, enabled-build checks, and a decision about the existing non-nesting and panic semantics; its benefit is external selection of the same later prefixes already covered privately. A broad schedule adds hook placement and independent cleanup evidence at uninstrumented matrix, bindings and CAS sites, together with shared ownership and accounting. Neither option supplies the first evidence that returned capabilities can recover. The narrow option has a smaller cost and a correspondingly narrower benefit; this concept identifies no unmet external reproduction need that outweighs even that cost. The broad option is less justified without a specific additional fault family (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-options`), (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-existing-schedule`), (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-paths`).

The comparison is resolved by (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-product-choice`); a concrete external reproduction need can reopen the product choice. The following four proposals remain recorded and unadopted as constraints for any future schedule design, not committed work (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-scope-cost`). Public restoration is excluded by (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-native-boundary`), while defensive evidence retains its private home under (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-private-evidence`).

**Proposal (Real-event admission)** · `proposal:nrz:fault-injection-real-events`

**Proposal, not adopted.** Admit a schedulable site only with an existing fallible transition, its real event, allowed pre-state and unchanged error/owner postcondition; keep impossible-state mutations private. Reason: avoid testing a fictional engine or granting repair authority. Cost: a maintained site inventory and a proof obligation for each new fault family, potentially leaving late native failure private (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-events`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-authentication`).

**Proposal (Bounded opt-in scope)** · `proposal:nrz:fault-injection-scoped-schedule`

**Proposal, not adopted.** If an unmet product evidence need justifies a shared surface, evaluate a feature-gated finite schedule with lexical cleanup, explicit target ownership and defined nested/thread behavior; no raw internal setters, executable callbacks, tracing-dependent selection or default process-global installation. Reason: external controls can reach real consuming failures without weakening private seals or disturbing parallel work. Cost: feature/build matrix, scope machinery, explicit treatment of existing non-nesting and panic behavior, enabled idle checks and propagation constraints; new hooks and cross-owner cleanup evidence are additional costs only for coverage beyond the existing loan hooks; lexical drop alone is insufficient if ownership crosses threads (`arg:nrz:fault-injection-dispatch`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-publication`).

**Proposal (Determinism and resource account)** · `proposal:nrz:fault-injection-accounting`

**Proposal, not adopted.** Give schedule storage and matching an explicit bounded cost account; distinguish harness-owned configuration from engine-owned state, preserve cumulative engine Work and prepaid cleanup, and select occurrences in stable semantic order. No address/hash/tracing order or global allocation ordinal would define portable failure identity. Reason: enabling diagnostics must not conceal allocations, refill budgets or corrupt comparisons. Cost: a cost model and controls for enabled/disabled and installed/empty schedules; any additional engine tariff needs owner approval rather than silently changing existing pins (`rule:nrz:stable-collection-order`), (`dec:nrz:stable-collection-scratch-cost`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-ledger`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-cleanup-tariffs`).

**Proposal (Independent failure evidence)** · `proposal:nrz:fault-injection-evidence`

**Proposal, not adopted.** Pair each injected failure with an uninjected success and a public-limit neighbor where available; assert the same returned capability, unchanged rightful owner, actual destroyed prefix, retained spent Work, and exactly-once retry/cancel or reconciliation. A site-not-reached result would fail the control rather than count as fault coverage. Test nested scopes, parallel isolation, drop/unwind removal and no-schedule equivalence separately. Reason: the scheduler's own fired counter cannot prove repair safety or account preservation. Cost: integration fixtures in both native formats, private independent observations and configuration comparisons, without inferring public adapter coverage from private controls (`alg:nrz:matrix-loans-handback`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-abandonment`), (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-source-boundary`).

## Surface decisions · `sec:nrz:fault-injection-questions`

**Decision (No supported fault-injection feature)** · `dec:nrz:fault-injection-product-choice`

There is no supported fault-injection feature. Public caller byte limits, sampling host operations and caller-sized CAS reservations already establish refusal, retry or cancellation, preserved ownership and charged Work within their respective scopes (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-public-controls`), (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-paths`). Exposing the existing private schedule would mainly add API/configuration support and decisions about non-nesting and panic behavior, without additional recovery evidence (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-existing-schedule`), (`arg:nrz:fault-injection-recommendation`). A concrete external reproduction need reopens this product choice.

**Decision (No public restoration scenario)** · `dec:nrz:fault-injection-native-boundary`

Restoration is not a public fault scenario. Restoring a retired generation conflicts with (`rule:nrz:matrix-loans-authentication`), while clearing a transient refusal is already covered by the public byte-limit retry path (`[MATRIX-test:integration:lc-omega-graph-drive]`). Private removal and restoration of the exact live occupation remains defensive evidence, distinct from a public restoration contract (`[MATRIX-test:unit:graph-native-failure-repair]`), (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-private-evidence`).

**Decision (No schedule or cost ownership)** · `dec:nrz:fault-injection-scope-cost`

No schedule is built, so no schedule storage, matching cost or scope ownership is assigned. This follows from the absence of a supported feature (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-product-choice`) and avoids assigning tariffs or propagation semantics to an unbuilt mechanism. The four proposals remain recorded, unadopted constraints for any future schedule design: real-event admission (`proposal:nrz:fault-injection-real-events`), bounded opt-in scope (`proposal:nrz:fault-injection-scoped-schedule`), deterministic resource accounting (`proposal:nrz:fault-injection-accounting`) and independent failure evidence (`proposal:nrz:fault-injection-evidence`). The separate factual reachability investigation is low-priority backlog and does not block LC (`step:nrz:backlog-native-failure-reachability`).

**Decision (No CAS extension and in-memory evidence scope)** · `dec:nrz:fault-injection-coverage-choice`

No CAS fault-injection extension is added; evidence scope remains in memory. The existing oversized-reservation control covers caller-sized reservation refusal and its charged retry without a new injection surface (`tab:nrz:fault-injection-paths`), (`dec:nrz:stable-collection-scratch-cost`). Local sink evidence does not establish external delivery or I/O recovery (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-graph-evidence-sink`). An exporter with real I/O would have a separate product contract and design, rather than acquiring failure semantics from this concept.

**Decision (Permanent private defensive evidence)** · `dec:nrz:fault-injection-private-evidence`

Private library controls are the permanent evidence home for defensive late native failure and exact-occupation removal and restoration, whatever fault surface is later chosen. This keeps evidence about sealed authority distinct from public behavior in control attribution (`arg:nrz:fault-injection-corruption`), (`dec:nrz:matrix-loans-lc-origin-encoding`). It agrees with F1 in (`tab:nrz:tiles-streams-graph-controls`), which names `graph_native_failure_repair` for these defensive paths (`[MATRIX-test:unit:graph-native-failure-repair]`).

**Decision (Owner-local private fault seams)** · `dec:nrz:fault-injection-package-owner`

Fault seams stay owner-local and private beside public limit neighbours. No cross-crate schedule exists to own; keeping the seams with the code whose failure and cleanup they exercise avoids new dependency, lifetime and accounting responsibilities without losing the existing evidence (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-existing-schedule`), (`obs:nrz:fault-injection-public-controls`), (`dec:nrz:fault-injection-product-choice`).
