# Descriptor graphs and bindings to floating heaps · `spec:nrz:descriptor-binding`

This record defines the exact meaning boundary and its execution over floating storage. It refines the shape, heap and computation contracts for the LC target (`spec:nrz:matrix-shapes`), (`spec:nrz:descriptor-heap-computation`), (`spec:nrz:matrix-core-design`), (`spec:nrz:lc-resonance`). The definitions specify the target architecture; the source findings below delimit the implementation. The ownership refinements in this record take precedence over the older matrix-owned meaning boundary, while retaining its numerical theorems, lifetime protection and original-expression checks. No numerical capability follows from this design alone.

## Six positions and their consequences · `sec:nrz:descriptor-binding-positions`

**Principle (The heap knows floating point only)** · `prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-float-heap`

The numerical heap stores finite floats in versioned regions, their independently shaped error and statistical parameter regions, and the storage/dependency metadata needed to use them safely. Every region has one symbol owner through a `nrz-bindings` binding; that opaque association gives access to meaning without putting meaning in the float storage. It has no Real/Complex sort, physical kind, unit, sign assertion or affine role. Binary32/binary64 precision, extents, layouts and companion target versions are numerical facts; voltage, current and Complex interpretation are descriptor facts. This refines the plain storage of (`def:nrz:scalar-arena`) and replaces the physical homogeneity requirement of (`dec:vision:homogeneous-numerical-leaves`): one format per arena enables uniform float procedures without requiring one meaning per region. Finite admission and nonnegative radius checks are numerical payload checks, not physical sign declarations. Exact discrete counts remain outside these floating arenas under the existing separate representation obligation.

**Principle (Descriptors are checked CAS graph structures)** · `prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-cas-meaning`

A descriptor is exact CAS graph structure: nodes and checked edges retain sorts, kinds, units, roles, ordered axes, partitions, per-entry meanings, component readings, domains, coordinate maps and their exact relations. Bindings constructs and checks the physical vocabulary through that graph; CAS owns generic structure, identities and proof rules without knowing LC physics or heap values. Equal dimensions cannot identify kinds, axes or sources, so flattening the structure into one matrix-owned quantity tag loses required meaning. This refines bindings authority (`dec:nrz:descriptor-unit-authority`) and replaces the matrix semantic ownership in (`dec:nrz:shape-descriptor-ownership`), (`def:nrz:heap-descriptor`): matrix retains numerical records and opaque evidence references, while meaning is reached through bindings. A new graph relation needs a defined syntax and checked rule, not an unchecked annotation or a second algebra AST.

**Principle (A symbol has some heap values or none)** · `prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-optional-values`

A CAS symbol keeps its immutable declaration sort and exact relations whether a particular use binds it to zero, one or several heap floats. The binding supplies their reading; no sort chooses a storage representation. A no-region binding denotes an exact graph value or structural fact with retained defining evidence and no numerical companion. It is distinct from a missing required input and cannot invent a value for an unconstrained or unresolved symbol. Exact symbolic operations can remain in the graph; requesting numerical export introduces an explicit binding and the corresponding import/evaluation error. This refines (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-declarations`) and extends (`def:nrz:plan-binding`) beyond mandatory numerical slots, preserving original checks before any exact zero is eliminated.

**Principle (Reproducibility is agreement within tracked error)** · `prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-reproducibility`

For the same checked descriptor operation, exact inputs or admitted input family, coordinate interpretation and assurance scope, successful executions in admitted arithmetic environments enclose the same exact result. Their centers may differ: after restoration to the same coordinates, two bounds `abs(y-c_1) <= r_1` and `abs(y-c_2) <= r_2` imply `abs(c_1-c_2) <= r_1+r_2` componentwise by the triangle inequality. Numerical-only bounds apply when both runs target the same exact nominal input; different approximations to one input require their import errors and propagated total bounds. Overlap of unrelated input boxes establishes no common target. Descriptor definitions and checked judgments are machine-independent; runtime graph/heap IDs and allocation order need not match. This strengthens the non-bitwise replay boundary of (`conv:nrz:batch-order-and-replay`) with a checkable comparison, not a new identical-bit promise.

The guarantee is conditional on each kernel's admitted arithmetic model, explicit input coverage and successful verification. Covariance describes a statistical model, not a deterministic tolerance; comparing covariance parameters uses those parameters' evaluation enclosures. A rerun may refuse because of resource limits or an unproved numerical domain; a failed result has no agreement claim. Build identity, compiler/profile, target, backend and kernel revisions, precision, floating environment assumptions and error-model revision identify which enclosure argument applied. This refines (`def:nrz:operation-record`) and (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-rounding`) and replaces the canonical-numerical-snapshot objective proposed in (`arg:nrz:project-review-portable`); reproducibility checks and provenance follow the first physics slice instead of gating it.

**Principle (Two graphs joined by bindings)** · `prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-two-graphs`

The descriptor graph states the exact operation and why it is meaningful; the computational graph records which float procedures produced which region versions. `nrz-bindings` bindings join their symbols to numerical representations or to none, and assign every heap region exactly one symbol owner. The separation is necessary because one exact operation can have several layouts and numerical schedules, while a single float kernel can serve unrelated physical meanings. This refines (`dec:vision:physical-and-computational-graphs`) and (`def:nrz:computation-plan`): physical model relations remain exact descriptor structure, and a plan organizes descriptor operations and permitted bindings; an executed computational graph is the resulting heap-version/kernel graph. Physics assembles and schedules through these contracts rather than maintaining another authority for descriptor algebra.

**Principle (Heap-to-symbol binding is total)** · `prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-total`

Every float region on the heap is bound to exactly one CAS symbol, including value regions, deterministic bounds, numerical errors, covariance parameters/factors, lowered constants, grids, temporary heap regions and results. A symbol can own several regions or none, but the inverse association from regions to symbols is total and single-valued. Without that association a meaning-free region could not be checked, converted, combined or cited. Bindings owns the binding relation and matrix enforces its inventory alongside region versions; an opaque owner reference is storage metadata, not a physical tag. A read-only alias is a selection of its owner's region, not a second symbol owner; a distinct derived symbol that needs its own numerical representation receives a fresh bound region. This completes the optional symbol-to-values principle and replaces the implicit permission for standalone numerical regions in (`def:nrz:heap-descriptor`), (`def:nrz:matrix-core-companion-records`).

## Graphs, kernels and lowering · `sec:nrz:descriptor-binding-execution`

**Definition (Descriptor graph)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-descriptor-graph`

The descriptor graph consists of immutable, scoped CAS symbols, operation nodes such as `C = A*B`, and checked relational evidence. Ordered operand/result edges, exact sort and quantity declarations, axes, blocks, domains and coordinate maps determine the operation; a physical relation may be implicit, and its chosen executable solve is a separate operation. Syntax admission establishes well-formed structure, while checked witnesses establish the asserted relations under their contexts. Proof and construction dependencies are acyclic even when model relations express coupled constraints. Bindings validates every original occurrence before normalization as required by (`rule:nrz:plan-validation`); an operation with valid units still needs its own lowering proof.

**Definition (Computational graph)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-computational-graph`

The computational graph has region versions as nodes and closed kernel applications as directed edges, with ordered input/output selections and companion dependencies. A multi-input application is represented by one application record linking all its region versions; it cannot silently omit an operand or shared source. Mutation creates a new version and never rewrites the historical derivation. Every region-version node retains its `nrz-bindings` binding to one descriptor symbol, including an intermediate or companion symbol. Matrix owns these numerical records and their lifetime checks, refining (`dec:nrz:heap-ownership-and-sharing`). The graph depends on precision, arithmetic environment and chosen schedule, which explains why exact descriptor equality does not imply identical centers or identical execution graphs.

**Definition (Symbol binding)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-symbol-binding`

A `nrz-bindings` binding is an immutable record joining one exact descriptor symbol and declaration snapshot to an explicit representation definition and an ordered selection of zero or more region versions. It retains component order, reading and restoration maps, structural/domain evidence, source-symbol identities, companion-symbol relations and both covariance target maps. Its inverse inventory associates each region with exactly this owner symbol; equality of numbers or descriptors cannot authorize a second owner. The representation may be None, a component pair, a block or another closed admitted layout; these are use-specific contracts, not alternatives in the symbol's sort. Extents, selections, precision and revisions identify the numerical realization, while the graph supplies meaning. This refines (`def:nrz:plan-binding`) and keeps the distinction between schema, value and source identity in (`def:nrz:quantity-schema-key`).

**Definition (Kernel)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-kernel`

A kernel is a closed float procedure and edge type of the computational graph, identified by its revision, numerical shapes, layouts, precision, arithmetic assumptions, exact law and enclosure theorem. It reads numerical inputs and produces a private candidate center, total radius and numerical error, or a typed refusal. It sees no physical descriptor or Real/Complex tag and accepts no arbitrary caller callback as a new law. Scalar owns primitive enclosure arithmetic; matrix owns region procedures and their verified application records. This refines the numerical substrate of (`def:nrz:matrix-core-scalar-elements`) without moving physical checking into scalar.

**Definition (Kernel law)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-kernel-law`

A kernel law is a specified exact operation on real arrays of fixed shapes for an instantiated call, with an explicit mathematical domain. Matrix product means exact real contraction, independent of the candidate backend's reduction tree; reciprocal has a domain excluding zero. The law distinguishes the mathematical target from the actual rounded procedure, so a fast candidate, an exact copy and a verified solve each have a precise target rather than inheriting correctness from finiteness. Shape parameters may select a supported instance, but cannot change its law. This makes explicit the operation-specific targets of (`tab:nrz:matrix-core-operation-propagation`).

**Theorem (Kernel enclosure contract)** · `thm:nrz:descriptor-binding-kernel-enclosure`

For finite input centers `c` and nonnegative radii `r`, let the box be all real arrays `x` with `abs(x-c) <= r`. A successful kernel application establishes that this box lies in the law's domain and returns finite `y_hat`, nonnegative `r_y` and `e` satisfying `abs(law(x)-y_hat) <= r_y` for every x in that box and `abs(law(c)-y_hat) <= e`. A theorem must justify its candidate discrepancy, propagated input error and all interaction/verification rounding under the recorded arithmetic assumptions; shape agreement alone proves none of them. The bounds are componentwise, or conservatively expanded from an explicitly named numerical norm. The numerical error e is already covered by the total r_y and must not be added twice. This abstracts the proved inclusion arguments in (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-jacobian-contract`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-solve-theorem`) and (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-affine-conversion`); it grants no theorem to an unverified procedure.

**Definition (Checked lowering)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-lowering`

A lowering is a checked map from a descriptor operation under specified operand bindings to a finite schedule of kernel applications and the exact reading/restoration maps for its output bindings. Before any kernel runs, lowering derives the output and all required companion symbols, their sorts, kinds, units and axes, and reserves their result bindings. Its correctness obligation is that executing the schedule's exact laws and reading its outputs computes the original descriptor operation on the entire admitted family. Bindings retains this operation-specific correspondence through CAS evidence and closed recipe rules. Kind, unit, role, semantic domain, component order, physical metric and layout choice are checked on the descriptor side before issuing the schedule. A value-dependent premise remains a named numerical guard: the schedule first obtains its enclosure evidence, and a partial kernel is called only after that evidence establishes its domain. A positive declaration alone cannot make a zero-containing box admissible. This refines the finite recipe boundary of (`dec:nrz:matrix-core-cas-boundary`) and (`alg:nrz:matrix-core-sensitivity-lowering`).

For example, the same Complex multiplication lowers under component-pair bindings to four real products and two sums, under block bindings to one 2×2 real product, and under exact no-region bindings to a checked exact result with no kernel when the admitted exact evaluator can establish it. An unavailable exact evaluation remains unresolved; it is not permission to drop a necessary numerical input. The arithmetic schedule and all coefficient imports have their own errors. Symbolic equivalence authorizes the law correspondence, not an untracked reassociation of floating instructions.

**Definition (Lowered computation subgraph)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-lowered-subgraph`

A computation is a finite directed acyclic subgraph of kernel applications obtained by lowering the selected descriptor operations and their bindings. It includes imports, normalization, numerical domain guards, candidate verification, restoration and required companion contractions, with a symbol owner for every heap region. The descriptor dependency graph identifies grid-invariant nodes for shared evaluation rather than constructing an independent schedule for every row. Purely exact operations contribute no numerical nodes. Coupled model relations become explicit supported solve nodes; iteration is a bounded admitted schedule with its own stopping and error contract, not an accidental cycle. Physics chooses nodes and policies, bindings supplies the checked lowering, and matrix executes its numerical dependency graph. This specializes (`def:nrz:computation-plan`) without confusing model coupling with execution order.

**Rule (Composition of enclosure theorems)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-error-composition`

Induct on a topological order of the lowered subgraph. The admitted inputs enclose the exact values selected by their bindings; each kernel's domain check and enclosure theorem then enclose its exact law on the incoming boxes. Its resulting box supplies the next edge's input, including prior import, conversion and evaluation error once through its recorded coverage. Restoration is another checked map with its own numerical error, so the final box encloses the original descriptor result by the lowering correspondence. Repeated inputs remain the same underlying exact variable; a box relaxation may be conservative, but neither aliasing nor a copied center proves independence or permits error cancellation. Statistical source maps compose separately with their stated assurance. This refines the coverage rule (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-coverage`) and supplies the reason for within-error reproducibility.

**Rule (Kernel contracts contain no descriptor obligations)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-kernel-boundary`

The kernel contract holds numerical operand/result shapes, layouts and the law with its enclosure/domain requirements; precision, work limits and arithmetic revision select and constrain its executable instance. Per-operand sorts, physical schemas, axis identities, units and homogeneity belong to the lowering contract. Thus the `RegionContract` fields carried by `crates/matrix/src/kernels/contract.rs::KernelContract` must move to the descriptor side rather than merely being renamed. Matrix may expose a facade retaining a bindings witness beside an execution request, but the heap and numerical kernel must not inspect that witness to derive meaning. This replaces the conflated semantic/numerical admission in (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-shape-checks`) while preserving shape safety and numerical certification.

## Concrete binding and representation contracts · `sec:nrz:descriptor-binding-contract`

**Rule (Storage-neutral binding and live numerical validation)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-validation`

Bindings defines the binding's exact graph references, representation, ordered component selections and semantic witnesses; it does not depend on matrix or resolve a heap handle. Clients request a value by its symbol and selected binding; raw region handles remain internal numerical locators and cannot select or bypass its meaning. A concrete instance can be parameterized by opaque region tickets supplied by the matrix client. A ticket records or refers to owner scope, slot and arena generations, base value identity/version, selected extent/layout, precision and companion target versions; it grants no access by itself. Matrix retains the actual handles and, under one exclusive execution lease, resolves the tickets, validates the numerical facts, and matches the resolved inventory to the exact binding instance. Bindings checks meanings against that inventory through its storage-neutral interface. Only their matching successful checks produce executable lowering authority, stamped with one binding/execution identity. Imports and heap scratch allocation also require a prepared symbol binding, so there is no path that creates an orphan region. Caller-supplied metadata cannot forge live storage authority. This implements the two responsibilities within (`rule:nrz:binding-validation`) and preserves the dependency direction matrix → bindings → CAS.

A None binding has an empty region inventory and checked exact defining evidence; a numerical slot with missing storage refuses. Pair and block bindings enumerate every component and repeated/negated occurrence in logical order; another layout requires an admitted definition and its law correspondence. Original operands, typed zeros and unused/inactive declarations are checked before choosing a reduced representation. Matrix checks extents, integer-safe selections, finite centers and radii, nonnegative bounds, current versions, complete companion coverage, unique symbol-owner inventory and agreement of both target maps; bindings checks the value, bound/error, covariance and result symbols, their checked relations and the physical meanings of all selections. Descriptor homogeneity may remain a bindings predicate for a particular recipe, but it is no longer an allocation requirement. This preserves (`rule:nrz:descriptor-layout-validation`) and (`rule:nrz:descriptor-publication-validation`) while splitting their semantic and storage obligations.

**Rule (Binding invalidation and atomic publication)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-invalidation`

A foreign owner scope, retired occupation or changed generation refuses before indexing. A changed base value version, layout selection, companion target, declaration snapshot, representation/map revision, structural premise, source model or assurance invalidates the dependent binding even if center bytes agree. Read-only aliases and repeated selections retain the same base version and source; a write through any alias invalidates all affected evidence, conservatively the whole base. Layout relocation can preserve the mathematical value version but invalidates cached views. Matrix detects numerical staleness; bindings rejects stale exact witnesses and the facade requires both checks. This refines (`rule:nrz:descriptor-lifetime-and-mutation`), (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-invalidation`) and (`rule:nrz:computation-cache-validity`).

The result symbols and planned binding inventory exist before evaluation; reserved private regions already have their unique symbol owners but expose no live value. All output centers, companions and kernel evidence remain private until the complete bundle is validated. Matrix checks the exact execution token, reservations, finite payloads, target versions, output injectivity and disjointness, numerical coverage and requested budgets against bindings-prepared output meaning/restoration evidence. Preparation completes fallible work before a single publication installs every live output-region version in its planned symbol binding. No kernel or client callback runs in the commit phase. A failure publishes neither a prefix nor a readable symbol with absent storage; planned failed outputs remain explicitly unavailable. The exclusive lease prevents mutation between validation and publication, retaining (`alg:nrz:matrix-core-publish`).

**Definition (Companions are quantity symbols)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-companion-symbols`

The numerical-error companion of x is a distinct CAS symbol e_x of kind numerical error bound, with x's logical entry shape, per-entry difference measure and a checked numerical-error-of edge to x and the law/coverage it bounds. A deterministic bound r_x is likewise a distinct bound symbol, with difference measures and an explicit deterministic coverage relation; physical/input bound coverage and numerical evaluation error remain distinguishable even when their dimensions and arrays match. A covariance block U_xy is a symbol with ordered edges to x and y and per-entry unit `u_x,i*u_y,j`, paired covariance kind and zero affine offset. Bindings checks these dimensions, kinds and relations like any other quantity. A covariance parameter's numerical error is another bound symbol targeting U_xy, not a physical uncertainty on x or y. Normwise bounds name a numerical norm/coordinate contract rather than pretending one physical unit covers mixed entries. Outward upper bounds terminate their numerical construction proof; they do not require an infinite chain of error-companion symbols.

The implemented homogeneous subset records deterministic-bound and numerical-error associations as explicit CAS symbol-relation edges between distinct admitted Free endpoints. Their kinds and ordered targets are graph structure; physical difference units and coverage interpretation are checked by bindings. The endpoints may have independent bounded declaration headers, so adding a companion does not copy prior symbol inventories. An association edge is neither an equation nor a proof, and supplies no rewrite, normalization, solving or numerical authority.

This replaces the semantic authority of `JointRecord::pairings/evaluation`, `CompanionSetDraft`, `MeanParameters`, `CovarianceParameterView` and `ParameterErrorView` in `crates/matrix/src/companions/` with graph-backed symbols and bindings witnesses. It refines (`def:nrz:matrix-core-companion-records`), (`def:nrz:companion-shape`) and (`def:nrz:covariance-shape`), while retaining their distinct shapes and target meanings. The assurance distinctions in (`tab:nrz:matrix-core-assurance-modes`) become relations on those symbols: DeclaredInputs and RepresentedInputs name covered deterministic families; AffineMoments and LinearizedMoments name the exact or local statistical law; declared-model and estimated-diagnostic status stay explicit. Bindings checking does not establish a numerical guarantee. Matrix still owns finite payloads, nonnegative bound checks, current companion/target region versions, two-sided selection agreement, PSD verification, numerical certificates and atomic publication; its binding records attach the numerical evidence required by those declared relations instead of treating an assurance enum as a proof. The CAS receives no heap payload or machine-specific numerical certificate; the exact descriptor relation states the obligation and the computational graph records its numerical discharge.

**Definition (Sources and their propagation are graph structure)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-source-symbols`

Every uncertainty source is a declared CAS symbol, such as shared source voltage s or a component-tolerance symbol, with its deterministic support or statistical model represented by checked relations on that symbol. Numerical model parameters have their own bound region symbols; an abstract source need not itself have a sampled heap value. Joint-source relations name their ordered source symbols and explicitly record known covariance, declared zero covariance with its premise, or unknown correlation; an omitted edge is incomplete input, never independence. Copies and grid points refer to the same source symbol when they share a physical source. This refines (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-joint-sources`) and (`rule:nrz:batch-source-covariance`) and replaces matrix `SourceId`/`JointModel` as authorities for source meaning; matrix may retain opaque numerical dependency indices into the checked relation.

A source Jacobian is an exact graph expression and witness derived once for the operation's original graph, ordered source symbols, declaration/context, domain and rule revision, then shared by every compatible lowering. Graph composition applies the chain rule without rebuilding independent source declarations per operation. Conditional derivative and implicit-root/solve premises remain attached; an unavailable proof still refuses. Its numerical evaluation is a separately bound Jacobian region with error companions keyed to the actual input versions and operating point; shared symbolic evidence does not reuse stale evaluated derivatives or upgrade LinearizedMoments. This refines (`alg:nrz:matrix-core-sensitivity-lowering`) and (`rule:nrz:shape-sensitivity-propagation`), replacing the fresh bindings `Context` constructed by `crates/matrix/src/solve/sensitivity.rs::check` for each solve. Cumulative ledgers charge actual graph construction, proof replay and lookup work without re-deriving an unchanged witness.

**Definition (Indexed grid symbol)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-grid-symbol`

A grid is one float region bound to an indexed CAS symbol such as omega[k] over an exact axis descriptor. The descriptor records extent, order, index identity, coordinate kind/unit and any point-key mapping; k is structural metadata rather than a physical count or frequency. Selecting a grid entry is a checked selection of that one symbol's binding, not a newly independent source or a second owner of the same region. Covariance over entries or outputs names the corresponding ordered indexed symbols and source relations. Ragged grids require explicit extents/selections instead of an invented rectangle. This refines (`def:nrz:batch-axis`), (`def:nrz:computation-batch`) and (`conv:nrz:shape-entry-order`): a physical frequency axis and a batch ordinal stay distinct even when one map connects them.

**Rule (Hoist and bind grid-invariant values)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-hoisted-constants`

A lowered constant is a numerical region bound to its exact CAS node through that node's declared result symbol, with an enclosure/error companion symbol; examples are a descriptor-defined 2*pi and 1/C when C is fixed across the grid. Dependency analysis on the original descriptor graph hoists every node independent of the grid index into a shared prelude for its admitted domain/context. It evaluates each such node once per compatible binding/version key, and every applicable grid kernel reads the same bound region. Constants of a sweep can depend on numerical inputs such as C; they are not therefore exact source-free descriptor constants, and their input uncertainty propagates. Exact definitions use certified coefficient import or constant evaluation, whereas 1/C of a measured C uses its numerical reciprocal law and domain guard. Hoisting preserves original definedness, branch guards and source relations; it never evaluates a partial node outside its admitted domain. This refines (`alg:nrz:matrix-core-constant-lowering`) and (`rule:nrz:computation-cache-validity`) as the heap counterpart of exact graph sharing.

**Definition (Hoisted-value reuse key)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-binding-reuse-key`

Reuse is identified by descriptor node identity and the ordered input-region identities/versions on which its evaluation depends, extended by the exact context/domain, representation/maps, source/companion revisions, precision, arithmetic/error-model and kernel/recipe revisions required by that claim. A source-free constant has an empty input-region version vector; equal printed formulas are not node identity, and equal version integers from different heaps are not equal inputs. The graph's dependency analysis proves that the grid index is absent, while matrix records and revalidates the numerical dependency closure before each use. If C changes version, the cached 1/C region and every dependent result become stale through reverse edges and version comparisons; a new evaluation publishes a fresh bound version, never silently updates the old proof. Symbolic Jacobian sharing can survive a numeric-input change, but its evaluated region follows this same invalidation rule. This specializes (`rule:nrz:computation-cache-validity`) without confusing reusable factors, constants or derivative expressions with point-specific certificates.

**Rule (Derive result bindings before execution)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-result-symbols`

Lowering derives each output CAS symbol, its sort, kind, unit and axes, and every required deterministic-bound, numerical-error and covariance symbol with its checked target relations before any kernel runs. It creates a planned binding with exact symbol and representation identities and unavailable output slots; numerical regions are allocated only under those owners. Internal heap temporaries and lowered coefficients receive their own descriptor symbols in this schedule as well, so private storage is no exception to total binding. A future output is not an exact None binding: it is pending until success or explicitly unavailable after refusal. Publication atomically installs the output and companion region versions into the planned bindings after semantic and numerical validation. Later operations resolve the result by symbol plus the selected binding, following that checked inventory to live regions; a raw handle cannot supply the result meaning. This refines (`def:nrz:plan-binding`) and (`rule:nrz:descriptor-publication-validation`), retaining exclusive-lease and foreign-candidate protection.

For homogeneous copy, the predeclared result has an explicit Copy symbol-relation edge to its exact admitted source, and its bound/error symbols have their own ordered target edges. Each bundle uses a bounded independent declaration header while all symbols and edges remain in the same graph. Bindings checks the closed recipe and numerical binding validates the selected entry map; only complete checked publication makes the result readable. A derived result cannot acquire arbitrary import bytes to bypass that publication.

**Rule (Representations describe whole families)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-family-representation`

A representation definition states its exact free coordinates, expansion/reading maps, admissible family and retained relation proofs. Structural zeros, duplicated entries, signs and conjugation apply to the entire represented family only when checked relations establish them there; matching center bits establish at most a center fact. A fixed affine expansion `v = T*z+d` maps deterministic radii by `abs(T)*r_z` plus evaluation error, covariance by `T*U_z*T^T`, and a cross block by the two sides' expansion maps. Repeated entries keep one source identity, and numerical error in the covariance has its own companion. Rounding may break a relation between stored centers while their enclosures still contain the structured exact target; preserve the free-coordinate lineage and discrepancy instead of asserting exact center equality or silently projecting. This refines (`def:nrz:shape-structure`) and (`rule:nrz:structured-companion-coordinates`). A representation change is a checked rebind with conversions and new numerical evidence, never a retagging of existing floats.

**Example (Complex multiplication through a float block)** · `ex:nrz:descriptor-binding-complex-block`

For the descriptor value `z=a+i*b`, define `R(z)=[[a,-b],[b,a]]`. Direct expansion gives `R(z*w)=R(z)*R(w)`; on a pair `(u,v)`, the block gives `(a*u-b*v,b*u+a*v)`, and conjugation is the exact map `(a,b) -> (a,-b)` with `R(conj(z))=R(z)^T`. In logical column order the expansion is `vec(R(z))=(a,b,-b,a)`, so its fixed 4×2 map T carries both repeated coordinates and the negative sign. Its covariance is `T*U_(a,b)*T^T`; four independent radius boxes may overbound the family but cannot replace those source relations in statistics or structural proofs. The Frobenius norm of R(z) is `sqrt(2)*abs(z)`, while its operator 2-norm is `abs(z)`; a solve/error transfer must state which norm it uses. This is an example of a binding, not a storage rule for Complex symbols. Pair, block and no-region bindings all leave the symbol's Complex sort unchanged, and physical adjoints still need their declared metrics.

**Rule (Per-entry restoration over plain floats)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-restoration`

Let logical coordinate i restore by the exact descriptor map `y_i=s_i*z_i+o_i`, with positive source-free difference scale s_i and an origin o_i only for a point. Bindings checks these definitions and their target meanings; scalar encloses exported coefficients and matrix runs the float map. If `[s_i]` and `[o_i]` enclose their exact definitions, a new candidate c_y gets evaluation bound e_y from `[s_i]*c_z+[o_i]`, and total radius `r_y,i = sup(abs([s_i]))*r_z,i + e_y,i`, evaluated outward. Origins affect centers and their evaluation error, never radii. The numerical-only error relative to the original nominal target also propagates the earlier numerical error through the scale; e_y alone covers just this map at its represented input. This specializes (`def:nrz:shape-normalization`) and (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-affine-conversion`) without requiring any quantity tag on a float leaf.

For two restored selections y and w, covariance is `U_yw = D_s*U_zw*D_t^T`; entry `(i,j)` carries both scale factors `s_i*t_j` and neither origin. Interval coefficient/parameter evaluation encloses this exact covariance parameter, with separate evaluation error and retained paired target versions, kinds and source model. A fixed affine statistical map preserves exact finite-moment assurance; a nonlinear sensitivity remains a declared linearization. For `A: X -> Y`, restoration scales operator entry `(i,j)` by `S_Y,i/S_X,j`; a solve result on `W -> X` uses `S_X,i/S_W,j`. Point-origin removal includes the original equation correction before normalization. The LC spectrum uses the retained similarity `B=t_ref*S^(-1)*A*S` and rate restoration `s=lambda/t_ref`; arbitrary independent row/column scales do not preserve eigenvalues. These are descriptor relations lowered to ordinary float maps, refining (`rule:nrz:shape-eigenproblems`) and (`rule:nrz:shape-sensitivity-propagation`).

**Example (LC symbols without numerical storage)** · `ex:nrz:descriptor-binding-lc-exact`

The LC operator's typed diagonal zeros, the ideal impedance's real-component zero, exact zero entries in the phasor expansion, identity coefficients and fixed references `5 V`, `1/10 A`, `1/2000 s`, one radian, one second and one hertz can remain exact graph definitions with None bindings. Their roles and quotient/angle/rate meanings still require original-expression evidence. Fixed ideal reference L and C can also stay exact when a request uses their declared exact values; varying or measured L and C need numerical bindings and their stated uncertainty. A zero center or a sample at resonance proves no structural zero for a varied family. These choices save region allocation, companion targets, version/source inventory and derivative directions for genuinely fixed symbols. If a kernel needs a constant, its explicit materialization creates a region bound to that exact node and a numerical-error companion symbol; the unmaterialized definition has no numerical companion. Grid-invariant materializations are shared under their complete reuse key. Exact source-free definitions do not acquire physical uncertainty merely by being used numerically, and undefined phase or admittance is never an exact zero output.
