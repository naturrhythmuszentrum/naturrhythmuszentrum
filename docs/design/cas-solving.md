# Solving in declared scalar spaces with graph witnesses · `spec:nrz:cas-solving-concept`

The solving layer discovers exact solutions in the unknowns' declared scalar space. The Complex route uses degree-generic Lagrange resolvents, symmetric-polynomial reduction and exact algebraic extensions; its quadratic lemma is derived by completing the square and reused only as a checked lemma. The Real route uses same-Real arithmetic, derived radicals and certified real cells/counts as specified in (`alg:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-cubic`). Neither route loads a quadratic or cubic formula as a producer primitive. This concept is owned by NRZ in area nrz and states mathematical contracts and proof boundaries; the coordinated symbol-sorts revision specifies later implementation, not a newly delivered capability.

The original concept baseline was `fd3f69c745d819a27ec45368c9a8d73be37a42bd`. Its source parser, heterogeneous graph, conditional witnesses, deterministic bounded search, derivatives, Jacobians, rational square-free certificates and native piecewise calculus remain the foundation. The current `Task::Solve` payload and supplied-certificate machinery are represented by (`spec:nrz:cas-solving-design-contract`); symbolic six-test acceptance is still pending. This revision follows (`spec:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-contract`) and replaces the earlier universal Complex ambient and Real lift/restriction proposal. `Operator::Set { complete }` and an algebraic-root request express intent, never a completeness theorem (`constr:nrz:cas-taylor-newton-roots`), (`tab:nrz:cas-task-walks`).

Calculus remains the first route: derivatives and gcd witnesses classify multiplicities, and a nonsingular Jacobian supports local inversion. Their information does not select a global radical expression or prove global coverage. The admitted supplement is exact field algebra with symmetric functions and explicit logical elimination. A Newton step solves its affine model only; no remainder is discarded to obtain a root of the original predicate. Published mathematics supplies the techniques, while graph objects and search policies below are proposals under (`rule:nrz:cas-cleanroom-scope`).

## Solution relations in the shared graph · `sec:nrz:cas-solving-graph`

**Definition (Request and solution family)** · `def:nrz:cas-solving-request`

A solve request links to the original covered predicate, ordered immutable unknown declarations, exact coefficient domain, checked ambient/placements, admissible unknown region, coefficient region, context and goal. Coefficients and unknowns retain their declared spaces: a,b,c,d are parameters and x is the cubic unknown. A Real unknown with Real-only original dependencies routes to Real; a Complex unknown routes to its Complex fibre over the original Real parameter base, which is a singleton for pure Complex input. The goal distinguishes candidate membership from a complete solution on the requested region and separately names presentation. `AnyExact` admits checked `RealExact`: Real radicals where applicable and cell-selected Real algebraic values elsewhere. `Radicals` retains its existing meaning and does not include a general cell-selected value. The symbolic Real fixture visibly changes its request from `Radicals` to `AnyExact` in S3; S0 records the original request without changing it. Retain the original occurrence root, every original dependency and denominator exclusion (`dec:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-contract`).

The solution-set node links to that predicate and to its ordered coordinate space, parameter region and solution domain. Its membership meaning is the predicate being defined and true at a tuple in that domain. Candidate values, root relations, parameter maps and regional solution families are shared nodes connected by membership or coverage evidence applications. Coordinate positions are semantic; printing order among a finite collection of solutions is not an order on complex numbers. Systems require a finite product coordinate domain and tuple-valued points; the present scalar `Set(AtomicSort)` vocabulary does not by itself provide the needed tuple and family contract. The design must extend that graph vocabulary without encoding a system as unrelated scalar roots.

**Rule (Membership and coverage have different witnesses)** · `rule:nrz:cas-solving-claims`

A candidate witness proves domain membership, definedness and the original predicate after capture-safe same-sort simultaneous substitution. For a system it checks every conjunct at the same tuple. A complete piece witness proves both directions for every coefficient parameter on its named stratum and every unknown in the original admissible region: every represented member solves the equation and every admissible solution is represented. For a Real unknown this means all real roots and no further real roots there, without Complex intermediate relations. For a parametrised family φ on a region T, prove universal membership of φ(t) and recovery of every solution by some t in T. Injectivity or a unique parameter representation requires another theorem. Explicit family binders may not capture original declarations. A cover of a larger coefficient base is not a premise of this local completeness claim (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-piece-completeness`).

A zero set can be empty on a nonempty domain, and a checked contradiction or product argument can prove that complete empty solution set. This is distinct from the piecewise layer's refusal to prove a function equality merely because its requested domain is empty. Multiplicity belongs to polynomial-root evidence, not to set membership: two coincident root expressions denote one set member with a certified multiplicity. A degree-n product identity counts n factors with multiplicity, never n distinct roots without an additional square-free argument.

No result upgrades an assumed cover, a candidate membership proof or exhausted search into completeness. A complete piece retains its no-further-roots theorem while a separate whole-domain request awaits base or fibre coverage. That request additionally needs the exact base cover and a fibre-domain cover for every base point; report `BaseCoveragePending` or `FibreCoveragePending` with explicit unresolved regions. A count-one cell alone proves completeness only in that cell, not over the entire unknown domain. All residuals and used hypotheses remain visible; a local sign, unit, existence or count premise cannot be hidden by closure. Deferred work remains `Unresolved`; `Exhausted` retains only replayed facts (`rule:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-stratum-evidence`), (`tab:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-outcomes`).

**Construction (Excluded branches and regional composition)** · `constr:nrz:cas-solving-regions`

Original denominators define excluded regions. For N(x)/D(x) = 0, the exact solution condition is N(x) = 0 and D(x) ≠ 0 on the original defined domain. Clearing D proves an equivalence only there. The region D = 0 remains recorded as excluded, including factors canceled from a derived quotient; it is not solved as a new permissible branch of the original equation. By contrast, dividing an ordinary polynomial equation by a prospective pivot h requires solving its h = 0 branch separately. These two kinds of exclusion must never share an unexplained “discarded” flag.

For example, (x² − 1)/(x − 1) = 0 has original domain x ≠ 1. The identity x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1) gives the only admissible root −1; substitution gives a zero numerator and denominator −2. The numerator's other root 1 is excluded. Conversely t x = 1 has x = 1/t on t ≠ 0 and no solution on t = 0, while t x = 0 has x = 0 on t ≠ 0 and the whole x-domain on t = 0. These statements follow directly by multiplication and zero-product reasoning, and demonstrate why a denominator guard and a parameter degree drop have different consequences.

Parameter cases and unknown-domain cases refine the same original solution relation. Restrict to a proved region, prove complete local solution equivalence and close its exact local hypotheses; this completed piece needs no enclosing cover. To claim a larger requested domain, separately prove its cover and eliminate the closed piece claims. Unknown-dependent guards are checked after candidate or parameter substitution. Unions preserve overlaps as root-set semantics; overlapping root sets must agree there, and a numbered scalar branch advertised as one function additionally needs compatibility. Boundary singletons and reached-guard obligations remain. Undefined predicates do not become false guards or otherwise branches (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-conditional-closure`), (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-case-elimination`).

The rational affine interval decider does not decide symbolic multivariate parameter hypersurfaces. Proved-total polynomial zero/nonzero splits and RealSigns trichotomy establish finite covers without deciding each child's nonemptiness. Retain undecided children; prune only with emptiness evidence. Real signs need exact Real expressions and their scoped sign derivations. Base facts reach a Complex family piece only through its projection-membership evidence, never by ordering Complex coordinates (`rule:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-stratum-evidence`), (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-count`).

## Declared scalar spaces and exact coefficient algebras · `sec:nrz:cas-solving-complex`

**Decision (Declared unknown spaces and explicit ambient placements)** · `dec:nrz:cas-solving-complex-field`

Declarations and binder slots have immutable exact scalar spaces. Real-only arithmetic has Real ambient, Complex-only arithmetic has Complex ambient, and mixed Real/Complex arithmetic has Complex ambient through the registered Real-in-Complex placement. A Real expression alone has no placement-induced cases. In a Complex use it has a separate native case retaining its original local expression and Real binding frame; the placement's real-locus incidence supplies that piece and leaves the rest undefined. Spaces form a graph with edge-specific piece definitions, operation agreements and law transfers, not an inclusion chain or an inferred promotion path (`dec:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-partial-views`), (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-space-contract`), (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-placement`).

Route Real unknowns with Real-only original dependencies entirely through Real proofs. A reachable Complex declaration, including under `Re(c)` or cancellation, yields `RealProblemHasComplexSymbol` before orbit work; unused header declarations do not. Pure Complex requests retain their original coefficient algebra over a singleton Real base; mixed requests use local Complex families over the original Real coordinates. A missing placement is `SpacePlacementUnavailable`, and multiple admissible ambients without a choice give `AmbientSpaceAmbiguous`. These capability boundaries never retag a declaration. `RealRestriction` and its Real/Complex round trip are removed without a reader, alias or replacement conversion; same-sort affine `SolutionTransport` and actual `ConjugationTransport` propositions remain (`alg:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-routing`), (`dec:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-preservation-decision`).

Exact coefficient constants Q and Q(i), rational-function domains and selected quotient algebras remain distinct from declaration spaces. A typed Complex literal with real numerical value is still Complex. Same-sort finite evaluation requires its actual bindings, denominator units and prior selected-root existence; canonical exact-Free interpretation remains narrow. In particular, the five symbolic Complex fixtures already have correct Complex declarations and initial maps. Their first coefficient failure must be diagnosed before any repair claim, never attributed to a missing Real bridge (`req:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-complex-diagnosis`), (`rule:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-complex-algebra`).

A formal nonzero rational function may vanish at specialization. Every inverse retains its regional nonzero condition and exceptional branch; arbitrary parameter relations require certified reduction. A registered space supplies only its own laws, and a placement transfers only explicitly registered agreements on its pieces. Complex has no order; no field or commutativity law is inherited by a prospective space merely through a placement.

**Construction (Tower nodes and exact reduction)** · `constr:nrz:cas-solving-tower`

An extension node shares its parent field, the defining polynomial, an adjoined root node and selection evidence. Typical dependencies are ω² + ω + 1 = 0, s² = Δ and u³ = U; ω also has order three, and an oriented complex embedding can impose Im(ω) > 0. Polynomial expressions in each adjoined generator reduce by its defining relation, recursively through earlier levels. A fixed monic irreducible relation gives a field basis of powers below its degree; equality reduces to zero coefficients. Proof edges retain reduction identities and coefficient equalities. A relation node without irreducibility evidence must not be labeled a field.

For a nonzero reduced h modulo a certified irreducible m, extended gcd yields A h + B m = 1, so A is its inverse in the quotient. Replay checks this identity and all lower-level inversions. A vanishing reduced expression is exactly zero; a nonvanishing normal form is nonzero only in the certified field or with suitable component evidence. Distinct tower presentations may describe equal numbers. Structural interning does not prove such equality; a checked common extension or embedding transports their defining relations and selections. Failure to construct that transport within bounds remains unresolved. These are polynomial and field arguments, supported by (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-exact-algebra`).

**Procedure (Dynamic evaluation and zero tests)** · `alg:nrz:cas-solving-dynamic-evaluation`

When a monic defining polynomial m is square-free but irreducibility is unknown, treat K[t]/(m) as a finite reduced algebra, potentially a product of fields. Do not invert every nonzero polynomial remainder. To test h at an unspecified root of m, compute a certified gcd g = gcd(m,h). If g = 1, the Bezout identity proves h is a unit on every component. If g = m, h vanishes on every component. If 0 < degree(g) < degree(m), split the root-selection region into g(t) = 0 and (m/g)(t) = 0. Square-freeness proves the two factors coprime; their product identity proves exhaustive coverage and their Bezout identity proves disjointness. On the first component h vanishes; on the second it is invertible. A previously selected root follows only the components compatible with its selection, or leaves that compatibility pending.

This is dynamic evaluation in the sense of Della Dora, Dicrescenzo and Duval: discovering a gcd refines the graph's component regions rather than declaring the entire computation invalid (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-dynamic-literature`). For example, t² − 1 is square-free, but the nonzero remainder t − 1 vanishes on the t = 1 component; gcd(t² − 1,t − 1) = t − 1 exposes exactly the necessary split. If m has repeated factors, first use derivative/gcd evidence to pass to its square-free root relation and retain original multiplicities separately. Nilpotents in an unreduced quotient are never interpreted as ordinary complex numbers.

Gcd computation at an upper level may require a coefficient inverse at a lower level; recursively split there first, then recompute or transport the dependent upper tower in each surviving component. Monicity itself may require a leading-coefficient zero/nonzero split. Over concrete characteristic-zero algebraic coefficients, finite degrees and recursive exact gcds supply a terminating algebraic procedure when resources are unbounded. The service always has finite work, bit, term, tower-depth, extension-degree and component limits, so a particular run may stop before that mathematical procedure completes. Over symbolic strata the admitted coefficient reduction determines what can be decided; an arbitrary named relation is not a zero oracle.

**Rule (Radicals are root relations with linked selections)** · `rule:nrz:cas-solving-selections`

A radical denotes a same-sort root relation w^n = U with positive n, exact coefficient domain and an explicit selection obligation. Complex root adjunction uses the named closure theorem for a nonconstant polynomial, without selecting a unique embedding. Real odd roots and nonnegative square roots use their Real existence/sign rules; a general polynomial is not assumed to split in Real. Selected values require prior existence on the same region. A Real count-one cell supplies an exact algebraic selection through independent root-count evidence and root-independent endpoints; it is not a radical. Complex binomial coverage can use one selected root times all roots of unity with a checked full product. Auxiliary choices must not alter the represented solution set (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-count`).

A principal radical is an additional convention: for nonzero z, use Arg(z) in (−π,π] and exp((log|z| + i Arg(z))/n); at z = 0 the root is zero. The convention has an explicit cut and does not establish analyticity across it. The real odd root of a negative real input is a different selection. Algebraic verification of a chosen radical can use its polynomial relation and a proved isolating region or argument-sector condition; symbolic selection existence or uniqueness that lacks a checker remains an obligation. Merely setting `Branch::PrincipalComplex` cannot discharge it.

Refuse principal identities such as ∛a ∛b = ∛(ab) and √(z²) = z without appropriate branch proofs. For a = b = −1 the principal cube roots multiply to exp(2πi/3), whereas the principal cube root of ab = 1 is 1. For z = −1, principal √(z²) = 1 ≠ z. Squaring or taking a power is valid polynomial arithmetic; applying its inverse is a multivalued step with coverage conditions. In the cubic, uv = −p/3 is a shared selection constraint linking u³ = U and v³ = V, not a rewrite multiplying two principal cube-root functions. It leaves three compatible pairs when p ≠ 0, rather than accepting nine independent pairs. Branch-cut and unwinding-number references motivate the explicit convention; no logarithmic simplification is needed for the algebraic derivation (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-branches`).

**Construction (Conjugation and realness)** · `constr:nrz:cas-solving-realness`

Conjugation, RealPart and ImagPart are typed operations. Conjugation preserves Complex sums and products and transports actual defining relations and selections; RealPart and ImagPart return distinct Real expressions. The component equations characterize a Complex value, but never substitute a Complex expression into a Real declaration. A proof that conjugation fixes a Complex value does not change that value's declaration sort. An abstract root of a real-coefficient polynomial is not automatically fixed by conjugation, and principal radicals do not commute across their cut without a side condition.

Base Real facts remain in their original local context. On a mixed piece, `family.identity` expands to the registered placement/component definitions, exact identities and case congruence; it may prove conjugation fixes base-dependent coefficients. Projection membership must justify every imported sign or unit. It cannot infer a sign for a Complex fibre coefficient or borrow positivity from a sibling stratum (`rule:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-stratum-evidence`), (`tab:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-certificates`).

For a Complex calculation, v = conjugate(u) and conjugate(ω) = ω² imply that each ω^k u + ω^(−k)v is fixed by conjugation. This remains a statement about those Complex values, useful in Complex examples and mixed families. A Real-unknown solve instead constructs Real radicals or cell roots and certifies their complete count within Real. Exact signs and Sturm counts, with singleton and exterior counts, replace the former Complex-solve/restriction route (`alg:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-cubic`), (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-piece-completeness`).

**Decision (Admitted arithmetic crates behind CAS budgets)** · `dec:nrz:cas-solving-num-crates`

Use the admitted rust-num family for exact arithmetic: num-bigint supplies arbitrary-precision signed and unsigned integers; num-integer supplies integer gcd and integer-root support, with a proposed perfect-power candidate verified by exact exponentiation; num-rational supplies the backing rational representation; num-complex supplies the Gaussian-rational pair representation and exact field arithmetic where its generic operations apply; num-traits supplies basic algebraic vocabulary such as zero and one. No other new dependency is proposed. The baseline lock already contains num-bigint, num-complex, num-integer and num-traits; num-rational is a new admitted addition for the later implementation.

The public budgeted `Rational` contract remains local. Replacing its private sign/magnitude arithmetic by a normalized num-rational value revises (`dec:nrz:cas-design-rationals`) and the explicit exclusion of num-rational in (`dec:nrz:cas-design-crate`). Preserve exact parsing, canonical zero, positive denominator, coprimality, bit ceilings and refusal semantics. Crate arithmetic is not itself metered: wrappers must reserve conservative work and intermediate-size bounds before calling it, then check actual sizes. Operations whose internal work cannot be safely bounded in the chosen API need a smaller admitted route or a refusal, not an unmetered call. Integer nth-root operations can return a floor root; equality of its nth power with the input is the exact perfect-power test. Sign and even-root restrictions are checked separately.

One degree-generic polynomial algorithm should work over each effective tower level through a fallible, budget-aware arithmetic capability. num-traits alone provides neither a field proof nor a suitable fallible inversion contract; the CAS adapter supplies those obligations. Do not implement metered arithmetic through infallible operator traits that hide exhaustion. Gaussian rational operations use exact rational components, never floating Complex values or their transcendental methods. These crates provide no extension tower, symbolic parameter strata, embedding selection, dynamic-evaluation cover, proof kernel or mathematical resource policy. Version/feature selection and conservative operation bounds belong to the later design and vendor audit; no dependency source or external CAS implementation was used here.

## Blind discovery by degree-generic methods · `sec:nrz:cas-solving-discovery`

**Decision (Lagrange resolvents and symmetric reduction)** · `dec:nrz:cas-solving-route`

Choose Lagrange resolvents over a direct radical ansatz. They generate useful expressions from permutation actions on formal roots; symmetric reduction then computes their coefficient equations. The selection has a degree-independent reason: search for an invariant with a smaller orbit, solve its smaller-degree equation, and recover the original roots by checked algebra. The cubic's two Fourier resolvents emerge from the nontrivial characters of a three-cycle; no producer is given their final coefficient identities or a proposed u + v. The historical method is attributed to (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-lagrange`); its exact cubic calculation is reconstructed below.

The generic inventory consists of derivative/gcd analysis, degree and leading-coefficient splits, monic normalization, translation eliminating the subleading coefficient, finite permutation and cyclic-character generation, bounded polynomial invariant generation, orbit construction, symmetric reduction, recursive solution of smaller-degree resolvents, root adjunction, exact linear recovery and product certification. For f(x) = a_n x^n + a_(n−1) x^(n−1) + … with a_n ≠ 0 in characteristic zero, x = y − a_(n−1)/(n a_n) eliminates y^(n−1): its coefficient is n a_n times the shift plus a_(n−1). This single rule includes completing the square. Coefficients of every substituted polynomial are computed by binomial expansion, never by a degree-indexed formula table.

Introduce formal root symbols with elementary symmetric functions e_j and the identity product(t − x_j) = t^n − e_1 t^(n−1) + … + (−1)^n e_n. This identity follows by choosing a root term from j factors; comparing coefficients links e_j to the monic input. These symbols are algebraic auxiliaries in a splitting extension, not output candidates already known in radicals. A general splitting-extension theorem supplies their existential interpretation. Final output coverage will have its own direct factor-product proof, so it does not depend on finding numerical representatives for these auxiliaries.

For a generated cycle of length m, adjoin a primitive mth root of unity ζ and form all character sums R_k = sum over j from 0 to m−1 of ζ^(k j) x_j. Include the trivial character. Verify the action of the cycle on each sum; raise to the computed character order to obtain cycle invariants, then enumerate their full permutation orbit. Deduplicate only after polynomial equality checking. The product of t minus every distinct orbit value is invariant under all root permutations, so each of its coefficients is symmetric. Orbit coverage requires the complete finite action, not a sampled collection. A smaller orbit gives a candidate resolvent of smaller degree; it is a search opportunity, not yet a root formula.

For each symmetric homogeneous polynomial, use lexicographic monomial order with x_1 before x_2 and so on. Its leading exponents λ_1 ≥ … ≥ λ_n give the leading term of e_1^(λ_1−λ_2) e_2^(λ_2−λ_3) … e_n^λ_n with coefficient one. Subtract the matching multiple and repeat. Leading monomials strictly decrease within finitely many monomials of the fixed degree; this proves termination of the symmetric-reduction algorithm. Handle each homogeneous degree separately. Replay expands the proposed expression in the e_j and checks the coefficient identity, so a mistaken symmetry claim cannot create a theorem. The method works over the coefficient field containing ζ, fixed by the root permutations (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-symmetric-literature`).

Cyclic characters alone are not promised to find cheap resolvents in every degree. A second degree-generic source enumerates bounded set partitions of root indices and sums of block products, then their permutation orbits. At degree four it includes x_1 x_2 + x_3 x_4 and its orbit of three pairings, which supplies Ferrari's resolvent cubic after symmetric reduction. The invariant is generated from the partition type, not stored as a quartic identity. Exact recovery must still be proved; discovering a small orbit does not automatically solve the original equation. This provides a later quartic route without claiming its implementation or importing a quartic test oracle (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-galois-literature`).

**Definition (Search states, priorities and stopping)** · `def:nrz:cas-solving-search-state`

Each state links the original predicate and goal to its declared unknown space, checked ambient/placements, exact coefficient algebra, base/family and current stratum, candidate roots, selections and proof obligations. Identity includes full context, exclusions, requested presentation and support. Candidate membership, complete roots on one piece and coverage of a larger requested domain are separate strengths. Complex states additionally carry orbit/resolvent and inverse-recovery work. Real states carry same-Real composition/division, sign derivations, radical existence, signed-remainder chains and cell/count obligations; no Complex formal splitting anchor is used. Regions and selections are immutable (`def:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-count`).

Retain the positive-cost deterministic queues and independently metered replay of (`alg:nrz:cas-design-queues`). For Complex discovery, prioritize strictly smaller resolvents and enumerate declarations, cycles, characters, partitions and monomials canonically; do not recognize cubic coefficient patterns or preload radical targets. The separate Real strategy classifies degree and discriminant strata, derives radicals by expanding generated root-variable sums and comparing coefficients, then proves real counts. This is the explicit policy extension in (`alg:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-cubic`), not a claim that the Complex orbit audit describes it. Both strategies meter attempted work and preserve exact local proof domains.

Charge normalization, attempted invariant construction, failed symmetry tests, orbit comparisons, zero tests, each gcd division, coefficient bit growth, component refinement and every proof dependency. Add explicit caps for root auxiliaries, permutation actions, invariant seed degree/count, orbit size, resolvent depth, tower height, product of extension degrees, component regions and pending recovery goals. Existing source, graph, unfolded-size, arithmetic, witness, output and replay ceilings continue to apply. A permutation orbit may have factorial size; preflight or lazy enumeration must stop before allocating it beyond the allowance. No wall-clock deadline determines a mathematical answer, and exhausting an invariant budget never proves insolubility by radicals.

For the cubic route below, the cyclic generator needs the two nontrivial characters, exponent three computed from their order, and at most six permutations to certify an orbit of size two. The cubic powers have at most ten root monomials each; an unreduced degree-six product in three roots has at most twenty-eight. The cheaper product route first checks r r′ and cubes that identity. The recursive quadratic needs two permutations and character order two, or the same generic translation followed by a binomial root relation. These finite bounds make the proposed route plausible within a declared budget; they are not measurements of the present solver. The design must pin an adequate complete policy and exact service limits, and implementation must demonstrate blind completion under those limits before claiming the acceptance target.

## The quadratic lemma from first principles · `sec:nrz:cas-solving-quadratic`

**Example (General quadratic and its lower-degree regions)** · `ex:nrz:cas-solving-quadratic`

Start independently with A x² + B x + C = 0. On A ≠ 0, put h = B/(2A), y = x + h. Direct expansion gives A(y − h)² + B(y − h) + C = A y² + (B − 2Ah)y + Ah² − Bh + C = A y² + C − B²/(4A). Thus y² = (B² − 4AC)/(4A²). This is completion of the square, generated by the degree-n translation at n = 2.

Let s² = D_2 = B² − 4AC, with any admissible square-root selection. The two values x_± = (−B ± s)/(2A) satisfy the equation. Independently multiply their factors: x_+ + x_- = −B/A and x_+ x_- = (B² − s²)/(4A²) = C/A, hence A(x − x_+)(x − x_-) = A x² + B x + C. Zero-product reasoning proves complete coverage. If D_2 = 0 the two expressions coincide at −B/(2A), with multiplicity two; otherwise their difference s/A is nonzero and both are simple. Choosing the other s permutes them and leaves the set unchanged.

Over real coefficients, D_2 > 0 yields two real roots, D_2 = 0 one repeated real root, and D_2 < 0 no real roots, while the complex set still has two factors. The negative case follows because (2Ax + B)² cannot be negative for real x. On A = 0, B ≠ 0 yields x = −C/B; on A = B = 0, C ≠ 0 gives an empty set and C = 0 the whole stated domain. Those regions cover coefficient space and precede every division. Intersect with the requested x-domain and any original exclusions. The derived quadratic lemma carries all these guards and this product proof; it is reusable without making its formula a trusted primitive.

The character construction reaches the same lemma: for two formal roots, r = x_1 − x_2 has r² = (x_1 + x_2)² − 4x_1x_2 = e_1² − 4e_2. The order-two root swap changes r to −r, so r² has orbit size one; the inverse linear transform is x_1 = (e_1 + r)/2 and x_2 = (e_1 − r)/2. With e_1 = −B/A and e_2 = C/A, this reproduces the completed-square relation. These identities explain the common machinery without preloading the quadratic formula.

## Worked symbolic cubic and complete coverage · `sec:nrz:cas-solving-cubic`

**Construction (Translation and formal roots)** · `constr:nrz:cas-solving-cubic-shift`

Let F(x) = a x³ + b x² + c x + d. First split a = 0 from a ≠ 0. The zero branch is the quadratic b x² + c x + d, including all its linear, constant and identically-zero cases from (`ex:nrz:cas-solving-quadratic`). On a ≠ 0 set β = b/a, γ = c/a, δ = d/a and h = β/3. Substitution x = y − h gives F(y − h)/a = y³ + (β − 3h)y² + (3h² − 2βh + γ)y − h³ + βh² − γh + δ. Therefore f(y) = y³ + p y + q, where p = γ − β²/3 = (3ac − b²)/(3a²) and q = δ − βγ/3 + 2β³/27 = (27a²d − 9abc + 2b³)/(27a³). Each denominator is permitted by a ≠ 0 and characteristic zero. The inverse x = y − h is bijective and transports solution coverage.

For formal roots y_1,y_2,y_3 of f, expansion of product(y − y_j) gives e_1 = y_1 + y_2 + y_3 = 0, e_2 = y_1y_2 + y_1y_3 + y_2y_3 = p and e_3 = y_1y_2y_3 = −q. The generic character construction at degree three supplies ω with ω² + ω + 1 = 0, hence ω³ = 1 and ω ≠ 1, and the two nontrivial sums r = y_1 + ω y_2 + ω² y_3 and r′ = y_1 + ω² y_2 + ω y_3. The trivial sum is e_1 = 0. At this point no u or v has been proposed.

**Argument (Computed symmetric reductions)** · `arg:nrz:cas-solving-resolvent-identities`

Multiplying the two linear forms gives r r′ = y_1² + y_2² + y_3² + (ω + ω²)(y_1y_2 + y_1y_3 + y_2y_3) = e_1² − 3e_2. Cubing and adding gives r³ + r′³ = 2 sum(y_j³) − 3 sum over i ≠ j of y_i² y_j + 12y_1y_2y_3. Independently, e_1³ = sum(y_j³) + 3 sum over i ≠ j of y_i²y_j + 6e_3 and e_1e_2 = sum over i ≠ j of y_i²y_j + 3e_3. Eliminating the two sums gives r³ + r′³ = 2e_1³ − 9e_1e_2 + 27e_3. These expansions are the results that the symmetric-reduction algorithm must compute and replay, not entries in its rule inventory.

Under the root cycle, r becomes ω²r and r′ becomes ωr′; interchanging y_2 and y_3 swaps them. A three-cycle and this transposition generate every permutation of three roots, so r³ and r′³ form the complete orbit, possibly with coincident values on a special parameter region. The generic orbit has size two; specialization can coalesce its values, in which case the specialized orbit product retains the repeated factor rather than silently dropping its multiplicity. Their sum and product are symmetric. Substituting e_1 = 0, e_2 = p and e_3 = −q yields r r′ = −3p, r³ + r′³ = −27q and r³r′³ = (−3p)³ = −27p³. Thus their resolvent equation is z² + 27q z − 27p³ = 0, since (z − r³)(z − r′³) expands to exactly that polynomial.

Apply the independently derived quadratic lemma. Set Δ = q²/4 + p³/27 and adjoin s with s² = Δ. The quadratic discriminant is (27q)² + 108p³ = 2916Δ = (54s)². Consequently z_± = (−27q ± 54s)/2 = 27(−q/2 ± s). Put U = −q/2 + s and V = −q/2 − s. Direct multiplication gives U + V = −q and UV = q²/4 − s² = −p³/27. These equations remain valid when Δ = 0; a repeated resolvent root is not an inversion failure.

**Construction (Linked radicals and inverse transform)** · `constr:nrz:cas-solving-cubic-recovery`

On p ≠ 0, UV = −p³/27 ≠ 0 proves both U and V nonzero. Adjoin any u with u³ = U and set v = −p/(3u). The nonzero condition on u follows from U ≠ 0. Then uv = −p/3 and v³ = −p³/(27U) = V. The scaled character values r = 3u and r′ = 3v have the required cubes and product. Taking independent cube roots of U and V would give nine pairs and would not establish these equations; the product constraint admits exactly the three pairs (ω^k u, ω^(−k)v), for k = 0,1,2, since every nonzero cube root differs by a cube root of unity.

The character matrix is invertible. Multiplying by its inverse, using 1 + ω + ω² = 0, gives y_1 = (r + r′)/3, y_2 = (ω²r + ωr′)/3 and y_3 = (ωr + ω²r′)/3. Thus the output family is x_k = −b/(3a) + ω^k u + ω^(−k)v for k = 0,1,2. This sum of two radicals is a consequence of linear inversion; it was not a search ansatz. Replacing u by ω^j u permutes k, and changing s to −s exchanges U and V and exchanges the paired roles. The output set is independent of those auxiliary choices.

Completeness is checked anew without the formal root names. Define Y_k = ω^k u + ω^(−k)v. Their sum is zero. Their pairwise product sum is −3uv: expanding sum(Y_k²) cancels u² and v² terms and leaves 6uv, so the pairwise sum is −sum(Y_k²)/2. Their product is u³ + v³: first multiply (ωu + ω²v)(ω²u + ωv) = u² − uv + v², then multiply by u + v. Hence product over k of (y − Y_k) = y³ − 3uv y − (u³ + v³) = y³ + p y + q. Multiplying by a and applying x = y − h proves F(x) = a product over k of (x − x_k).

Over the exact Complex ambient field, a nonzero product of nonzero factors is nonzero, so this identity proves the complete Complex solution set on its coefficient piece, with repetitions counted by the product. Preserve original domain exclusions and same-sort membership evidence. Three substitutions or the degree alone do not replace the product theorem. A requested whole coefficient domain separately requires its stratum cover; this Complex calculation is not the Real-unknown route.

**Construction (Degenerate pairing and discriminant regions)** · `constr:nrz:cas-solving-cubic-cases`

On p = 0, handle f(y) = y³ + q directly by the degree-generic binomial-root rule. If q ≠ 0, adjoin w with w³ = −q. The identity (y − w)(y − ωw)(y − ω²w) = y³ − w³ proves all three roots; they are distinct because w ≠ 0 and the cube roots of unity are distinct. If q = 0, y = 0 is the only root, of multiplicity three. The resolvent has roots 0 and −27q on this region; no attempted v = −p/(3u) is evaluated at a zero u. These p = 0 cases join the p ≠ 0 branch through a proved parameter cover.

The depressed discriminant is D = −4p³ − 27q² = −108Δ. To verify its repeated-root condition independently, a common root t of f and f′ satisfies 3t² + p = 0 and (2p/3)t + q = 0. For p ≠ 0 this forces t = −3q/(2p) and substitution into the first equation gives 27q² + 4p³ = 0. Conversely those equations produce a common root when that expression vanishes. For p = 0 a common root requires t = q = 0. Thus repeated roots occur exactly on Δ = 0. Substitution of the displayed p and q gives the original discriminant a⁴D = b²c² − 4ac³ − 4b³d − 27a²d² + 18abcd; it has the same zero condition only on a ≠ 0.

On Δ = 0 and p ≠ 0, let t = 3q/(2p). The relation 27q² = −4p³ gives t² = −p/3 and t³ = −q/2, so f(y) = (y − 2t)(y + t)² by expansion. Since t ≠ 0, 2t is simple and −t has multiplicity two. The p = q = 0 branch has the triple root already described. For complex coefficients only zero versus nonzero is meaningful; a sign classification of Δ needs real coefficients and a proof that Δ is real.

Within a Complex-unknown calculation whose coefficient realness is explicitly proved on its piece, `Δ<0` implies `p<0`. Choose s = i√(−Δ) with a positive Real square witness used through its checked placement/component agreement. Then V = conjugate(U), UV = (−p/3)³ and |u|² = −p/3 imply that the linked v equals conjugate(u); the three Y_k are fixed by conjugation and distinct. This proves facts about Complex values on that piece. For a Real unknown, use the three Real count-one cells with same-Real endpoints and full exterior counts instead; no Complex intermediate enters its proof closure (`alg:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-cubic`).

On a Real coefficient stratum with `Δ>0`, the Real route chooses s = sqrt(Δ) and the unique Real cube roots of −q/2 ± s. Cubing their product and using real cube injectivity proves uv = −p/3 without division by u or v; exact expansion proves u+v is a root and a RealRootCount certificate proves there are no others. This includes p = 0 with one zero radical. On Δ = 0 use the checked repeated-root factors, and on `Δ<0` use Real cells/counts. The symbol-sorts design calls this radicand D, while this worked Complex derivation calls it Δ; both equal (q/2)²+(p/3)³ and have the opposite sign to the conventional discriminant (`alg:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-cubic`).

## Independent examples and the radical boundary · `sec:nrz:cas-solving-examples`

The radical derivations below illustrate the Complex-unknown route and its exact component identities. A Real-unknown request uses the separate same-Real radical or cell/count route; the displayed Complex intermediates are not imported into that proof closure.

**Example (Bombelli through Gaussian intermediates)** · `ex:nrz:cas-solving-bombelli`

For F(x) = x³ − 15x − 4 the shift is zero, p = −15 and q = −4. Independently compute Δ = (−4)²/4 + (−15)³/27 = 4 − 125 = −121. Choose s = 11i; then U = 2 + 11i and V = 2 − 11i, with U + V = 4 and UV = 4 + 121 = 125 = −p³/27. Direct expansion gives (2 + i)³ = 8 + 12i − 6 − i = 2 + 11i and (2 − i)³ = 2 − 11i. Thus u = 2 + i and v = 2 − i satisfy both root relations and uv = 5 = −p/3.

The unscaled resolvent values are r = 6 + 3i and r′ = 6 − 3i, with r r′ = 45, r³ = 54 + 297i and r′³ = 54 − 297i. Their cube sum is 108 and product is 54² + 297² = 2916 + 88209 = 91125, so the resolvent is z² − 108z + 91125. These agree with −27q = 108 and −27p³ = 91125 without a floating calculation. Fourier inversion with ω = (−1 + i√3)/2 gives u + v = 4, 2Re(ωu) = −2 − √3 and 2Re(ω²u) = −2 + √3.

Check the answer independently in the original variable: (x − 4)((x + 2)² − 3) = (x − 4)(x² + 4x + 1) = x³ − 15x − 4. The roots are distinct because √3 is positive and less than 2, so the two negative roots differ and neither equals 4. The factor identity proves coverage; conjugation of the displayed paired radicals proves realness. Complex intermediate arithmetic is essential to this derivation even though the final expressions are real.

**Example (Irreducible cubic with three real roots)** · `ex:nrz:cas-solving-irreducibilis`

For F(x) = x³ − 3x + 1, independently p = −3, q = 1 and Δ = 1/4 − 1 = −3/4. Take s = i√3/2, giving U = (−1 + i√3)/2 = ω and V = ω², with U + V = −1 and UV = 1 = −p³/27. The resolvent cubes are 27ω and 27ω², their sum is −27 and product 729; hence z² + 27z + 729. Choose u³ = ω and v = 1/u. Since |ω| = 1, |u| = 1, so v = conjugate(u) and v³ = ω². The complete root family is u + u^(−1), ωu + ω²u^(−1), and ω²u + ωu^(−1).

For an explicit independent interpretation choose u = exp(2πi/9). The three values become 2cos(2π/9), 2cos(8π/9) and 2cos(14π/9), the last equal to 2cos(4π/9). This trigonometric display is an explanatory identity, not an additional discovery primitive or a real-radical formula. For each t = ω^k u, t³ + t^(−3) = ω + ω² = −1, and expansion of (t + t^(−1))³ gives F(t + t^(−1)) = 0. Independently F(−2) = −1, F(−1) = 3, F(0) = 1, F(1) = −1 and F(2) = 3. Continuity supplies a root in each of (−2,−1), (0,1) and (1,2), so all three are distinct and real; a degree-three nonzero polynomial has at most three distinct roots by successive factor division.

The only possible rational roots are ±1, and neither vanishes, so this cubic is irreducible over Q. The casus irreducibilis theorem excludes an all-real radical tower over the coefficient field, under its stated hypotheses (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-galois-literature`). It does not exclude exact Real algebraic values or the explanatory Complex/trigonometric display. A Real request uses `AnyExact` with `RealExact`: same-Real selected roots in certified cells, whose total counts prove all roots on the piece. A separate demand for real radicals retains that complete answer at `AnyExact` and reports `RequestedRealRadicalsUnavailable` for presentation. The rational sign intervals independently check the three-root result; no Complex detour is authorized (`dec:nrz:cas-symbol-sorts-real-contract`).

**Example (Repeated root and multiplicity evidence)** · `ex:nrz:cas-solving-repeated`

For F(x) = x³ − 3x + 2, p = −3, q = 2 and Δ = 2²/4 + (−3)³/27 = 1 − 1 = 0. Thus U = V = −1. Select the real roots u = v = −1, with uv = 1; then r = r′ = −3 and r³ = r′³ = −27. The resolvent is z² + 54z + 729 = (z + 27)². Fourier inversion gives −2, 1 and 1. A principal complex cube root of −1 would be a different auxiliary choice and would still require the pairing constraint.

Independently multiply (x − 1)²(x + 2) = (x² − 2x + 1)(x + 2) = x³ − 3x + 2. Also F′ = 3x² − 3 = 3(x − 1)(x + 1), and −F/2 + x F′/6 = x − 1 is a Bezout certificate for their monic gcd, with the displayed factorizations giving both divisibilities. At x = 1, F = F′ = 0 but F″ = 6 ≠ 0; at x = −2, F′ = 9 ≠ 0. The complete set is {−2,1}, with multiplicities one and two, not three distinct values.

**Example (Zero depressed linear coefficient)** · `ex:nrz:cas-solving-p-zero`

For F(x) = x³ + 8, p = 0, q = 8 and Δ = 8²/4 = 16. With s = 4, U = 0 and V = −8; the unscaled resolvent is z² + 216z = z(z + 216). Its product is zero, so dividing by a cube root of U would be invalid. The separate binomial route chooses w = −2 since (−2)³ = −8. Multiplication by the cube roots of unity gives −2 and 1 ± i√3.

Independently, (x + 2)(x² − 2x + 4) = x³ + 8. Completing the square in the second factor yields (x − 1)² = −3, hence the two nonreal roots just displayed. F′ = 3x² is nonzero at each of these roots because none is zero; all multiplicities are one. This example exercises the p = 0 branch even though Δ ≠ 0, and its real solution set is the singleton {−2}.

**Caveat (General quintic and an honest stop)** · `cav:nrz:cas-solving-quintic`

Take a_5 x⁵ + a_4 x⁴ + a_3 x³ + a_2 x² + a_1 x + a_0 with algebraically independent coefficients and a_5 ≠ 0. The same translation x = y − a_4/(5a_5) eliminates y⁴, because the new coefficient is 5a_5 times the shift plus a_4. Derivative/gcd reasoning and algebraic-root requests still apply. They do not imply a radical formula. The generic polynomial has Galois group S_5 over its rational function coefficient field, including the Gaussian-rational constant extension; S_5 contains the nonabelian simple group A_5 and is not solvable. The characteristic-zero radical-solvability criterion therefore forbids a general radical formula, which is the Abel–Ruffini theorem (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-galois-literature`). The obstruction schema is degree-generic: the generic degree-n polynomial has group S_n, and for n ≥ 5 that group contains a nonsolvable S_5 subgroup. This is a theorem about generic independent coefficients, not about every polynomial of degree five or higher; the producer must not replace its hypotheses by a degree-five-only switch.

A roots-by-radicals request should stop on a checked generic-polynomial/Galois obstruction, returning an algebraic-root solution description where permitted and `Unresolved` for the radical form, with a precise obstruction reason and its theorem provenance. Registering that obstruction requires the genericity and field hypotheses and a named checker theorem; until that checker exists, the executable reason is the unsupported generic radical route, not a sealed impossibility proof. The request must not spin through larger invariant searches after reaching its declared boundary. For particular quintics whose Galois structure is unknown, bounded attempts may end `Unresolved` or `Exhausted`; degree alone is not a proof of nonsolvability. For example x⁵ − 2 has the complete family ζ_5^k w with w⁵ = 2 and k = 0,…,4, verified by the binomial product identity.

An algebraic-root request carries the original polynomial, coefficient field, ambient field, multiplicities and selection obligations. A generic square-free quintic has five roots in an algebraic closure, but five distinct selected scalar nodes require a splitting or isolation witness; writing five identical unresolved requests supplies neither distinctness nor coverage. The relation “all roots of this polynomial in this domain” can itself be a complete implicit set description when its equivalence to the original predicate is proved. It does not satisfy an explicit-radical output goal. A real-domain answer needs real-root isolation or a retained real-membership predicate, and any ordered selection remains separately pending.

## First capabilities beyond the cubic · `sec:nrz:cas-solving-next-slices`

**Procedure (Rational polynomials, rational equations, systems and inverses)** · `alg:nrz:cas-solving-next-slices`

First broaden univariate polynomial solving over Q using the existing derivative and square-free layers. Clear rational coefficient denominators with a nonzero scalar, remove integer content and retain the exact primitive polynomial identity. The rational-root theorem bounds a reduced root r/s by r dividing the constant coefficient and s dividing the leading coefficient: substituting and reducing modulo coprime r and s proves those divisibilities. Split off a zero constant coefficient first. Bounded divisor enumeration, exact substitution and exact quotient identities can extract rational roots; exhausted enumeration is not a proof that none remain. Square-free multiplicities transfer to every extracted factor.

A remaining factor becomes an algebraic-root relation even when no radical form is found. Call it irreducible only with an irreducibility certificate. Having no rational roots proves irreducibility for degrees two and three, since a reducible polynomial of those degrees has a linear factor; it does not do so for degree four and above. For example (x² + 1)(x² + 2) has no rational roots but is reducible. A later bounded factor search can certify a complete irreducible factorization, with independently checked field-specific irreducibility evidence, or retain a plainly unclassified remainder. Certified irreducible remainders and their multiplicities become exact algebraic-root requests; unclassified remainders use the same root-relation representation without an irreducibility claim. Exact real isolation is a separately admitted extension, while complex implicit root-set coverage can use splitting and product evidence (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-exact-algebra`).

Next execute rational equations using those polynomial capabilities and (`constr:nrz:cas-solving-regions`). Clear denominators only under their original guards, solve the resulting numerator relations, and intersect the solution families with the original domain. For parameter-dependent denominators, cover their exceptional parameter strata as well. If a resulting root's denominator cannot be decided in its extension, use the tower zero test or component split; otherwise retain a conditional membership result. Do not return complete coverage after silently dropping an unresolved exclusion test. The quotient example above is the minimal acceptance case, including its canceled hole.

Then expose exact finite linear systems over Q and the admitted exact extensions. The cubic needs a small generic linear-recovery producer earlier; this slice supplies the complete public system contract. A checked reversible elimination transforms A x = b into a rank-revealing system. Nonzero pivots and the full transformed zero rows prove rank; a row 0 = c with c ≠ 0 proves inconsistency. Otherwise construct one particular solution and a basis of the nullspace, with a parameter map and a recovery proof for every solution. Arbitrary unused coordinates are explicit free parameters. Symbolic pivots require zero/nonzero regions; solving a generic rank stratum does not cover rank drops.

For an independent family example, x + 2y = 3 and 2x + 4y = 6 reduce by subtracting twice the first row from the second to the first equation alone. The nonzero coefficient of x and the vanishing second row prove rank one. The complete family is (x,y) = (3 − 2t,t) for every t in the requested scalar field: substitution proves membership, and every solution is recovered by t = y. Replacing the second right side by 7 produces 0 = 1 and therefore an empty solution set. These exact calculations certify both rank and consistency; the number of equations alone would not.

Finally add local inverse and implicit-branch requests. For a C1 real system F(t,y) = 0 on an open neighborhood of (t_0,y_0), prove F(t_0,y_0) = 0 and that the square Jacobian with respect to y is invertible. The implicit function theorem gives neighborhoods and a unique local y = φ(t); differentiating the identity gives Dφ = −(D_y F)^(−1)D_t F. An explicit requested neighborhood needs evidence that it satisfies the theorem's local contract; an existence theorem for some neighborhood does not certify an arbitrary supplied region. Holomorphic complex inversion uses its own hypotheses. At a repeated root the relevant derivative can vanish, so this ordinary theorem is unavailable. Local uniqueness never proves there are no remote branches (`credit:nrz:cas-solving-analysis`).

**Rule (Boundary to certified numerical solves)** · `rule:nrz:cas-solving-matrix-boundary`

These are exact mathematical identities, rank statements and symbolic families. They do not replace nrz-matrix's certified numerical solves, conditioning evidence, floating algorithms or enclosure contracts. A symbolic invertibility theorem may be an input fact for a numerical client; it is not evidence about a particular rounded factorization or a numerical error bound. Likewise a numerical root enclosure cannot become a CAS algebraic identity without a separately admitted exact certificate. The CAS retains mathematical shapes and coordinate order, while physical meanings and numerical assurance stay with their owners (`rule:vision:solves-and-inverses`), (`rule:vision:layer-enforcement`).
