# LC input streams and standing output questions · `spec:nrz:lc-streams`

**Principle (One indexed evaluation over finite and open axes)** · `prin:nrz:lc-stream-contract`

A stream specializes the two graphs joined by bindings in (`prin:nrz:lc-grid-contract`), (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-two-graphs`), (`spec:nrz:descriptor-heap-computation`). Common indexed domains, cached exact authority, tiled input/output bindings, LC projections, decision barriers, masks and graph costs are defined in (`spec:nrz:lc-grid-tiles`), especially (`def:nrz:lc-grid-indexed-domain`), (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-mask-ownership`), (`def:nrz:lc-grid-barrier`), (`def:nrz:lc-grid-cost`). This document adds the open axis, host arrivals, standing questions and source refinement, and owns the host loan, publication, flush and bounded-retirement mechanisms for streams and finite grids. nrz owns tile memory, size, layout, masks and lifetime; the host controls data origin and flow. Readings are heap values, and decision barriers are their sole path to new semantic descriptor facts. All behavior below is a required contract. The implementation standing of standing answers, decisions and lifecycle is recorded in (`claim:nrz:lc-closing-standing-streams`), (`claim:nrz:lc-closing-stream-lifecycle`); only (`sec:nrz:lc-closing-register`) decides LC completion.

## Open axis and arrivals · `sec:nrz:lc-stream-axis-contract`

**Definition (Open axis, epoch and clock relation)** · `def:nrz:lc-stream-axis`

A stream axis is an exact descriptor of an ordered, append-only index domain with no declared final extent. Supplied associations connect heap cells to original indices; prior published associations are immutable. Indexed input symbols, an epoch node and the declared clock relation connect each sample's reported time and physical time to the original LC relation. An index denotes order and sample association, not a float timestamp or inferred physical count. The clock relation retains per-sample timestamp support and the one shared constant offset under (`rule:nrz:lc-relation-two-layer-measurements`). Appending adds masked tile-region versions through (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-array-binding`), not a new relation or descriptor per reading. Data-source ordering, lateness and corrections are the host's concern under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-host-flow`); the axis does not create a reorder frontier. A standing output can use this axis without a numerical input at every possible index.

**Definition (Stream tile and original index association)** · `def:nrz:lc-stream-segment`

A stream tile is bounded nrz-owned heap storage with its layout, matching one-bit-per-cell mask and indexed binding under (`def:nrz:lc-grid-indexed-domain`), (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-mask-ownership`). The mask identifies filled cells within the allocated capacity, including noncontiguous cells. Reserved memory does not assert that a sample exists. Original axis/source associations survive handback, branch masks and any sorting into new layouts; numerical buffer position is not source identity. Masks and scoped evidence account for actual outcomes without a separate filled-prefix, suffix-obligation or selection structure. A finite grid tile uses the same mechanism with a finite parent axis under (`def:nrz:lc-grid-tile`).

**Rule (The host controls input flow)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-host-flow`

The host decides when to request a tile, fill its permitted cells and companions, hand it back, flush or skip readings. It supplies the original index/source associations required by the question. Data origin, acquisition order, late data and corrections are host concerns; late or corrected data enters as new host input with the meanings the host supplies. nrz preserves those meanings and immutable published versions through ordinary binding and decision dependencies, rather than maintaining a correction model or reordering frontier. It does not infer an observation from an unfilled cell or interpret repeated bytes as an independent source. There is no nrz-side backpressure or loss policy. nrz reports actual capacity and operation outcomes within its bounds; the host chooses its next flow action. Mathematical questions over data that is absent retain their declared scope and are handled by the relevant decision node, including under flush (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-flush`).

**Rule (Time support and the clock-drift boundary)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-clock-boundary`

The epoch and clock relation remain attached to every tile and window. A constant offset is not a clock-rate parameter. Per-sample time intervals can enclose drift only over their declared observation window; they neither infer a drift law nor authorize extrapolation under (`claim:nrz:lc-closing-excludes-clock-drift`). An open axis does not make finite physical-time evidence, a cycle cover or an error goal valid forever. A decision barrier checks the arrived time data against the actual question, reuses applicable cached strata and establishes missing domain or original-epoch transport evidence with charged work. Unavailable evidence leaves a scoped undecided or refusal outcome under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-stop`); a cache miss is not an out-of-range sensor event. Storage or local-index rollover does not reset physical time, uncertainty or the shared source, and unaffected relation, source, pi and root authority remains reusable under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-shared-nodes`).

## Host-filled tiles and arrived-value decisions · `sec:nrz:lc-stream-preparation`

**Rule (Decisions operate on arrived heap values)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-arrived-decisions`

There is no declared sensor input range and no support certification required before a loan or reading. Readings, deterministic bounds and numerical error companions are heap values with masks, not descriptors. A decision barrier over the arrived data decides whether and how the reading makes sense for the original LC question, retaining its source equations and joint uncertainty under (`rule:nrz:lc-relation-two-layer-measurements`); statistical moments alone do not establish bounded support (`rule:nrz:lc-relation-certified-sets`). Exact domains prepared beforehand are only cached reusable strata. A miss establishes the needed evidence at the barrier, while a stratum, cut or lemma already decided is a charged content-keyed lookup under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-decision-cache`). Mathematical invalidity, unsupported evidence and boundary uncertainty have distinct masked outcomes; there is no out-of-support outcome relative to a sensor range. Generic positive non-square inputs retain the root and projection obligations of (`req:nrz:lc-grid-irrational-cover`), (`req:nrz:lc-grid-family-kernels`). Authenticated pi and numerical prelude reuse follow (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-pi-reuse`).

**Rule (Pending input versions and exclusive host loans)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-input-loan`

nrz allocates the tile, its matching bitmask, layout and required companion regions, and binds the indexed input family before lending writable selections to the host. No support certificate is a loan prerequisite. The computational graph contains a pending input-region version with an exclusive loan edge. Only the borrower writes the lent storage and records filled cells through the loan's mask contract; nrz cannot read that storage or run consuming kernels while ownership is outstanding. The host supplies readings outside the engine. The loan carries nrz's chosen capacity, layout, source/axis correspondence and companion obligations. It is an ordinary safe exclusive borrow or an owned, non-cloneable handle: initialization and ownership transfer require neither unsafe code nor a numerical copy. nrz still owns allocation and lifetime; the host's size hint confers no layout or capacity override. A cancelled loan releases its storage only after exclusive ownership returns, and its unfilled cells assert no observation. The same mechanism serves finite grids.

**Rule (Handback, validation and input publication)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-handback`

Handback returns exclusive access to nrz. Validation authenticates the loan, generation, layout, mask-to-cell correspondence, initialization and required companion coverage. It checks only cells marked filled, never uninitialized storage, and does not test native containment against a declared sensor box. Successful handback atomically publishes the input/companion/mask bundle so consuming leaves can run; ownership return alone is not publication. Foreign, stale, multiply returned or incomplete bundles are refused before consumption. Physical and mathematical validity is decided over arrived heap values under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-arrived-decisions`); published input is not a certified LC answer. Fallible handback checks precede the atomic commit with no host callback inside it under (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-atomic-publication`). Output publication follows (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-publication`).

**Rule (Partial tiles use filled-cell masks)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-partial-tile`

The host may return any initialized subset of a tile, including an empty tile. Its matching mask identifies precisely the filled cells with complete required companions; holes and unused capacity are never read or silently treated as values. Partial handback creates no outstanding suffix obligation or required future observation. Already associated cells in other tiles keep their original indices and can proceed whenever their actual graph inputs are ready. A barrier that needs additional data waits on its own inputs; the host may later provide new input or flush the relevant subgraph. Cancellation and empty handback publish no numerical answers for unfilled cells. Masks propagate through kernels and decision continuations under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-mask-ownership`), replacing separate prefix and selection structures.

**Rule (Several tiles in flight within one capacity)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-inflight`

Distinct pending and published region versions allow the host to fill a tile while nrz evaluates another. Loan edges and kernel dependencies preserve exclusive access. nrz's capacity bound includes every lent tile, live input and result, mask, companion, refinement region, earlier tile protected by a descriptor reader, and scratch allocation. A buffer is reused only after all readers and borrowers release its prior occupation; a new generation cannot authenticate an old binding. Independent ready leaves need no stream-wide holdback for an unrelated earlier tile. If a request cannot fit the bound, nrz reports that resource outcome and the host chooses how to proceed under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-host-flow`); no engine backpressure, data-loss policy or allocation beyond the bound is introduced. Overlapping fill and evaluation changes cost but not evidence or ownership.

**Rule (Tile size trades fill latency against setup cost)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-tile-size`

nrz chooses tile size from its own capacity and numerical constraints and the host's optional latency or size hint under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-size`). For a tile with `b>0` filled readings and attributable setup cost W_setup, amortized setup Work per reading is `W_setup/b`. Decisions and cache lookups remain charged at their actual barriers, which may span several tiles or less than one; their cost is not automatically once per tile. A reused certificate's construction cost is counted once across its reuse scope, with lookup and refinement separately reported. Smaller tiles usually return sooner; larger tiles can amortize graph and kernel overhead but take longer to fill. At constant source rate `r>0`, ideal time from the first to the last of b readings is `(b-1)/r`; with no additional dependency wait, first-reading latency adds handback, computation and publication. Partial return uses actual filled population and fill time; an empty tile has no per-reading quotient. Host-filled grids share this tradeoff, with host preparation time in place of assumed sensor cadence. The hint never imposes a fixed extent, and tile size never defines barrier readiness.

## Standing answers and decisions · `sec:nrz:lc-stream-answers`

**Definition (Standing output symbol)** · `def:nrz:lc-stream-standing-question`

A standing question is an output symbol indexed over the open axis and attached to the original LC relation, supplied/query roles and explicit question domain. Lowering binds each concrete result tile with its matching mask, deterministic-bound and numerical-error companions and any required branch/sample axes before its kernels run under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-output-binding`). Asking for phase at each new reading uses (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-forward-series`); asking whether that phase is below a threshold additionally declares the predicate, boundary ownership, ordered quantifiers and retained joint physical family of (`def:nrz:lc-grid-predicate`). Shared-source aggregates obey (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-quantifier-order`) and refuse unsupported quantifier structures. A per-reading question, a fixed-window question and an aggregate over the observed domain have distinct dependency edges. An aggregate barrier may need several tiles, while a local comparison may need only part of one. Their meanings cannot be inferred from the most recent answer or from tile boundaries.

**Rule (Complete atomic publication with original associations)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-publication`

Publication makes the participating output cells, masks and required companions available together, or identifies their explicit undecided, domain or refusal outcomes. Mask coverage and its scoped evidence account for every participating original index; unfilled cells do not become observations. Atomic binding publication follows (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-atomic-publication`), and decision evidence follows (`rule:nrz:decision-selected-publication`). Consumers resolve answers by output symbol, original index association, mask and published version, never by buffer address. Original order remains recoverable after branch sorting, but publication readiness follows graph dependencies rather than a stream completion frontier. A decision result can be a mask over an older tile or a new tile/layout; its continuation's descriptors protect earlier regions still read under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-lifetime`). Refinements and invalidations name their prior versions and depend on them without overwriting their history. Failure publishes no incomplete answer bundle. Flush completion follows (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-flush`), with no separate end-of-stream or finality protocol.

**Rule (Stream decisions share cached strata)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-decision-strata`

A decision barrier consumes its own arrived input tiles and masks under (`def:nrz:lc-grid-barrier`), then produces scoped branch/outcome masks through (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-cell-decisions`), (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-region-decisions`). Already decided strata, cuts and lemmas are content-keyed charged cache hits; new semantic evidence and attachment work is charged at that barrier under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-fast-path`), (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-decision-cache`). A matching cached theorem does not authenticate unseen inputs or altered source versions. Arbitrary stream values sort into branch tiles with preserved original associations, including alternating settled interiors. Each dependent subgraph is tiled, and the node may choose a mask-only result or a new tile/layout. It may also choose a bounded tiled refinement subgraph; more data creates neither an unlimited retry allowance nor a promise that uncertainty will disappear. Open obligations and typed reasons remain on undecided masks. Original identities, versions, branches and attempts resolve through (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-outcome-view`). A simple node can perform one cached comparison and emit one mask; a complex node can contain many kernel and barrier results. Its input, result and flush behavior belong to that node's design.

**Rule (Decision conclusions retain their quantified scope)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-decision-scope`

A decision conclusion names the actual index domain, immutable assumption snapshot and ordered joint physical quantifiers it proves, including when the node runs under flush. Coverage over observed readings proves nothing about arbitrary future host input. For a universal index claim, a feasible counterexample refutes it on extensions retaining that counterexample; for an existential index claim, a feasible witness proves it on extensions retaining that witness. Inner obligations must be discharged in their declared scope under (`def:nrz:lc-grid-predicate`), (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-quantifier-order`); separate per-index witnesses do not establish one common source realization. A witness or counterexample can disappear when the joint feasible set narrows, so transporting such a conclusion requires persistence evidence. Universal coverage and complete exclusion transport through checked inclusion with their nonemptiness/domain premises; an empty physical family has an explicit impossibility outcome. A fixed-window assertion keeps its window version. These are mathematical scope rules, not a separate finality state machine. Flush allows the node to decide over data it actually has; it cannot silently replace the requested quantifiers, invent absent data or assert a whole-future result without proof.

## Shared sources and refinement · `sec:nrz:lc-stream-sources`

**Definition (One systematic source over the stream)** · `def:nrz:lc-stream-shared-source`

A shared systematic error is one source node for the whole stream, with immutable versions of its support and joint constraints. Per-sample uncertainty remains on the indexed input family; segments do not create independent copies of the shared source. Conditional fibres, clock/phase offsets and calibration gains retain the original equations and source-map edges under (`def:nrz:descriptor-binding-source-symbols`), (`rule:nrz:lc-relation-two-layer-measurements`). New observations may constrain this same source. Their arrival alone does not replace its declared support, and marginal bounds cannot prove joint feasibility. A physical recalibration or changed error law requires an explicitly related new assumption domain, not an unlabelled mutation of the old source.

**Rule (Monotone information is published by refinement edges)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-refinement`

A source refinement admits a new joint feasible domain with checked inclusion in the old domain. If proposed from numerical observations, the decision chooses a refinement subgraph whose barriers validate the corresponding evidence before a later descriptor snapshot is lowered. For the same output selection, publish a new answer version with refinement edges to the old version and the inclusion proof, satisfying (`req:nrz:lc-relation-refinement-monotonicity`): the new covering set is contained in the old, branch selections map to old covered branches, and proved information is retained. Intersection with prior coverage may be needed; it must retain the same-target and joint-domain evidence. A nonempty refinement cannot move a certified horizon earlier; emptiness has no physical horizon. Exhausted refinement leaves earlier coverage valid and an explicit unresolved obligation. The old publication is immutable and remains a valid, possibly wider historical enclosure. Refinement work and cache hits are charged at that subgraph's barriers, with no exact work in the intervening kernels. A tighter source version may refine earlier and future answers; recomputation walks affected graph dependencies within the node's bounds. This is a decision choice, not a separate engine refinement policy.

**Rule (Refinement, stale attachments and changed assumptions)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-invalidation`

Appending disjoint inputs leaves independent historical answers untouched. Compatible narrowing preserves their old coverage as information under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-refinement`), but live bindings or decision attachments tied to a replaced source version must still be revalidated under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-lifetime`); information preservation is not live-version authentication. New refined versions cite the prior answer and inclusion evidence. A dependent reading changed by new host input without inclusion evidence, widened physical assumptions, a changed epoch/map/source law or lost version authenticity invalidates the transitive live dependency closure under (`rule:nrz:decision-invalidation`); a new question/version cites an invalidation or supersession edge, not a monotone-refinement edge. Unaffected disjoint selections survive only with proven unchanged dependencies; otherwise conservative whole-family invalidation applies. Consumers see whether old coverage remains valid under a refinement or is unavailable for the current assumptions after invalidation, and can still resolve its historical theorem under (`rule:nrz:decision-attachment-lifetime`). No event overwrites published values or turns an old theorem into evidence for a new snapshot.

## Bounded residence and retained evidence · `sec:nrz:lc-stream-resources`

**Rule (Declared bounds on both live graphs)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-bounded-resources`

nrz bounds live heap bytes, masks, resident descriptor nodes/bytes, referenced proof closures, caches, pending loans, in-flight tiles and refinement subgraphs independently of stream duration. Bounds include index representation, proof bit size, output-family extent, scratch and ready-walk storage. Descriptor-held earlier tiles remain live and count toward capacity; no graph or cache reference may hide their retention. Compatible tiles share relation, source and cached authority, and unused occupations retire under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-retirement`). Exact growth and charged reuse are attributed at barriers; kernels between them satisfy (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-fast-path`). Historical registries, schema capacity and provenance owners are included, not just current numerical buffers. Cumulative construction and exported storage can grow with a run even though resident execution is bounded. Indefinitely retaining arbitrary historical answers requires external storage whose capacity and evidence guarantees are explicit. An operation that cannot meet its resource/evidence contract reports that limitation before publishing an unsupported result; it does not allocate beyond its bounds or impose an input-flow policy. The host controls subsequent requests and flush under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-host-flow`). The finite-grid application is (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-streaming`).

**Rule (Retirement preserves resolvable evidence paths)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-retirement`

A tile remains protected from collection while any live descriptor dependency, kernel reader or exclusive borrower needs its values, masks or companions, including across decision barriers. Once those uses end, nrz may retire the heap occupation. Before retiring required historical evidence, retain or export the immutable path from output symbols and published versions through original inputs/assumptions, source versions, enclosure/cut evidence and certificate authority. Exported references must resolve to actual scoped evidence; an unavailable premise behind a digest is insufficient. Historical proof resolution never authenticates a reused buffer. The retention contract states whether old numerical enclosures or only their evidence remain retrievable. A node refining retired answers needs bounded restoration of retained inputs/evidence or a declared refinement scope; missing history makes recomputation unavailable without invalidating a valid historical enclosure. Whole-history inference requires a proved sufficient summary or resolvable original dependencies. Every restoration is capacity-checked. Local-index rollover preserves axis/epoch correspondence, with exact transport and growing endpoint bit size charged at barriers. Reusable cache authority is detached from retired live occupations so full flush can empty the heap.

**Definition (Window as a subdomain, not a new source)** · `def:nrz:lc-stream-window`

A window is a declared subdomain of the open axis, selected by an index interval or a clock-domain predicate with checked inclusion and boundary ownership. Moving it creates a new domain version and releases only dependencies whose scope permits release. It does not reset the systematic source or erase whole-history constraints; dropping constraints changes that question unless a checked sufficient summary preserves it, and widening is not monotone refinement. Uncertain timestamps retain their boundary uncertainty. The decision node defines which window inputs it needs and how a flush affects an incomplete set; its conclusion keeps the actual domain and quantifiers under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-decision-scope`). There is no window-completion frontier. Mathematical inference scope and retained history remain distinct.

**Rule (Flush drains a graph scope and preserves warm caches)** · `rule:nrz:lc-stream-flush`

The verb `flush` announces to barriers in the targeted graph that no more work is expected. A notified barrier unblocks once it becomes a runnable leaf under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-ready-leaves`), then decides over the data it actually has. Notification alone does not supply unavailable upstream results, reclaim an outstanding host loan or bypass a live dependency. Flush may change a decision node's behavior, including how absent inputs affect its result; that behavior belongs to the node's design and must preserve (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-decision-scope`). The ready-leaf walk continues through its dependants, including any chosen refinement subgraph, until scoped work and required result/evidence delivery are complete and heap readers release their occupations. When a full flush is finished, the heap is empty, including input, output, mask, companion, scratch and numerical prelude occupations; reusable caches stay warm. A cache hit may then require a charged fresh numerical import without re-proving its exact authority. Outstanding ownership or evidence-delivery obligations keep flush unfinished and their wait/stop visible. A partial flush targets a subgraph, never a stream or input as such; it drains that scope while respecting dependencies shared with work outside it and does not claim that the whole heap is empty. Full flush is the drain operation, not a declaration that the host can never provide new input. It replaces separate end-of-stream and finality machinery without adding nrz-side backpressure or loss policy.

## Cost and acceptance evidence · `sec:nrz:lc-stream-acceptance`

**Definition (Exact growth and native work on an observed domain)** · `def:nrz:lc-stream-cost`

Attribute exact construction, CAS calls and Work to optional cache preparation and actual decision/refinement barriers, including failed attempts, warm lookups and authentication that adds no node. **Definition (Arrival-to-answer latency is its own measurement)** · `def:nrz:lc-stream-latency`

For each reading, latency runs from host-observed arrival to visibility of its complete answer or explicit outcome. Components are remaining host fill time, handback validation, graph dependency waits, kernel time, decision lookup/proof and any chosen refinement, publication and consumer delivery. Report host pacing separately from engine work. A barrier waiting for several tiles can outlast one tile's fill, while a barrier needing less than a tile has its own readiness condition. A monotonic host timing source measures elapsed intervals independently of uncertain physical timestamps. Throughput counts published readings per elapsed time and does not replace latency. Report first valid answer, requested refinement and flush completion separately, including unresolved waits and stopped outcomes. No fill deadline alone guarantees completion while required inputs or ownership remain outstanding.

## Decided positions · `sec:nrz:lc-stream-decisions`

**Decision (Host input and nrz-owned tiles)** · `dec:nrz:lc-stream-owned-inputs`

The host controls when to request, fill, return, flush or skip input and supplies late data or corrections as new input. nrz owns tile memory, size, layout, masks and lifetime, interpreting a host size preference as a hint. Filled, branch, refused and undecided states travel as kernel-owned heap masks; actual readings meet semantic authority at decision barriers, with no declared sensor range or pre-arrival support gate (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-host-flow`), (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-arrived-decisions`), (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-mask-ownership`). This keeps data origin with the host and bounded residence with nrz.

**Decision (Cached barriers, tiled continuations and flush)** · `dec:nrz:lc-stream-barrier-flow`

The graph walk runs leaves whose inputs are available. Barriers hold only their dependants, cache decided content with charged lookups and choose their own tiled refinement or continuation, retaining older tiles through descriptors when needed. Flush announces no more expected work in its graph scope and lets runnable barriers decide over actual data; full completion empties the heap and retains warm caches (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-decision-strata`), (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-flush`). Partial flush targets a subgraph. This requires no scheduler, stream-finality machinery or nrz backpressure. Mask memory, the ready-leaf walk and each node's flush behavior must satisfy (`req:nrz:lc-grid-mask-design`), (`req:nrz:lc-grid-walk-design`); decided behavior does not establish delivery under (`sec:nrz:lc-closing-register`).
