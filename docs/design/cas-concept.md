# A graph and calculus concept for computer algebra · `spec:nrz:cas-concept`

The proposed nrz-cas package replaces the symbolic core in a later migration. Its primary representation is one graph over petgraph, its organizing language is calculus, and its results carry paths of conditional mathematical identities. This concept does not change nrz-algebra or any consumer. The existing units client retains physical interpretation and checks original operations before normalization (`req:vision:expression-and-symbol-contract`), (`rule:vision:check-before-normalization`), (`rule:vision:layer-enforcement`).

The proposed crate is `crates/cas`, with package owner CAS and label area cas when it is implemented. This document and the subsequent design belong to owner NRZ, area nrz. The concept establishes mathematical and computational meanings; the design chooses concrete graph types, APIs, number representations and an executable implementation slice. No crate, dependency or consumer migration is introduced by this document. A named operation can be representable without a reduction algorithm, and a symbolic derivation can exist without numerical evaluation. These three levels remain separate throughout.

The calculus premise is constructive: first ask what differential, jet, antiderivative, discrete difference or local inverse exposes the desired relation, then turn that relation into a checked identity. A derivative is not automatically equal to its operand, and a numerical approximation is not an identity. The graph stores the hypotheses and relation kind that make each construction meaningful. Exceptions to the calculus frame need a reason about information or domains, rather than merely the historical classification of an algorithm as algebra.

## Graph and evidence · `sec:nrz:cas-concept-graph`

**Definition (Graph model)** · `def:nrz:cas-graph-model`

One heterogeneous directed graph contains original source occurrences, shared mathematical expressions, binder scopes, assumptions, goals and instantiated identities. Operand-position, occurrence, provenance, premise and conclusion edges have distinct meanings. A mathematical expression shares its syntax without merging its source occurrences. Original operand order and source locations remain available even when an identity leads to a smaller derived expression.

An expression vertex denotes an operation in a mathematical sort and scope; operand edges carry ordered positions. Scalar multiplication and matrix composition are different operations, so scalar commutativity cannot reorder matrix factors. Mathematical extents, index ranges and scalar fields belong to this vocabulary; physical axes, dimensions, measures, kinds, coordinate origins and measurement provenance do not. Symbols are declared identities with exact spellings, and named mathematical functions have explicit signatures and any admitted derivative rules. Unknown meanings remain unknown. The expression-operand subgraph is acyclic; the graph of relations may cycle. Syntax sharing is not equality saturation: no unconditional equivalence class is formed by merging expressions that agree only under different conditions.

Original occurrences are graph vertices with source identities and half-open byte spans, ordered operand occurrences, and a link to their mathematical expression. Source text is retained or immutably identified so the span remains interpretable. Two occurrences of x+x can share one mathematical expression while retaining two sets of source locations. Generated expressions record derivation provenance and never invent a source span. A binder explicitly records its variable scope, body, limits and bound-variable occurrences; substitution must avoid capture. Renaming a bound variable is a scoped relation, not replacement of every equally spelled free symbol. A source occurrence tree can be a subgraph without becoming the core's separate authoritative data model.

One bounded traversal covers every original syntactic operand before any rewrite of that source root is enabled, including canceled terms, arguments beneath zero and branch alternatives. A sum's bound body is visited as original syntax; this does not mean enumerating an unbounded number of instantiations. Its index-dependent obligations remain quantified or require a separately bounded expansion. The units client performs its own physical validation over these occurrences, preserving occurrence paths and declaration snapshots. Sharing never permits that client to skip evidence for an occurrence. Normal forms and traversal receipts are not physical proofs. A substituted input receives new occurrence/provenance evidence and requires renewed client validation (`rule:vision:check-before-normalization`), (`rule:nrz:plan-validation`).

**Definition (Mathematical contexts and requests)** · `def:nrz:cas-contexts-and-requests`

A context names the coefficient field, free and bound variables, real or complex domains, branch conventions and explicit assumptions. Canonical context identity is part of every goal, identity application and cache key. The same printed expression in two contexts need not have the same meaning. Exact rational literals are the default; symbols such as pi and unevaluated roots have exact symbolic meanings, not floating substitutes. General powers retain an expression-valued exponent and their domain convention. Identities for positive real powers, integer powers and principal complex powers are registered separately; x to the zeroth power does not silently choose a convention for zero to the zeroth power.

A request has an operation, operands, context, target relation or output form, goal ranking and explicit budgets. Derivative, integral, sum, limit, series, solve and spectral requests are first-class graph objects, including unresolved ones. A solve returns a predicate or set relation over a declared variable/domain, rather than pretending that an equation is a scalar expression. A series congruence, an equality of functions, a derivative relation and a first-order approximation are distinct judgments. Only transitive relations of compatible kind and context can compose into a path claim. A theorem converting one kind of judgment into another must supply an explicit checked step.

**Rule (Conditional witnesses)** · `rule:nrz:cas-conditional-witnesses`

A proof walk takes only checked identity applications between their conclusion endpoints. Syntax, provenance and premise edges do not establish equality. Every step records its rule, substitutions, mathematical context, definedness and branch conditions, and the evidence or explicit hypotheses on which those conditions depend. A bounded checker can replay the witness independently of the search that found it. Equality cycles are allowed; a proof cannot justify itself through a cycle of dependencies.

An instantiated identity is a vertex encoding a conditional inference, with its left and right conclusion ports, premise references, binder-safe substitution and proof justification. It represents a multi-premise inference within the one ordinary graph. Traversing one premise does not establish the other premises. A walk from one conclusion port through this vertex to the other is admissible only in a direction justified by its relation and with every premise accounted for. Replacing a subexpression also records the containing expression and a congruence justification; composition of partial functions may add definedness requirements. Structural interning, provenance and operand edges never supply such a step. These distinctions specialize published substitution and rewriting principles to this graph model (`credit:nrz:cas-rewriting-literature`).

For an equality step the claim is: under context Gamma and obligations P, both endpoint expressions are defined and have equal values in the specified domain. A path composes these claims and retains all needed obligations, with capture-safe binding for quantified ones. Proven obligations link to their proofs; assumptions link to their declarations. A remaining obligation is displayed in a conditional result, not silently added to Gamma and reported as discharged. Refuted obligations block that route. If context consistency is unknown, the result remains a conditional theorem, not a claim that its domain is nonempty. Contexts with different assumptions or branch choices are not interchangeable search states.

Thus x/x can reach one under x nonzero, while the original quotient and the restriction remain in the witness. Over real x, sqrt(x²) reaches abs(x); reaching x additionally requires x nonnegative. Replacing a partial expression by zero after multiplication must preserve its definedness, and must first visit the expression's original operands. Formal collection equality in the existing core remains a common-defined-domain relation, not equality of definedness sets and not permission to change floating evaluation (`rule:vision:algebra-domain-boundary`), (`cav:vision:unit-checking-scope`).

The witness names the graph-independent mathematical endpoints, source links where applicable, context, rule identifiers and versions, ordered steps, substitutions, positions and the DAG of supporting proofs. A checker validates scope, sorts, premises, intermediate expressions and conclusion composition under its own bounds. It neither repeats heuristic search nor accepts the searcher's cost estimate as mathematical evidence. Primitive mathematical rules form an explicit trusted vocabulary; a derived shortcut must expand to previously checked rules and hypotheses, with acyclic dependency order. External declarations remain hypotheses unless supported by such a proof. Successful replay proves that conditional mathematical statement, not its physical relevance, completeness, numerical accuracy or global optimality.

## Search and cost · `sec:nrz:cas-concept-search`

**Decision (Path cost and goal ranking)** · `dec:nrz:cas-path-cost`

Each identity application has a strictly positive integer cost, and path cost is the sum of its logical steps rather than raw graph edges. A learned identity is retained as a shortcut when it lowers the best known cost between the same endpoints under the same context. Its original proof remains available and consumes proof-storage and checking budgets. Search cost and the task's ranking of resulting expressions are separate: expansion can make an intermediate expression larger while opening a route to a better goal.

Concretely, a versioned policy assigns each logical transition s a weight w(s) in the positive integers, and C(P) is the sum of w(s) along P. The policy can charge different rule families differently; weights measure the declared search preference, not elapsed seconds, floating accuracy or the literal number of petgraph edges. Cost arithmetic is checked for overflow. A learned rule receives a fixed positive weight when admitted, and is compared with an incumbent route that does not already use it or depend on it. The context comparison includes residual obligations: a new assumption cannot masquerade as a cheaper proof of the original claim. A checked identity connecting previously unconnected endpoints creates a finite route where the incumbent cost was infinite. Storing a lemma once can reduce future search decisions, but does not make its proof or its replay free.

Goal ranking R is separately declared. A simplification request can rank the number of unresolved requested operations, expression size and depth in a specified lexicographic order; a polynomial-expansion request instead prioritizes reaching a coefficient representation in its declared variables. A factorization request names the coefficient field and requested factor properties. A solve request ranks progress toward an explicit solution predicate with its exceptional branches retained. Canonical expression order breaks equal ranks. These are task policies rather than a universal simplicity theorem. An unconditional result and a conditional result are not interchangeable candidates merely because the latter is smaller; ranking within a hypothesis class cannot erase obligations.

Search uses two ordered kinds of work: the lowest accumulated path cost and a task-directed estimate of progress toward R. A deterministic finite schedule reserves work for both, with explicit positive quotas per epoch, so a promising-looking expansion cannot consume the whole run. Heuristic estimates need not be lower bounds and do not prove optimality. The design will choose concrete quotas and frontier data structures; the conceptual contract already requires that their values, all rule orientations and every tie-break be part of the replayable policy. Generation occurs in bounded deterministic batches. Shortest-path claims use a frozen finite snapshot of checked transitions; adding a shortcut can reopen an earlier state, so a node is not permanently settled across graph growth. Positive-weight shortest paths on such a frozen snapshot use the standard shortest-path argument (`credit:nrz:cas-dijkstra`).

Expansion is an intended operation and an exploration resource. Local increases in R or expression size are allowed up to a declared peak-size limit and a count of exploratory expansion steps. Rules suggested by the request's differential structure, variable set or target form receive task priority, rather than expanding every product indiscriminately. An expansion and its inverse cannot cycle for free: identical expression/context/obligation states retain the least known cost, equal-cost ties follow a total order, and returning to the same state with a greater cost does not reopen it. Distinct ever-larger expressions can still proliferate; frontier, match, graph-size and expansion quotas bound them. Reaching those limits returns an incomplete result rather than silently pruning work and claiming completeness. These controls guide the walk; they cannot guarantee that an allowed expansion will be useful.

**Example (Expansion opens a derivative route)** · `ex:nrz:cas-expansion-route`

For real x and nonzero real h, the difference quotient ((x+h)²-x²)/h can expand to (x²+2xh+h²-x²)/h, collect to (2xh+h²)/h, and cancel to 2x+h. The expanded numerator is locally larger. A derivative goal makes it useful because h is now exposed and the limit as h tends to zero is 2x by continuity. The equalities retain h nonzero; a separate punctured-domain limit theorem justifies the derivative and does not assert equality of the quotient with 2x at h=0. Direct multiplication verifies the expansion and cancellation. The limit argument uses the definition of the derivative and continuity (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`). A cached route between the quotient and 2x+h can be one cheaper logical step under the same restriction while retaining the three-step proof.

**Rule (Bounds and deterministic outcomes)** · `rule:nrz:cas-search-bounds`

Every request supplies finite limits for source bytes, original occurrences, operand depth, expression and identity vertices, graph edges, pending matches, frontier states, generated expansions, arithmetic size/work, condition-proof work, witness bytes and output bytes. Every repeated action consumes a finite counter, including a failed match, rule instantiation, substitution, comparison of large exact objects and premise attempt. A single large integer calculation, binder expansion or proof replay must be internally bounded; an outer queue counter alone is insufficient. Checked construction refuses index and counter exhaustion without wraparound. The design must make allocation and rollback boundaries explicit so an interrupted construction cannot publish malformed graph state. Safe Rust, typed failures and the unchanged workspace lint posture apply.

Termination follows from finite generation/work counters and bounded primitive operations, not from an assertion that all rewrite rules decrease a term order. Proof-checking dependencies are acyclic and consume their own limits. Parsing, original traversal, formatting and source/proof export are bounded too; sharing in memory is not permission to emit an exponentially expanded tree. No mathematical result depends on a wall-clock timeout. External cancellation may stop a run, but cannot supply its canonical mathematical conclusion. The numeric limits and exhaustion classification are part of the request/output contract, rather than secret implementation constants.

Determinism requires the same ordered sources and declarations, mathematical context, rule versions, goal and cost policy, and budgets. Canonical structural keys, scoped binders and collision-resolving equality define interning; source identity does not collapse into term identity. Logical identifiers are assigned by deterministic admission order and never derived from addresses, clocks, random seeds or hash-table iteration. Rule matches, premise requests, graph insertions, diagnostics and proof serialization all have stable total orders. Petgraph supplies storage and graph operations, not an externally visible neighbor-order guarantee. Sorting and explicit ordered work queues must supply that guarantee. Equal input under this pinned semantic contract therefore has the same admitted graph, path, witness and serialized bytes; changing the policy or rules changes the input.

The result distinguishes a replayed witness, an explicitly conditional witness, a well-formed unresolved request, an unsupported operation/domain, an invalid input and resource exhaustion. An exhausted search may retain a best verified witness, its conditions and cost, alongside the exhaustion reason and counters. Exhaustion is not inequality, nonexistence of an antiderivative or absence of solutions. An optimality statement applies only after complete shortest-path exploration of a named finite frozen graph, and only for its cost policy. A best-ranked expression is only best among the admitted compared candidates. No graph-search mechanism makes the ungenerated mathematical universe finite or complete.

## Calculus and its boundaries · `sec:nrz:cas-concept-calculus`

**Caveat (Calculus requires its hypotheses)** · `cav:nrz:cas-calculus-boundaries`

Every operation begins with a calculus route. Polynomial multiplicities arise from vanishing derivatives, repeated factors from the common divisor of a polynomial and its derivative, rational integration from derivative identities, and finite sums from discrete antidifferences. A failed route needs a mathematical reason before the concept adopts another frame. Derivative equality alone leaves a constant undetermined on each connected domain component; anchoring that constant is part of an equality witness.

**Construction (Differentiation and local models)** · `constr:nrz:cas-differential-models`

A derivative request starts from its original expression and a declared differentiation variable or direction. Constant, linearity, product, quotient and chain identities transform that request, sharing repeated subexpressions and repeated derivative requests. The derivative of a quotient requires a nonzero denominator on the differentiation domain; differentiation of an unknown function remains an explicit derivative application unless its regularity and derivative rule are supplied. A variable-dependent power can be differentiated through exp(b log(a)) on positive real a, with the chosen real logarithm, giving D(a^b)=a^b(Db log(a)+b Da/a). That route does not authorize the same formula across zeros or complex branch cuts. The checker verifies each identity and its hypotheses; a shared computational representation alone does not establish differentiability (`credit:nrz:cas-differentiation-literature`), (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`).

The local model of a differentiable map at a is f(a+h)=f(a)+J(a)h+r(h), with r(h)/norm(h) tending to zero. The remainder clause is part of the claim, rather than an omitted term licensed by small h. For scalar C^(n+1) functions on a real interval containing a and x, Taylor's integral form is f(x)=sum from k=0 to n of f^(k)(a)(x-a)^k/k! plus the integral from a to x of (x-t)^n f^(n+1)(t)/n!. This identity identifies the operating point, regularity, interval and remainder; it does not evaluate a numerical error bound. Formal power series instead use algebraic coefficient rules or congruence modulo a stated power, without an analytic convergence claim (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`).

**Construction (Equality through derivatives and anchors)** · `constr:nrz:cas-derivative-equality`

For differentiable real functions f and g on an interval I, a proof that D(f-g)=0 throughout I and an anchor f(a)=g(a) at some a in I establish f=g on I. For any two points, the mean value theorem on the segment between them makes their difference zero; the anchor fixes the remaining constant. A symbolic derivative premise must hold on the domain, not only at sampled points. Disconnected domains need an anchor on each component, and different branches need separate contexts. The multivariable analogue follows by integrating the differential along admissible paths in a connected open domain; local equality on an isolated neighborhood does not automatically cover another component (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`).

For polynomials over a characteristic-zero field, Taylor expansion terminates. If the degree of f-g is at most d, equality of derivatives of orders zero through d at a point a in that field proves f=g, since those derivatives divided by factorials are exactly its coefficients in powers of x-a. At a=0, ordinary coefficient comparison is a compressed exact jet comparison. This frames simplify, expand and polynomial equality as changes of coefficient representation with finite derivative evidence; a practical checker need not construct d redundant derivative expressions to check the same coefficient identities. Multivariate polynomials use their finitely supported multi-index jets. Rational identities can reduce to polynomial numerator equality after clearing denominators, with the original nonzero conditions retained. For general functions, finite equal jets are insufficient: zero and x^(d+1) agree through order d at zero but differ elsewhere. Thus finite-series agreement never silently becomes function equality.

**Construction (Factorization from derivative structure)** · `constr:nrz:cas-derivative-factorization`

Let f be a nonzero nonconstant polynomial over a characteristic-zero field. At a root a in an extension field, multiplicity m means f=(x-a)^m u with u(a) nonzero. Differentiation gives f'=(x-a)^(m-1)(m u+(x-a)u'), whose remaining factor is nonzero at a. Consequently the monic gcd of f and f' contains exactly m-1 copies of each root factor; dividing f by it leaves a square-free polynomial up to a nonzero scalar. Repeated gcd/quotient steps recover multiplicity layers. Equivalently, multiplicity m is the first nonzero derivative order: f(a) through f^(m-1)(a) vanish and f^(m)(a) does not. The finite Taylor identity proves this characterization. These are the calculus route into square-free factorization, not an appeal to a separate factoring oracle (`credit:nrz:cas-polynomial-literature`).

For f=(x-1)^3(x+2)^2, differentiation yields f'=(x-1)^2(x+2)(5x+4), and the monic common divisor is g=(x-1)^2(x+2). The quotient f/g=(x-1)(x+2) removes the repeated copies. A factorization walk records these derivative relations as premises, then an identity between the original polynomial and its factor product, checked by exact multiplication. It never steps from f to f' as though they were equal. A gcd witness can include divisibility identities for both inputs and a Bezout identity Af+Bf'=g, making the common-divisor claim independently checkable. Monic normalization and the field are explicit. The zero polynomial and constants have separate cases; multiplicity for the zero polynomial is not inferred from a first nonzero derivative that never occurs.

The derivative route detects multiplicity but does not determine all coefficient-field splitting. The polynomial x²+1 is square-free over the rationals and also over the complex numbers, while its irreducible factorization differs. Its derivative alone cannot decide that field question. Completing irreducible factorization therefore deliberately admits exact coefficient arithmetic and field-specific methods beyond the differential invariant, justified by the missing information. Positive characteristic is also a separate domain: a nonconstant pth power can have zero derivative in characteristic p, invalidating the multiplicity argument above. The default rational characteristic-zero model does not silently generalize the theorem. Exact field arithmetic is mathematical infrastructure for calculus, not permission to import an existing CAS layout or factorization implementation.

**Construction (Integration and Hermite reduction)** · `constr:nrz:cas-hermite-integration`

Integration searches for F with a checked derivative relation DF=f under the requested domain, then attaches an arbitrary constant on each connected real interval of definition. A definite integral also needs integrability, endpoints and a valid fundamental-theorem step. Change of variables carries differentiability, transformed endpoints and the relevant domain/orientation hypotheses. Matching a familiar-looking integrand without these conditions supplies no proof (`credit:nrz:cas-integration-literature`), (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`).

For a rational integrand A/B over a characteristic-zero field, repeated factors in B are exposed by gcd(B,B'). Hermite reduction uses derivative identities to write A/B=DR+S, where R is rational and the proper rational remainder S can be taken with square-free denominator after the polynomial part is handled. Repeated poles are integrated into DR; simple-pole terms remain for logarithmic or other domain-dependent treatment. The simplest step is 1/(x-a)²=D(-1/(x-a)) away from a. Exact cross multiplication checks a proposed reduction while preserving denominator exclusions. This is a direct connection between derivative factor structure and integration, not a promise of a universal elementary antiderivative. The statement about rational reduction and its scope follows the standard treatment in (`credit:nrz:cas-integration-literature`).

The absence of a primitive in the admitted expression vocabulary is a representational limitation; failure of a bounded search does not prove such absence. An unevaluated integral is a legitimate graph expression. An antiderivative certificate can be smaller or larger than the discovery process, so both generation and replay retain explicit budgets. Branches matter even for simple poles: log(abs(x)) differentiates to 1/x on the two real components excluding zero, with independent integration constants; a complex logarithm needs its own branch domain.

**Construction (Polynomial roots and local inversion)** · `constr:nrz:cas-taylor-newton-roots`

Taylor's first-order model at a gives f(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h plus a remainder. Solving that affine model when f'(a) is nonzero gives the Newton candidate a-f(a)/f'(a). The graph can derive this exact formula for the root of the affine model. It cannot conclude that the candidate is a root of f by deleting the remainder. Local convergence or an error estimate needs a separately stated theorem with its neighborhood and derivative bounds; numerical iteration and certified bounds are outside this lane. The implicit/inverse-function route similarly requires a nonsingular derivative at the operating point and establishes only a local branch (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`).

Repeated roots are classified first by vanishing derivatives, as in (`constr:nrz:cas-derivative-factorization`). Higher Taylor terms expose the leading local power when the linear derivative vanishes, explaining why an ordinary Newton denominator is then invalid. Polynomial expressions may retain exact algebraic-root requests with their defining polynomial, field, multiplicity and selection conditions; radicals are not required for representability. A candidate root is verified by exact substitution into the original equation. A complete solution-set witness must also account for every branch and any excluded denominator cases; a single successful substitution cannot do so.

Local derivative information at finitely many points does not by itself count all roots on a global domain. For example, zero derivative at an operating point cannot distinguish a multiple root from a nonroot without the zeroth-order value, and a nonsingular local inverse says nothing about a distant branch. Polynomial jets determine a bounded-degree polynomial, but deciding its global ordered roots still needs real order, field and global-domain reasoning. Exact polynomial and order arguments are a deliberate supplement where a purely local calculus certificate lacks that information (`credit:nrz:cas-polynomial-literature`). Solving an equation by division must retain or separately solve the vanishing-divisor case. These limits concern the claim a walk proves, not just the choice of a root-search algorithm.

**Construction (Spectra through variational and differential relations)** · `constr:nrz:cas-spectral-calculus`

For a differentiable matrix family A(t), let p(lambda,t)=det(lambda I-A(t)). At a simple eigenvalue branch with p(lambda(t),t)=0 and the partial derivative of p with respect to lambda nonzero, the chain rule gives lambda'(t)=-(partial p/partial t)/(partial p/partial lambda). The implicit-function theorem supplies the local branch under its regularity hypotheses. The equation and the nonzero denominator are both part of the witness. Differentiation of the determinant is polynomial in the entries: D det(M)[H]=trace(adj(M) H), which can be checked from the signed product expansion. Replacing the adjugate by det(M) M^(-1) additionally requires invertibility. Thus the graph can differentiate characteristic relations without silently inverting a singular characteristic matrix (`credit:nrz:cas-spectral-literature`), (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`).

For real symmetric A with a differentiable normalized simple eigenpair, differentiating A v=lambda v and multiplying by v transposed gives lambda'=v transposed A' v, since v transposed v=1 and the terms containing v' cancel. This supplies an alternative derivative witness. Repeated roots instead satisfy vanishing lambda derivatives of p; their first nonzero order gives algebraic multiplicity. The simple-root quotient above is then unavailable. Geometric multiplicity is a different property: the zero two-by-two matrix and the matrix with one in position (1,2) and zeros elsewhere both have characteristic polynomial lambda², but their zero eigenspaces have dimensions two and one. Characteristic derivatives alone do not recover eigenspace dimension; rank/nullspace relations must supplement them.

Ordering also has a calculus route. For a nonempty real symmetric matrix on Euclidean coordinates, stationary values of the Rayleigh quotient v transposed A v divided by v transposed v satisfy the eigenvalue equation. On the unit sphere, the multiplier equation is A v=lambda v. Compactness gives extrema, and the symmetric spectral theorem identifies their values as the least and greatest eigenvalues. The derivative stationarity condition alone does not label which critical value is largest: the witness also needs the global extremum/order argument. Sorted branches need not be differentiable at crossings; for diag(t,-t), the maximum eigenvalue is abs(t), although the unsorted branches t and -t are smooth. The concept therefore uses calculus for sensitivity, multiplicity and variational characterization, with explicit spectral/order evidence where local derivatives are insufficient (`credit:nrz:cas-spectral-literature`).

Matrix operations remain ordered mathematical maps. Differentiating A Z=B at an invertible operating point yields A delta Z=delta B-delta A Z, a linear solve relation that preserves multiplication order. This is a calculus path to sensitivities, not permission to substitute scalar commutativity or manufacture an inverse without a domain condition. Mathematical shape checking belongs to the symbolic relation. Physical axes and the units of each sensitivity remain client responsibilities (`rule:nrz:shape-sensitivity-propagation`), (`rule:nrz:shape-eigenproblems`).

**Construction (Sums as discrete integration)** · `constr:nrz:cas-discrete-integration`

For an integer argument k, define the forward difference Delta F(k)=F(k+1)-F(k). If Delta F(k)=f(k) on the integer range from m through n-1, summing cancels every interior F value and proves sum f(k)=F(n)-F(m). The witness states m and n are integers with m at most n, all involved terms are defined, and the binder is capture-safe. The empty range m=n gives zero. This cancellation proves finite telescoping directly; searching for F is the discrete antidifference problem, the calculus frame for indexed sums (`credit:nrz:cas-discrete-literature`).

Polynomial antidifferences are naturally expressed in a falling-factorial or binomial basis: Delta of the polynomial binomial(k,r+1) is binomial(k,r) for each nonnegative integer r. This follows by subtracting the two adjacent binomial polynomials and can be checked as a finite polynomial identity. Changing into that basis can enlarge an expression before making the antidifference immediate, giving another purpose for exploratory expansion. A special function or unresolved sum remains available when the requested antidifference is not in the admitted vocabulary. Infinite sums additionally need convergence and justified exchanges of summation, limit or differentiation; a finite telescoping identity alone does not provide those hypotheses.

For positive real q and nonnegative integer N, Delta(q^k)=(q-1)q^k gives (1-q) times the sum of q^k from k=0 through N-1 equal to 1-q^N. This form does not divide by 1-q and therefore includes q=1, and N=0 gives the empty-sum case. The positivity assumption keeps all powers in a single admitted real domain without choosing a convention for zero to zero. Recurrences are also discrete differential equations: Delta g(n)=g(n) with g(1)=1 has the candidate g(n)=2^(n-1) on integers n at least one. A base-case check and the recurrence check prove the candidate for every such n by induction. That is a finite induction witness schema, not an unbounded graph expansion pretending to have visited every integer.

**Rule (Limits and series retain their relation)** · `rule:nrz:cas-limits-and-series`

A limit request specifies the variable, approach point, side or path, and domain. Continuity supplies a route only when it holds at the requested point; identities on a punctured neighborhood can supply another route, as in (`ex:nrz:cas-expansion-route`). A singular expression is not evaluated at the excluded point to obtain its limit. In more than one variable, agreement along one path does not prove a path-independent limit. Unsupported existence arguments leave the request unresolved. The checker distinguishes a statement about a limit from equality of the original functions at that point (`credit:nrz:cas-analysis-literature`).

A series request specifies its expansion variables, point, order and whether it asks for a formal jet or an analytic statement. Differentiation and coefficient recurrences generate the jet. For an analytic claim the remainder identity and its regularity hypotheses accompany it; for a formal claim the stated congruence or coefficient equality accompanies it. Truncation is not an equality rewrite that discards the remainder. Neither a divergent expansion nor an unjustified exchange of an infinite sum and a derivative becomes valid through graph connectivity. These requests can be represented before the design admits algorithms for reducing them.

**Table (Classical tasks as calculus walks)** · `tab:nrz:cas-task-walks`

|Task|Calculus route|Witness and boundary|
|----|--------------|--------------------|
|Simplify|Change to a lower-ranked expression using differential or jet identities and exact arithmetic.|Identity path with original domains; no universal simplest form is promised.|
|Expand|Expose Taylor coefficients, distributive polynomial structure or a discrete-calculus basis.|Exact polynomial identities; intermediate growth is explicitly allowed and budgeted.|
|Factor|Use derivative multiplicities and square-free layers before field-specific splitting.|Derivative, gcd and multiplication identities; derivative data alone does not determine irreducibility over a chosen field.|
|Differentiate|Apply local derivative rules and compose shared Jacobians.|Derivative judgment with variable scope, regularity and branch conditions.|
|Integrate|Seek a primitive, reduce repeated poles, or change variables.|Checked derivative/fundamental-theorem relation; constants, components and endpoints remain explicit.|
|Sum|Find discrete antidifferences, telescope, or solve recurrences.|Finite-range or induction witness; infinite ranges need additional hypotheses.|
|Solve and find roots|Construct local inverses or Newton/Taylor models, then verify roots and exceptional cases.|Candidate membership and full solution-set equivalence are different claims; no discarded remainder or singular branch.|
|Series|Generate jets and coefficient relations.|Formal congruence or analytic expansion with remainder, as requested.|
|Limit|Use continuity, punctured identities or a proved remainder relation.|Specified approach/domain and existence justification.|
|Matrix operations|Differentiate defining equations and retain ordered linear maps.|Mathematical shape and invertibility conditions; physical descriptors belong to clients.|
|Eigen problems|Use characteristic derivatives and Rayleigh variational structure.|Local sensitivity, multiplicity or global order evidence, distinguished from each other.|
|Prove equality|Prove a vanishing derivative and anchor, or finite polynomial jets.|Contextual equality on the stated domain; no conclusion from sampled values or a bare graph path.|

## Reference formulas and their evidence · `sec:nrz:cas-concept-reference-formulas`

**Example (Hilbert extrema from an integral Gram matrix)** · `ex:nrz:cas-hilbert-integral`

In `data/problems/fixtures/hilbert-matrices/fixture.toml`, H_n has entries 1/(i+j-1) for integers i and j from one through n. The graph represents a finite matrix comprehension with two scoped indices, rather than an opaque function call whose matrix meaning is missing. Each entry equals the integral of t^(i+j-2) from zero to one. Hence for any nonzero real vector v, v transposed H_n v equals the integral of (sum v_i t^(i-1))². A nonzero polynomial is nonzero on some subinterval, so this integral is strictly positive. This directly proves symmetry and positive definiteness for each positive n using calculus.

The real symmetric spectral theorem then gives positive least and greatest eigenvalues; their ratio is the Euclidean spectral condition number (`credit:nrz:cas-spectral-literature`). The quotient's nonzero-denominator condition is justified by positivity. Eigenvalue extrema are typed mathematical operations with this domain, rather than uninterpreted names or an ordering of arbitrary complex roots. This makes all six H_3 through H_8 expressions representable. It does not evaluate their spectra, establish decimal digits, certify a solve or promise an inexpensive exact root computation. The numerical fixtures retain their separate certification contract.

**Example (Kahan and Wilkinson through discrete calculus)** · `ex:nrz:cas-matrix-fixture-sums`

For the Kahan formula in `data/problems/fixtures/kahan-and-wilkinson-matrices/fixture.toml`, set q=s² and N=j-1 in the discrete identity above. Under c²+s²=1, s positive and integer j at least one, c² times the sum of s^(2i) for i=0 through j-2 equals 1-s^(2(j-1)). Adding the final diagonal square gives one. The j=1 range is empty; q=1 also works without a division. The fixture's s=4/5 and c=3/5 meet these hypotheses. Bound indices are mathematical integer variables, not physical counts inferred from integer spelling.

The Wilkinson formula uses n_wilkinson at least one and the fixture's stated elimination tie rule. The mathematical premise that each step doubles the active last-column entries leads to g(n+1)=2g(n), g(1)=1. The discrete exponential witness from (`constr:nrz:cas-discrete-integration`) proves 2^(n-1). The CAS can represent and verify that recurrence theorem; it does not thereby prove that an arbitrary numerical factorization used the required tie rule or achieved a particular rounding error. Such execution evidence belongs to the matrix client.

**Example (Pendulum derivatives retain shared inputs)** · `ex:nrz:cas-pendulum-linearization`

The measurement model in `data/problems/pendulum-gravity-estimate/problem.toml` is g_i=4 pi² (l_i+r)/(t_i²(1+q)²), where r is the common ruler offset and q the common stopwatch rate. The source variables are the three l_i, the three t_i, r and q. Their mathematical identities remain distinct except where the same shared variable is intentionally reused. The graph first represents this full model, not just the specialized nominal g_i expressions, and retains the nonzero conditions on t_i and 1+q.

At l_i=621/625, t_i=2 and r=q=0, the derivative with respect to the matching l_i is pi²; with respect to the matching t_i it is -(621/625) pi²; with respect to r it is pi²; and with respect to q it is -(1242/625) pi². Derivatives with respect to the other readings vanish. These follow directly by differentiating the displayed rational model. The Jacobian has three rows and eight columns, including the two shared columns; algebraic sharing never replaces those columns with independent copies. Physical descriptors of these derivatives and their contractions are supplied and checked by the units client (`rule:nrz:shape-sensitivity-propagation`).

For the random input vector X with declared covariance U and finite second moments, define the affine model Y_lin=f(a)+J(a)(X-a) at this nominal point a. By bilinearity of covariance, Cov(Y_lin)=J(a) U J(a)ᵀ exactly. Relative to the nonlinear f(X), this is first-order propagation, with approximation status explicitly retained; no equality Cov(f(X))=J U Jᵀ is asserted. Neither dimensional consistency nor symbolic differentiation proves that U is a valid covariance or that source independence describes the experiment (`credit:nrz:cas-metrology-literature`), (`rule:nrz:shape-data-time-evidence`).

Using the input standard uncertainties in that fixture, let w=pi²/25000. The three independent length contributions have magnitudes 40w, 25w and 20w; each independent timing contribution has magnitude 10w; and the common contributions are 18w and -24w in every row. Under the fixture's independence declarations for these eight inputs, the shared contributions give each off-diagonal covariance (18²+24²)w²=900w². The diagonals are (40²+10²+900)w²=2600w², (25²+10²+900)w²=1625w² and (20²+10²+900)w²=1400w². The covariance of the mean is therefore (2600+1625+1400+6 times 900)w²/9=1225w². Since w is positive, the standard uncertainty of this affine model is 35w=7 pi²/5000. Every arithmetic step is exact; the nonlinear approximation qualification remains attached.

The independence-assumed comparison is a separately labeled hypothetical model that contradicts those shared-output covariances. Keeping the same three marginal variances but setting the cross terms to zero gives (2600+1625+1400)w²/9=625w² and standard uncertainty 25w=pi²/1000. Its numerical coefficient is derivable under that deliberately false scenario; removing cross terms is not an identity in the original context. The difference of the two reported scenario expressions is 10w=pi²/2500. This comparison object retains both scenario provenances; it does not combine their contradictory assumptions into a supposedly stronger proof of the original physical model. These three expressions account for the three pendulum identifiers in (`tab:nrz:cas-reference-coverage`).

**Example (Kepler period sensitivity)** · `ex:nrz:cas-kepler-linearization`

In `data/problems/kepler-two-body-orbit/problem.toml`, put a=rp/(1-e) and hold a and M fixed. On the positive domain a, G and M greater than zero, the period is T=2 pi sqrt(a³/(G M)). Differentiating on this branch gives partial T/partial G=-T/(2G). The derivative may be checked by the chain rule or by differentiating the positive-branch logarithmic relation. It is not a derivative with fixed initial speed: the problem instead derives that speed coherently from G while holding the geometry fixed.

At a named nominal G, the one-input affine period model has standard uncertainty abs(partial T/partial G) u_G when u_G is nonnegative and has its declared finite-second-moment meaning. Dividing by the positive nominal T gives u_G/(2G), the expression `relative_period_standard_uncertainty`. The positivity assumptions justify removing the absolute values and keep every denominator nonzero. This is exact propagation for the affine model and first-order propagation for the nonlinear period, with neither a deterministic radius nor a numerical evaluation certificate implied (`credit:nrz:cas-metrology-literature`).

## Exactness, limits and later migration · `sec:nrz:cas-concept-boundaries`

**Rule (Exact arithmetic and explicit domains)** · `rule:nrz:cas-exact-domain-policy`

The mathematical coefficient domain begins with exact rationals. Integer, decimal and rational source literals have exact rational meanings; no float is inserted as a convenience. Symbolic constants, radicals, algebraic-root requests and transcendental expressions extend what can be represented without promising algorithms to compare or evaluate all of them. The design chooses bounded storage and arithmetic policies. Exceeding an integer-size or intermediate-arithmetic limit is a representation/resource refusal, not proof that the mathematical expression is undefined. Regrouping may change whether a bounded implementation completes; any admitted result must remain exact. Exact numbers do not make approximate mathematical relations exact.

Real and complex branches, integer-index domains, matrix extents, nonzero denominators and differentiability are mathematical conditions in the core. Counts, units, dimensions, measures and statistical/physical meaning remain client declarations. A named uncertainty calculation in this document is an application of generic derivative and matrix relations, not a physics-specific intrinsic in nrz-cas. The client retains source identities and the distinction between declared statistical hypotheses and mathematical consequences. Scalar equality never authorizes a change in floating evaluation order or a claim about an enclosure (`cav:vision:unit-checking-scope`).

**Caveat (Incompleteness and cost)** · `cav:nrz:cas-incompleteness`

Derivative sharing can save repeated work but cannot bound the number of distinct generated terms. Expansion, multivariate coefficient growth, exact elimination, root selection and combinations of side conditions can consume the entire budget. A small expression can require a large witness, and a short path can refer to a costly lemma. A graph can share those objects without making their checking cost disappear. Incomplete domain reasoning blocks sound rewrites; relaxing it to improve coverage would change the mathematical claim. Conditions involving a whole interval or all integer indices need theorems or induction, not a finite set of sampled checks. There is no promised universal terminating decision procedure for all the represented operations; the terminating service is the bounded search with an honest unresolved result.

The calculus exceptions are deliberate and localized. Derivative multiplicity data do not settle coefficient-field factorization. Local root or eigenvalue derivatives do not settle global real ordering or recover a repeated eigenspace. General functions are not determined by finite jets. These limitations require additional field, order, rank or global-domain arguments, as established in the corresponding constructions above. They do not justify bypassing the calculus route for a whole family of tasks. Similarly, finite-dimensional matrix multiplication is exact ordered algebra underlying differential identities, rather than a claim that multiplication itself requires an analytic limiting computation.

**Rule (Cleanroom scope and evidence)** · `rule:nrz:cas-cleanroom-scope`

Implementation will be written from first principles and published mathematics. No code, data-structure layout or test suite is copied, ported or paraphrased from another computer algebra system, rewriting engine, e-graph or units library, including the existing core. Standard techniques may be named and their mathematics cited; the representation remains the heterogeneous graph and checked path defined here. Petgraph is the permitted graph substrate. No other new direct dependency, including its transitive closure, is admitted without the design's argument and implementation PROCEED. Reading existing client contracts establishes compatibility requirements, not an implementation template.

The design must turn each proposed executable operation into acceptance laws and refusal cases. These include source-occurrence preservation, scope/capture checks, exact arithmetic under finite limits, conditional cancellation, derivative/antiderivative relations, retained series remainders, finite-sum endpoints, matrix order, proof tampering, budget exhaustion and byte-identical repeated results. Polynomial tests can use independent coefficient and derivative derivations; the pendulum example has an independent grouping by shared source. Coverage of a representation does not stand in for execution evidence. Safe Rust, documented errors, unchanged lint settings, commit-level verification and the documentation fixed point remain mandatory (`rule:vision:gate-of-record`), (`rule:nrz:required-verification`).

**Procedure (Consumer migration after coexistence)** · `alg:nrz:cas-later-migration`

First establish nrz-cas as an independent crate with no engine-package dependency or consumer. Keep nrz-algebra, nrz-units, the descriptor prototype, numerical packages, generator behavior and problem data unchanged in this lane. The design will specify graph handles, source/span ownership, exact arithmetic, rule registration, witness replay, error/budget outcomes and the bounded Stage 3 slice; this concept does not preempt that review. Full general integration, complete equation solving and exact spectral reduction of every representable matrix are not implicit Stage 3 promises.

A later migration inventories the old rational, symbol, span, original-expression, parser, substitution and collection APIs across units, the prototype and any data-validation consumer added meanwhile. It maps original trees into occurrence subgraphs without erasing order or source evidence, ports client checks to the complete occurrence traversal, and retains immutable declaration/context snapshots. Mathematical term equality, original-occurrence identity and formal collection comparison remain distinct. A compatibility layer must not wrap a normalized result and pass it back as an original input. Changed substitutions and newly supported binders require renewed client validation. The migration preserves diagnostic locations, companion contributors and physical validation boundaries (`req:vision:expression-and-symbol-contract`), (`rule:nrz:plan-validation`), (`rule:nrz:computation-cache-validity`).

Only after supported behavior and refusals have evidence under the new contract does that later lane move dependencies and remove nrz-algebra. It updates layer enforcement and problem support records explicitly, with each generated artifact accompanying its source change. Numerical evaluation and certified enclosures stay with their separately admitted numerical owners. The new algebra graph neither replaces the physical graph nor becomes the computation scheduler: those clients refer to symbolic expressions and witnesses through their own schemas (`dec:vision:physical-and-computational-graphs`), (`def:nrz:computation-plan`).
