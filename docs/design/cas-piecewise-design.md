# Native piecewise graph and calculus design · `spec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-specification`

This design implements the mathematical contracts of (`spec:nrz:cas-piecewise-concept`) in the heterogeneous graph of (`spec:nrz:cas-design`). Owner NRZ, area nrz. The implementation base is the seed CAS plus the landed cas-tests-1 test layout. The four first-lane commits are green on `main` at `331c7759`, with the supplied baseline of 345 workspace tests. The second-lane split and germ calculus implementation is pending its external gate. The concept mathematics is unchanged.

**Decision (One bounded executable slice)** · `dec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-slice`

The first slice executes finite cases over one real coordinate with rational interval endpoints. Selection and local identities also admit values from the existing strict scalar grammar, including a reciprocal with its restricted NonZero requirement. New complete boundary and distribution calculations use rational polynomial branch values. The slice includes local contexts, guarded obligations, selection, elimination, merge, polynomial boundary jets, classical derivative and nonexistence evidence, regular distributions, deltas, polynomial smooth multipliers, delta pairings and distributional jump derivatives. Named abs, sign, ramp and rational-height steps have transparent case definitions and checked shortcuts. Existing scalar algebra, derivatives, Jacobians and square-free operations remain available on their existing domains. The new slice does not enlarge their coefficient field or silently turn a Jacobian into a full differential.

Arbitrary predicates, symbolic boundary locations, higher-dimensional regions, general locally integrable functions, distribution pullbacks, integration, sums, limits and side series have typed representations and explicit pending premises or deferred algorithms. Rational integer-range predicates are decidable even though split summation follows later. This separates the concept's mathematical scope from the two-hour implementation target: the first slice proves every requested abs/J/Q, delta-pairing and rational oscillator-impulse acceptance case without requiring a general regularity prover, root isolator, integrator or test-function synthesizer.

## Graph, syntax and admission · `sec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-graph`

**Definition (Semantic headers and mathematical spaces)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-spaces`

Keep the existing immutable environment header: field, branch convention and declarations sorted by declaration key. A syntax key uses this header and its de Bruijn scope, not the assumptions of whichever context first admitted it. Add an explicit `Layer` tag: `Logic`, `Pointwise` or `Distribution`. Add `SpaceRef::None` or a reference to a mathematical `Space`. Logic and ordinary pointwise scalar syntax use `None`; distribution and test syntax require the same declared space. Scalar-valued distribution actions and their exact rational arithmetic retain that space and the distribution layer. These tags occur in syntax, statement, residual, application, search and portable keys, including an explicit `None` where inapplicable. A bridge statement carries both endpoint sorts and spaces as well as its conclusion layer. A pointwise expression embedded in a distribution keeps its own pointwise key.

A `Space` record contains `DPrimeInterval`, an ambient real coordinate declaration, scalar field `Real`, an open connected interval region and test family `CompactSmooth`. The interval may be the whole real line. Its admission checks that its declared bounds describe an open nonempty interval in the executable fragment; a named open interval requires evidence before a theorem uses it. `Sort::Distribution(space)` denotes `D'(I)` and `Sort::TestFunction(space)` denotes `D(I)`. A declared test is a variable of that mathematical sort, not a Boolean flag asserting that an arbitrary scalar expression is smooth. `TestFromFunction` requires smoothness and compact-support premises; `PairNearSupport` is a different operation admitting a smooth germ near a proved compact support. No polynomial is admitted as a compactly supported test merely because a delta can act on it (`dec:nrz:cas-piecewise-distribution-space`).

To avoid a header/space/declaration cycle, a `Header::Core` contains only the existing space-free declaration sorts. `Header::Extend` references an earlier header and appends fresh declarations, including test or distribution declarations whose spaces already exist. It may not shadow or alter an inherited declaration, field or branch. A space and its interval syntax use a core domain header; a later full header may declare tests in that space. Admission checks the exact conservative header embedding whenever a full-context statement uses an object from an earlier header. Keys retain the actual header, and proof transport records the embedding through `ctx.weaken`; no terms are silently re-interned across headers. This also makes the dependency order executable: core header, endpoint expressions, interval region, space, extended header, test expression, context, proof.

**Definition (Region constructors and relation edges)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-regions`

`RegionExpr` is `Ambient`, `Empty`, `GuardTrue(base,p)`, `GuardFalse(base,p)`, `Meet(members)`, `Union(members)` or `Difference(base,excluded)`. Ambient domains distinguish the real line, integer line and an opaque declared domain, including coordinate identities and sorts. `GuardTrue` means defined and true within the base; `GuardFalse` means defined and false there. An undefined guard belongs to neither. Set difference is relative to its stated base and is not the false branch of an undefined predicate. Meet and union members are structurally sorted and deduplicated because their constructors denote unordered finite families; no distributive, complement or predicate-equivalence simplification occurs during interning. Empty meet denotes its ambient domain; empty union denotes the empty region.

Regions remain graph objects even when an exact interval-cell certificate is available. A certificate is evidence attached to a checked application, never a replacement for its predicate syntax. Shared boundary locations likewise acquire relations through applications. `BoundaryIncidence(region,boundary,side,neighborhood)`, `Ownership(function,boundary,Present(value) or Absent)`, `Jet(function,region,boundary,side,order,values)` and `Jump(function,boundary,order,value)` are statement variants. Their application edges are `IncidentRegion`, `AtBoundary`, `OfFunction`, `SideRegion` and ordered `JetValue(index)`, with context on the application. `Absent` requires a proof of absence, not a missing ownership record. Divergence and unknown traces are separate statements or pending results. No mutable jet array is installed on the shared boundary (`def:nrz:cas-piecewise-breakpoints`).

Preserve existing snapshot edge-role codes 0–17. Append codes 18–41 in this order: InHeader, ParentHeader, DeclarationSpace(index), SpaceDomain, RegionInput(index), MembershipPredicate, ArmRegion, ArmValue, BoundaryLocation, SourceArgument(index), CaseDomain, CaseMember(index), InSpace, AdmissibleRegion, ParentContext, ConclusionObject(index), IncidentRegion, AtBoundary, OfFunction, SideRegion(side), JetValue(index), ComparedRegion(index), CoverMember(index), ArgumentRegion(index). ComparedRegion positions distinguish subset from superset; cover members use canonical set order; sides are explicit Below/Above. Application admission reconstructs these edges from the typed statement, so they cannot contradict its payload. Extend diagnostic snapshots under NRZGRAPH02; snapshots remain distinct from portable mathematical witnesses.

**Definition (Distribution and test expression families)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-distribution-terms`

New executable operators include `DistributionZero`, symbolic `RegularAction`, `RegularEmbed`, `Delta { order }`, `DistributionAdd`, `DistributionScale`, `DistributionDerivative { order }`, `SmoothMultiply`, `Pair`, `PairNearSupport`, `TestDerivative`, `TestMultiply` and `TestApply`. General `TestFromFunction`, `AffinePullback`, `SimpleZeroDeltaPullback` and `DistributionPrimitive` remain later constructors. `Delta` takes a location in its interval and a nonnegative order. Scalar coefficients are coordinate-independent; a coordinate-dependent scalar uses `SmoothMultiply`. Distribution addition and multiplication by scalars check identical spaces. Ordinary `Mul`, `Div`, `Call` and scalar point evaluation do not accept a distribution sort. There is no unrestricted distribution-product operator. A request attempting one can still return a named refusal without admitting an ill-sorted expression.

A distribution sort checks the intended value category, not the definedness of every constructor. An unresolved RegularEmbed still contributes its L1loc obligation, and a delta still contributes location-in-interval evidence. Every derivative, linearity, multiplication or pairing checker reconstructs those input requirements before using distribution membership. The regular-embedding constructor cannot be used to erase an unproved integrability premise.

`NamedCase { name, coordinate, parameter }` is a pointwise expression with a retained definition application: abs, sign, ramp and step have the concept's exact point values; step's first executable parameter is rational. Indicator, min and max remain representable with their definitions; the first producer can elaborate them when their guards reduce to the admitted one-coordinate fragment. A name grants no primitive theorem. Canonical definition unfolding is checked within `case.congruence`: the retained case operand must reconstruct exactly from the name, coordinate and rational parameter before local agreement is used. A general smooth function, compact-support claim, local-integrability claim or zero set can be represented with typed predicates but requires hypotheses or checked evidence. Pointwise cases with distribution-valued arms are outside this first homogeneous scalar case grammar; distribution linear combinations and regular embeddings supply the required initial uses.

`TestMultiply` takes a smooth scalar function and a test in the same coordinate space, retaining the smoothness premise; `TestApply` evaluates a test or its TestDerivative at an interior scalar point. These typed operators express the symbolic right sides of pairing rules. They do not route test functions through ordinary scalar multiplication or treat them as distribution point values.

**Rule (Interning and transactional admission)** · `rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-interning`

Keep `DiGraph<Vertex,Edge,u32>`, store ownership tokens and `LogicalId { kind, serial }`, with new serial counters for tags 10–15. Runtime handles never serialize. Each new interner key is `kind, schema, header, scope, layer, space, payload`; payload references use the already validated logical IDs within a store. Region keys include ambient domain and exact constructor; arm keys include region, value and sort; boundary keys include ambient domain and exact location; space keys include interval, coordinate, field and test family. Cases include domain, result sort and the deduplicated arm set. Two differently written but equivalent guards, equal numerical expressions or overlapping regions do not share merely because a prover could equate them. Portable ordering uses structural keys without logical serials, so different admission orders still export the same proof bytes.

Context keys instead contain the complete header, parent context key, admissible region, ordered local frame and canonical assumptions with their provenance keys. Application keys contain full context, layer, space, rule key/version, typed conclusion, ordered premises, substitutions, certificate and the reconstructed conditions. A proof key names the authoritative application. Two applications differing in assumptions, ownership, region or space cannot collide even when their result expressions share. Occurrences retain their existing distinct-use behavior and source metadata; equivalent native cases never collapse different original source trees.

Space-dependent declaration drafts use a `Test` or `Distribution` sort marker plus an exact earlier-space link keyed by declaration. `ExpressionDraft::space` supplies an explicit earlier-space reference for nonleaf space-dependent expressions; a supplied leaf link must equal its declaration binding, and ordinary pointwise scalar syntax rejects a space link. Scalar-valued pairings, test evaluations, symbolic regular actions and their exact arithmetic retain an explicit space link. Expressions retain that resolved space separately from the marker. This preserves exact space identity while keeping runtime handles out of the shared scalar sort vocabulary; neither a marker alone nor a mismatched link is admitted.

Header keys are the acyclic exception to the common header-reference prefix: Core keys contain the field, branch and complete sorted space-free declarations; Extend keys contain the parent header and sorted fresh declaration records with earlier space references. They use Logic/None and never reference themselves. Drafts for expressions, predicates, regions and contexts now take a header reference; the former expression/condition first-context field is removed. Source and occurrence payloads keep their existing ownership/source keys rather than pretending to be mathematical space objects.

Extend the existing draft transaction: resolve only existing handles or backward local references; validate all referenced kinds, sorts, scopes, binders and headers; meter counts, edges, key comparisons, payload and arithmetic before allocation; stage records and indices; publish together only after success. A draft can name an unresolved region, case or mathematical condition, but cannot draft an application, proof, coverage receipt, ownership fact or jet. Contexts refer to region syntax under an environment header rather than through a context back-edge, preventing the domain/context cycle. The full heterogeneous graph may contain descriptive back-relations through applications; expression/source dependencies, context ancestry and proof dependencies remain acyclic. Any invalid reference, unsupported schema, allocation failure or budget exhaustion leaves the public graph unchanged.

## Source, contexts and conditions · `sec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-contexts`

**Procedure (Complete source elaboration)** · `alg:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-elaboration`

1. Admit `SourceForm::OrderedCases` and an original occurrence tree containing every guard, value and otherwise occurrence in written order. The complete walker visits all of them, including unreachable clauses, and visits retained declaration and binder metadata by the existing descriptor rules. It seals a receipt only after the whole original root was visited. No region normalization, name expansion or branch selection can issue this receipt.
2. Execute `ElaborateCases` only with that receipt. Starting at domain `R_1 = D`, construct `E_i = GuardTrue(R_i,g_i)` and `R_(i+1) = GuardFalse(R_i,g_i)` in source order. Associate `E_i` with its value, and associate the final reached region with the explicit otherwise value when present. Record `Defined(g_i)` guarded by `R_i`; require a value's definedness only on its associated region. Retain an unknown guard obligation as unknown. A guard proved undefined on a reached point produces a domain blocker there and never falls through.
3. The checked `case.ordered` application recomputes the ordered recurrence from the source form, checks the exact association map, and obtains disjointness and conditional coverage from region applications. A case without otherwise has no claimed final cover unless separately proved. `CaseLiteral` preserves its source order as occurrence metadata but admits the native unordered association set with overlap obligations. A construction witness identifies the original form's semantic signature, so permuting guards cannot replay as the old construction.
4. Produce a generated case occurrence with provenance pointing to the construction application, plus the original root and receipt retained on the request. Its graph traversal follows domain, membership predicates and values with typed edge roles; it is not a replacement for the original source walk. A standalone mathematical identity need not serialize source text, but an elaboration result must include its source-form signature and construction proof. A traversal receipt remains local to its store and source tree.

**Rule (Substitution across the graph)** · `rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-substitution`

Extend simultaneous free substitution and bound instantiation from operands to typed semantic dependencies: region predicate arguments and ambient coordinate references, case domains and arms, boundary locations, distribution/test operands and source forms. Lift replacements through existing de Bruijn frames, preserving free declaration identities; cases add no binder. Memoization keys include syntax kind, original scope and lifting depth. Reject a substitution that changes the sort of a coordinate, invalidates a space's interval, or leaves an out-of-scope bound reference. Stage the entire transformed dependency DAG before publication. A substituted statement must be rechecked under a correspondingly instantiated context; proofs, ownership, jets, cover certificates and traversal receipts are never copied as still valid. Generated occurrences carry substitution provenance, and clients renew physical evidence. This extends (`rule:nrz:cas-design-occurrences`) without discarding inactive original branches.

An ambient coordinate is a semantic axis, not an arbitrary scalar operand. Free substitutions rebuild predicates, values and location parameters; a coordinate rename additionally needs an exact header/space renaming map. A general replacement of a distribution coordinate by a function is a pullback request, not ordinary substitution. Substitution into a classical case may leave the decidable one-coordinate fragment, in which case its correctly rebuilt guards remain open. Derivative statements are rechecked after substitution and acquire no implicit chain-rule proof.

**Definition (Scoped contexts and obligations)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-context-model`

`ContextDraft` becomes `Root { header, admissible, assumptions }` or `Local { parent, region, hypotheses }`. A local context's admissible region is the exact meet of its parent's region and the supplied region; its hypothesis frame has stable local slot numbers and explicit provenance names. Hypothesis identity is `(introducing context, slot)`, never an unqualified string. The frame contains an explicit membership binder for the restriction and any supplied local predicates. Only ancestors are in scope; sibling frames cannot be read. Root hypotheses keep their keys and remain reported as assumptions used. Admission rejects recognized contradictions but still reports general context consistency as unknown. An empty requested domain is an explicit `EmptyDomain` blocker for a function result, not a route to an unconditional identity.

A `GuardedObligation` is `(home context, scope, layer, space, region, predicate)`, meaning that the predicate is defined and true at every point of that region under that context. It is a primitive obligation constructor, not an expansion into Boolean Not/Or. A scalar requirement from old code becomes the same requirement on the full admissible region; a branch requirement is restricted to the reached/selected region. Conditions keep three separate collections: all requirements, still-open guarded residuals, and used hypothesis references. A refuted requirement blocks that candidate; it is never moved into the unknown set.

**Rule (Canonical residual union and discharge)** · `rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-residuals`

Residuals are structurally sorted sets, deduplicated by the entire tuple above. Composition unions requirements and residuals, unions used hypotheses, and retains provenance. Canonicalization does not replace a stronger residual with a weaker one by syntactic guesswork. A checked subset proof may restrict a requirement, and a checked union/cover proof may combine equal predicates over regions; these are recorded positive-cost applications. `Guarded(Empty,P)` can be discharged by a checked emptiness proof without evaluating P. This does not discharge a requested statement on an empty domain. The inherited `Defined` and `NonZero` atoms remain available; region coverage, compatibility, smoothness and regularity use additional typed atoms.

`ctx.close` takes a checked child proof and its exact local frame. It discharges the membership binder only by the child's restriction construction. A used additional local predicate is either proved in the parent by an explicit premise or converted to a guarded residual on the child region; it cannot disappear. Unused hypotheses need no residual. Existing child residuals are rebound to the parent with the same restricted region and scope. A local name cannot survive outside its frame. `case.eliminate` then joins identically stated child conclusions using a checked parent cover. This is the operational form of (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-conditional-closure`); top-level assumptions remain visibly assumed, while open predicates remain visibly conditional.

In case elimination, the required child conclusion is the mechanical restriction of the parent statement to that cover member: endpoints and layer/space stay fixed, while every domain-bound predicate and residual is intersected with the child region. The checker compares this typed restricted statement, not literally identical domain fields and not just printed endpoints. Closing a child frame does not itself enlarge the mathematical region on which its result holds.

**Table (First decidable predicate fragment)** · `tab:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-predicates`

|Input|Bounded checker result|Outside this fragment|
|-----|----------------------|---------------------|
|Real affine form `a x + b` with rational coefficients; Equal, Positive, NonNegative, NonZero; And, Or, Not of these total predicates|Compute exact rational cut points and truth on open cells and singleton endpoints; decide membership, empty, subset, disjoint and finite cover|Nonlinear sign problems, transcendental guards, multiple coordinates and arbitrary named predicates stay open|
|Integer coordinate, integer half-open `IntegerRange` and Boolean combinations of rational affine comparisons|Exact integer bounds and singleton membership; interval-set operations with open/closed endpoint conversion|Symbolic endpoints or arbitrary sets remain open; no finite-sum algorithm is implied|

|Finite cases on the resulting real cells with rational polynomial values|Check overlap agreement by exact coefficient identity, finite exceptions, complete endpoints and local polynomial regularity|A general branch function, unknown overlap or incomplete boundary set remains a premise|
|Rational boundary point and polynomial branch germ|Exact polynomial trace/derivative jet; interval neighborhood evidence|Unproved symbolic trace, divergence of a general function and infinite accumulating boundaries remain unresolved|

The interval checker collects roots of nonconstant affine atoms, sorts exact rationals and checks each resulting cell by coefficient signs and endpoint inequalities, including the exterior rays. Its certificate contains the cuts and truth vector, but replay recomputes both from syntax; numerical samples are not a proof. Constant affine atoms are checked directly. Rational arithmetic, predicate visits and cell construction consume existing arithmetic/condition budgets plus the new cell limit. An omitted cut, a changed endpoint openness or an undefined guard read as false must fail. A ledger may retain successful reconstructed partitions keyed by complete portable region syntax, ordered roots, an explicit optional cut and the active resource ceilings. Generation and checking own separate caches; reuse across receiving stores still compares every supplied certificate and checks its asserted relation, with retained memory, keys, lookup and copying charged to the original ledger. Successful evaluation of a finite family of guards supplies a finite boundary certificate; a finite source list alone does not (`def:nrz:cas-piecewise-breakpoints`).

## Registered rules and trusted checkers · `sec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-rules`

**Definition (Registration contract)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-registration`

Retain the seed's closed `Primitive` enum and `RuleSpec { primitive, key, version, weight }`. Extend its specification with `conclusion_kinds`, typed `premise_schema`, `certificate_kind`, `cost_class` and `availability`; dispatch remains an exhaustive kernel match, not a caller callback. Each key below has semantic version 1. Existing thirty primitive keys and version-1 meanings remain unchanged. A new rule that transports a legacy result adds a new application rather than weakening the old checker. A table row with several named keys denotes separate registry entries sharing a checker and class; it does not permit an unspecified family key on the wire.

`P` means a primitive trusted theorem schema with an independently checked certificate. `D` means a derived recipe whose recorded expansion contains only admitted primitive applications; its public recipe key is not accepted as a primitive manifest entry. `L` means a reserved later rule or producer: its schema is specified here but first-slice replay refuses its primitive key as an unsupported rule version. Costs are `Structural = 1`, `Local = 2`, `Split = 4`, `Jet = 4`, `Analytic = 8`, `Pullback = 12`. Preserve the existing logical-path convention: one multi-premise transition has its family weight; supporting proofs consume replay work, not extra path edges. Before shortcut admission, a recipe uses the sum of the actual transitions in its expansion route. A checked shortcut defaults to positive weight one only after strict-improvement admission with its full expansion pinned. Expanded primitive count and replay work remain separately measured; no recipe has a free logical transition.

For compactness, `C; E ⊢ S` means statement S under full context C, restricted to E, with its reconstructed conditions. All conclusions below inherit the exact layer, space, scope, requirements, residuals and used hypotheses prescribed above. A premise may be supplied conditionally, but a conditional theorem does not become discharged by being used. Unless explicitly stated, type admission is not a premise proof. Every analytic theorem's mathematical justification is given here or in the cited concept label; replay checks the hypotheses and exact formula, not a numerical approximation.

**Rule (Small trusted analytic boundary)** · `rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-trust`

The analytic primitives above are explicit theorem schemas. Delta evaluation and the signed derivative action are definitions; smooth multiplication preserves test smoothness/support. The delta-multiplier formula follows by the finite Leibniz rule in that action. The supported extension follows by a cutoff equal to one near the compact support; any two cutoffs give the same action because their difference vanishes there. The jump primitive is integration by parts on the certified finite polynomial cells: outer test-support endpoints vanish and each interior boundary contributes right trace minus left trace. Higher derivatives repeat that first-order rule and increment existing delta orders. There is no extra trusted order-n or named-function theorem (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-jump-formula`), (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-distribution-operations`).

These schemas are deliberately narrower than a proof assistant for arbitrary analysis. Their trusted code checks exact polynomial/cell certificates and the stated logical transports; search cannot add a theorem by changing a registry record. A general absolute-continuity hypothesis can support a conditional represented jump claim, but the first `dist.jump` checker accepts only its finite polynomial certificate schema. If that certificate cannot be built, the producer reports the missing supported regularity certificate; it does not label an arbitrary named smoothness assertion as a completed calculation. General affine/simple-zero pullback and primitive uniqueness remain reserved later schemas with their explicit mathematical premises (`tab:nrz:cas-piecewise-distribution-rules`).

## Calculus transforms and search · `sec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-calculus`

**Procedure (Boundary-aware classical differentiation)** · `alg:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-classical`

1. Elaborate names and source cases only after original coverage. Compute the finite cell partition if the guard fragment permits it, carrying the complete boundary-list proof. On each open cell use selection, the existing polynomial derivative producer and `diff.case-interior`. Publish a region-indexed partial derivative map even if a different cell remains pending; never call it a total derivative.
2. At every rational cut inside the requested domain, obtain incident left/right neighborhoods, actual ownership and branch jets. Shift each branch polynomial about the cut to compute its trace and first derivative. The boundary checker compares both value traces to the actual point value before comparing slopes. Equality of the neighboring derivative limits alone cannot establish a join.
3. For equal value traces and equal slopes, return `DerivativeAt`. If a finite value trace differs from the actual point value, discontinuity proves `DoesNotExist`; if continuous but slopes differ, the two difference-quotient limits prove `DoesNotExist`. Missing ownership, a missing side, unknown regularity or unknown coverage returns a pending premise, not nonexistence. A restricted-domain endpoint can have a one-sided result; it cannot acquire a two-sided ambient derivative without both neighborhoods.
4. For higher orders, differentiate the certified first derivative object, including any established point values, and repeat. Q therefore has a first derivative at zero but no second derivative there. Jet traces of higher branch derivatives do not assert that the whole function has those derivatives at the boundary. General divergence certificates and full multivariable differentials remain later work; the first checker can prove the discontinuity and unequal-slope cases needed here.

**Procedure (Distribution derivative and pairing)** · `alg:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-distribution`

For `Differentiate` in the distribution layer, require a distribution-sorted input or an explicit checked regular embedding. Decompose finite polynomial cases using their complete cell cover, compute right-minus-left jets and apply `dist.jump`. Repeat for the requested order, differentiating each regular piece and incrementing existing delta orders. Add exact equal delta terms by location, space and order; do not identify different supports by display names. Traces at exterior endpoints of I are excluded because the test support is interior. An unknown full boundary list blocks a full jump decomposition while the formal distribution derivative remains well-typed.

Admitting the formal derivative constructor does not complete a request for its jump expansion. The producer reports the missing decomposition premise or deferred fragment while retaining any already checked distribution facts. This preserves the distinction between existence of a distribution derivative and computation of its regular/delta representation.

A named shortcut producer and the case producer both propose proof routes. For abs, sign, ramp and step, the shortcut expands to the same case definitions, jets and jump applications before it can be stored. A test pairing of a delta sum applies linearity and `dist.delta-pair`. A polynomial smooth multiplier applies `dist.delta-multiply`; exact zero coefficients may then disappear through checked linearity. Ordinary declared tests can yield symbolic test-derivative evaluations. The executable numeric examples use `PairNearSupport` with the polynomial germ `x² + 3`; the supported-action proof makes this equal to the concept's compactly supported cutoff tests without inventing an explicit bump formula. General regular-distribution pairings remain unevaluated unless their integrals are separately proved.

Finite split statements retain the original function, coordinate, closed interval, oriented rational bounds and exact value. Their certificate reconstructs the entire selector partition through the existing ledger-local cache. Each integral open cell has a rational polynomial and a checked primitive, so it extends continuously to both endpoints; every cut records its actual finite value or certified absence. The theorem concerns any finite-valued extension at those finitely many points. A hole on an open cell remains pending. Sums have no finite-point exception: every included integer must be defined. The producer solves antidifference coefficients triangularly, while replay checks polynomial composition and explicit endpoint powers. Existing `case_arms`, `region_cells`, `boundary_nodes`, polynomial, arithmetic and wire limits bound both routes; no unmetered summand enumeration is used.

Standalone germ statements retain the original function, exact boundary, real coordinate, approach, mode and coefficients. A side series of order n means the checked identity f(a+h) = P(h) + h^(n+1)R(h) on a nonempty punctured side neighborhood. Its exact polynomial remainder supplies the asymptotic bound. A punctured side claim need not match an exceptional value at a; an ordinary two-sided Taylor claim additionally checks actual ownership. Analytic mode requires both full polynomial germs to agree, including the remainder and centre, even when a side was requested. It therefore proves a common polynomial analytic extension, never convergence of an arbitrary formal series. The existing jet-order and cumulative jet-entry limits apply unchanged. Each complete result replays its jet/trace dependency closure once using the shared partition cache.

Delta pullbacks use a map from the target interval into the source interval. The original delta, resulting delta sum, both exact spaces, map and optional affine inverse are all statement dependencies. Maps and inverses use total rational polynomial syntax: positive integer powers and divisions only by nonzero constant polynomials; strict zero powers and hidden denominator holes refuse. Affine checking proves a bijection of the complete open intervals by exact endpoint mapping, reversing endpoints for a negative scale. Simple-zero mode currently requires a source delta at zero on the whole real axis; the supplied strictly increasing rational roots must satisfy the complete coefficient identity p(x) = a product(x-r). Every listed root is checked, including roots outside the target interval, and only strict interior roots contribute deltas. The generator evaluates slopes by Horner recurrence; the checker independently evaluates derivative coefficients by explicit powers and verifies abs(p'(r)) times the claimed weight equals one. `pullback_zeros` is a peak ceiling, checked before root copies or wire allocation. Partition geometry reuses the existing cache.

`PrimitiveUnique { space }` consumes two covered distribution expressions. Its producer proves both first derivatives and, if necessary, their exact distribution equality. The checker retains these premises and reconstructs the connected open interval. The conclusion means there exists a constant distribution equal to the difference; it chooses no boundary condition or numerical constant. Step and step plus seven provide the acceptance example because both derivatives equal delta at zero. The named delta-primitive recipe remains derived, with no extra primitive key.

The split follows independent proof obligations, not the apparent simplicity of operator names. Integration and sums introduce endpoint and extension contracts; side limits and series introduce approach/remainder contracts; pullbacks introduce coordinate-space changes and zero completeness. They can use the first slice's regions, jets and wire machinery without enlarging its trusted checker during the same implementation round (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-integration`), (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-summation`), (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-limits-series`).

**Rule (Lazy common refinement and merge)** · `rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-search`

The common refinement of two case partitions is the family of nonempty meets of their regions. A lazy frontier item stores the two case IDs, parent context, operation, layer/space, the next canonical pair of arm keys, accumulated checked cells and their residuals. Creating the frontier is not a proof of its eventual cover. Expand one pair at a time, meter the attempted pair even if empty, request a subset/disjoint certificate and apply the strict operation only on that meet. A complete result needs a checked cover and all required compatibility/definedness evidence. Existing identical regions reuse the same region object and cached proof under an exactly matching context; worst-case work remains the product of arm counts. Nested cases use the same lazy mechanism.

Merge only after proving that the proposed value and definedness match each constituent on its entire region. Construct the union and prove both replacement directions. A smaller graph is a search candidate; it is not automatically a theorem. Both the named-calculus producer and case-split producer enqueue transitions before dominance filtering, using a stable producer order and canonical structural tie-breaks. No eager case expansion hides a shorter named route, and no name recognition suppresses a route with weaker residuals. Search retains the two existing cost/progress queues and their quotas (`alg:nrz:cas-design-queues`).

**Definition (Cost policy and dominance)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-policy`

The current default policy uses key `logical`, version 8 for 59 active checkers. The new `dist.affine-pullback`, `dist.simple-zero-pullback` and `dist.primitive-unique` schemas have weights 12, 12 and 8 and semantic version 1. The new `limit.case-restrict` and `series.case-restrict` schemas have weights 2 and 4 and semantic version 1. Existing scalar weights are unchanged; new weights use the classes in (`def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-registration`). Every active primitive has its key/version and positive weight in the complete immutable manifest; reserved later schemas are absent. Overrides name exact primitive keys and remain in the existing positive bounded range. A changed table increments the policy version and invalidates old improvement caches. Generation work, replay work and logical proof cost remain separate accounting dimensions.

No external consumers, policy records or stored artifacts exist yet, so no frozen legacy policy is retained. The 40-entry native-case table used version 3 and the 43-entry boundary table used version 4; the version 8 pullback table replaces these, the 52-entry distribution table at version 5, the 54-entry split table at version 6 and the 56-entry germ table at version 7.

Dominance compares only states with identical current endpoint, remaining task payload, requested statement, full context, layer/space, claimed domain, coverage status and source-receipt obligations. Coordinate order, jet order and pairing mode are part of that payload. Within that class, A may dominate B only if A has no greater cost, peak size, consumed expansion credits, proof depth or witness footprint, and its required proof/source/rule support is a subset of B's. Retain the same used-hypothesis class; residuals must be identical or related by a recorded checked implication, with that proof's cost/support included in A. Require a strict improvement in at least one resource; exact ties choose canonical proof bytes. Unknown totality cannot dominate a proved cover. A partial derivative map cannot dominate a whole-domain derivative; pointwise and distributional claims never compete. Region/boundary interning improves storage, not logical strength.

Frozen search pins the graph frontier, available applications/shortcuts, rule manifest, complete cost policy and all region/context/space keys. Only proof dependencies older than that snapshot may define a new shortcut. An in-round discovered region or jet may be a normal transition with its full cost, but cannot retrospectively make its own expansion an older shortcut. A strict improvement is replayed against the frozen baseline and records the entire expansion. Changing a region's description, a local hypothesis, a space, a rule version or a policy weight invalidates the comparison. This preserves (`dec:nrz:cas-design-search-policy`) with region objects present.

**Definition (Version-2 envelope and record table)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-wire-format`

The envelope is magic, sorted primitive manifest, bounded record count, length-delimited records, conclusion record reference and end of input. Integers use the existing canonical exact-number encoding; fixed unsigned counters are little-endian u32 for counts/indices and u64 for measured quantities, tags are one byte, booleans only 0 or 1, and UTF-8 names have bounded u32 byte lengths. No alternative numeric spelling, redundant sign, trailing bytes or duplicate canonical record is accepted. Each record is `tag, payload_length, schema_version, layer, optional_space_reference, payload`; all reference targets precede the record, and absent spaces are encoded explicitly. The space record itself uses `Logic/None`; a distribution/test expression references that already defined space. The wire IDs below belong to v2 and are independent of graph vertex tags.

The record `payload_length` counts every byte after that length field through the end of the record, including `schema_version`, layer and the space option. `schema_version` is u32; Layer is the one-byte tag 0 Logic, 1 Pointwise or 2 Distribution; the space option is 0 None or 1 followed by an earlier u32 record reference. Missing, surplus or ill-kinded space fields fail. Record-specific vectors are u32-counted and bounded before reading their elements; optional references use the same 0/1 convention.

**Procedure (Canonical export and replay)** · `alg:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-replay`

Traverse only the conclusion's transitive semantic/proof dependencies, including every context ancestor, retained source-form signature and residual predicate. Export their dependency DAG with Kahn topological order: among ready records choose the lexicographically least full structural descriptor using already assigned child wire indices; kind and schema are the first tie-breaks. Ordered operand/premise lists retain order; unordered region/arm sets sort by portable structural descriptor. Deduplicate exact identical records; occurrence source identity remains distinct in local receipts and is not part of a mathematical equality key. Reject dependency cycles rather than inventing forward references. This produces the same bytes from the same reachable structural dependency DAG regardless of allocation serials or producer scheduling.

Replay validates the manifest and lengths before allocation, decodes into an unpublished arena, checks every kind/sort/header/scope reference, reconstructs contexts and invokes the same primitive kernel independently of search. Recompute conditions, hypothesis use, coverage and statement conclusions. Re-encode canonically and compare every byte with the input, then compare with the caller's expected typed statement. Only after all checks pass may the result be published. A mathematical proof import cannot manufacture an original traversal receipt. A rejected import or exhausted replay leaves the store unchanged.

`WitnessBytes`, `ProofNodes`, `Vertices`, `Edges`, payload, depth, arithmetic and replay-work limits all apply, together with every specialized region/context/jet/test bound. Length multiplication and declared counts are checked before reserving memory. Test at exactly the needed limit and one unit below, including large false counts in tiny byte strings, deeply nested contexts, repeated shared references and cyclic/forward references. Old-version witnesses are refused before record decoding; resource exhaustion is a refusal, never reinterpretation.

## Public API and clients · `sec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-api`

**Decision (Explicit public API movement)** · `dec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-api-movement`

This is a breaking public API revision, not an extra scalar helper. Keep root `Cas`, `Handle`, `LogicalId`, `Reference`, `Rational` and structured errors. Move construction vocabulary (`Draft`, `ExpressionDraft`, `OccurrenceDraft`, declarations, sorts, operators and binders) into public `syntax`; regions, spaces, arms, boundaries and local-context drafts into public `domain`; traversal types into public `source`; request/outcome/search types into public `request`; statements, proof steps, witnesses and registry descriptions into public `proof`; limits and accounting into public `limits`. Private implementation files need not have those same names; facade modules can re-export them. Do not maintain two competing root aliases for the replaced claim/request vocabulary. Consumers are migrated together only after the new core is accepted.

Replace `Operation` with `Task`. Replace `Claim { relation, left, right, ... }` with `Judgment { context, layer, space, statement, residuals }`. Replace flat public `Guard { kind, term }` with `GuardedObligation`; keep Defined/NonZero as predicate constructors. Replace `ProofStep` with `ApplicationDraft { rule, context, conclusion, premises, substitutions, certificate }`, where a typed conclusion may have zero, one or several expression endpoints. `VerifiedWitness` still has a private constructor and exposes its checked judgment, used hypotheses and bytes. Retain public `check_proof` and `replay` names with typed expected judgments. Private scalar matcher records carry only lossless views of current typed theorems; they are not a compatibility API or wire format.

**Definition (Requests and typed results)** · `def:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-api-types`

`Request` contains `task`, `context`, `inputs: Vec<CoveredInput>`, `premises: Vec<VerifiedWitness>` and `limits`. `CoveredInput` pairs an occurrence root with its matching sealed traversal receipt; structural domain/boundary references belong to the task. `Task` adds `ElaborateCases`, `Select { region }`, `Eliminate { regions, conclusion }`, `Merge { regions, proposed_value }`, `Coverage { domain }`, `Boundary { coordinate, point, requested_facts }`, `Differentiate { coordinate, order, layer, domain }`, `Pair { space, action_mode }`, `SmoothMultiply { space }` and `Pullback { source_space, target_space, map_kind }`. `Pair` has two covered inputs; its mode is `Test` or `NearSupport { neighborhood }`. A distribution derivative accepts a distribution input; regular embedding is an explicit construction task with its own premises. Existing simplify, equality, expansion, Jacobian, square-free, integral, sum, limit, series and solve/spectral task intents remain, with their previous field/domain exclusions.

Add `EmbedRegular { space }`, `ApplyAt { coordinate, point }` and `Product { requested_sort }` tasks so mathematical refusals are expressible before ill-sorted expression admission. `CasesDraft { domain, arms }`, `RegionDraft`, `BoundaryDraft` and `SpaceDraft` produce only typed syntax handles through `admit`. `source::ordered_cases` and `source::case_literal` assemble complete original forms and occurrences, returning no receipt until the caller completes the walk. `substitute` and `instantiate_bound` accept and return typed syntax roots; `materialize_derived` constructs fresh generated occurrences with explicit provenance before a new complete walk. `rename_axes` requires an explicit exact coordinate/header/space map. `Eliminate` carries its typed target conclusion as well as its cover members. `run` and `run_with_policy` keep their execution names and return the revised outcome.

`Statement` variants include `PredicateFact { region, predicate }` and `Constructor { region, law: Membership }` for qualified hypothesis facts and exact defining membership laws, followed by `Equality { domain, domain_contract }`, `DefinedDomain`, `Subset`, `Disjoint`, `Cover`, `Empty`, `Compatibility`, `Elaboration`, `Incidence`, `Ownership`, `Jet`, `Jump`, `DerivativeOn`, `DerivativeAt`, `DerivativeDoesNotExist`, a boxed `Distribution(DistributionStatement)` family with `Regularity`, `Embedding`, `Equality`, `Derivative`, `Pairing` and `SupportAction` variants, plus the existing square-free/Jacobian relations and later integral/sum/limit/series relations. `domain_contract` is `DefinedAndEqual` only when the requested endpoints are both proved defined on the claimed domain, or `SameDefinedDomain` when both definedness directions are also proved. No two dummy scalar endpoints encode a cover, absence or nonexistence statement. Grouping the distribution family keeps its exact shared space explicit while bounding the outer statement size; primitive equations are represented by checked formal-derivative equalities.

`SameDefinedDomain` additionally names the ambient comparison region: it requires a separate equivalence of DefinedDomain regions there, and equality on the entire common defined region. It never supplies values at undefined points. The simpler DefinedAndEqual contract proves definedness and equality only on its stated requested region.

Boundary requests distinguish `Jets { order }` and `Jump { order }`, which admit order zero, from `Derivative { order }`, which requires a positive order. Existing `DerivativeOn` keeps its first-order meaning; `CaseDerivative` records an explicit positive order and domain. Higher point checks consume a previous `CaseDerivative` covering the point and both incident neighborhoods. The actual previous derivative includes independently established singleton values. The `diff.case-interior` assembly mode consumes the complete partition and a matching derivative fact for every inhabited cell in the claimed region; a partial result may assemble only a union of whole certified cells.

A result payload is `Expression`, `Cases`, `RegionFact`, `BoundaryFacts`, `DerivativeMap`, `Distribution`, `PairingScalar` or an existing scalar/Jacobian/square-free result, each with its typed witness and claimed domain. `DerivativeMap` lists proved open-cell derivatives, proved point derivatives, witnessed nonexistence points and pending cells; its completeness field itself requires cover evidence. The executable boxed payload retains sealed witnesses, a separate proved derivative region, and exact pending cells; a generic replayed-facts prefix survives when construction stops before a map can be completed. Higher derivative levels may replay a covering proof root once and retain its sealed subproofs only after verifying their membership in that root's premise closure. Facts outside that closure, including omitted nonexistence points, require another fresh replayed root under the same cumulative budget. `BoundaryFacts` carries ownership and independently scoped jets. An assumed theorem still reports every used root hypothesis; no API name promises a general consistency proof.

|Variant|Meaning and required evidence|What it cannot imply|
|-------|-----------------------------|--------------------|
|`Proved { result, witness, usage }`|Requested typed statement replayed with no open residual; used assumptions remain visible|General context consistency or unrelated domain totality|
|`Conditional { result, witness, residuals, usage }`|Replayed conditional statement with canonical guarded obligations|Discharge of its premises|
|`Nonexistent { statement, witness, usage }`|A replayed mathematical nonexistence theorem, such as the derivative at zero of abs|Search failure or unsupported syntax|
|`PendingPremise { partial, obligations, usage }`|An identified premise lacks evidence, for example ownership, smoothness, compatibility or a supported regularity certificate|A refutation of that premise|
|`UnknownTotality { partial, requested_domain, uncovered_obligations, usage }`|Coverage has not been proved; any partial result retains its own domain|A proved hole in the function's domain|
|`Partial { derivative_map, witness_set, pending, usage }`|Individually replayed facts with explicit incomplete coverage or mixed existence/nonexistence|A whole-domain derivative expression|
|`Refused { reason, request, evidence, usage }`|A named operation refusal or a proved domain blocker|An unrestricted mathematical impossibility theorem|
|`Unresolved { request, operation, usage }`|Well-typed task whose registered producer is deferred|A pending premise invented to explain absent implementation|
|`Unsupported { request, reason, usage }`|Existing field/coefficient/grammar exclusion, extended with the admitted piecewise fragment|Nonexistence of a mathematical answer|

`RefusalReason` includes `DistributionPointValue`, `DistributionProductUndefined`, `TestSpaceMismatch`, `RefutedDomain`, `EmptyDomain`, `OverlapConflict` and later `MultipleZeroPullback`. Delta times delta and a proposed delta-times-step product use `DistributionProductUndefined`; a `SmoothMultiply` request with an unproved smoothness premise uses `PendingPremise`, and a proved discontinuity at support refuses that smooth-multiplier route with the same named product category. Wrong test space is a type-level `TestSpaceMismatch`, while unknown compact support is a pending premise. A witness of an actual coverage hole is returned as a domain fact/refusal with its region, never `UnknownTotality`. Malformed references and unsupported wire versions are structured invalid-input faults. All paths return values; checked indexing, arithmetic and fallible allocation prohibit panics.

Classify a requested point derivative as Nonexistent only with its nonexistence witness. A whole-domain derivative with a mixture of derivative and nonexistence regions returns a DerivativeMap through Partial, even if every location is classified. Its classification cover and its actual derivative-defined region are distinct fields. For an otherwise unfinished request, an unproved requested cover selects UnknownTotality and includes the other obligations; with a proved cover but another missing premise use PendingPremise. A conditional completed derivation uses Conditional. Deferred producer availability uses Unresolved rather than either mathematical status. Invalid input, named refusal and exhaustion retain their own error categories.

**Procedure (Consumer call sketches)** · `alg:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-clients`

For nrz-units: parse the retained original syntax into `source::ordered_cases`, perform the complete walk and check every branch's physical signature, including inactive ones. Admit the mathematical context/regions, call `run(ElaborateCases)`, then `run(Select)` or the requested pointwise/distribution `Differentiate`. Match outcomes explicitly and retain the witness's guarded domain conditions in the physical receipt. Physical kind, affine point/difference role, coordinate chart and unit dimensions remain client data. An ordinary scalar slot rejects a distribution result. Substitution invalidates both the old traversal/derivation context and the physical receipt until revalidated (`rule:nrz:cas-piecewise-consumer-migration`).

For a coordinate x of dimension X, the one-dimensional delta `δ(x−a)` has dimension `X^(−1)` and its kth coordinate derivative has dimension `X^(−k−1)`. A regular embedding retains the dimension of its representing function; distribution differentiation divides that dimension by X. Pairing against a test of dimension P includes the coordinate measure and has dimension `dimension(T) × P × X`. Thus an impulse J with momentum dimension times a time delta has force dimension, and a polynomial smooth multiplier multiplies dimensions normally. Unit conversions also carry the absolute Jacobian; the core checks it only when the pullback rule is admitted. Affine physical coordinates require a difference/chart authorized by the client. These are client signature rules, not a new physical sort inside CAS (`ex:nrz:cas-piecewise-impulse`).

For docgen problem validation: keep the versioned parser and original byte locations, assemble covered inputs and use `ElaborateCases`, `Coverage` and `Equal` with an explicitly chosen domain contract. Produce deterministic records containing schema version, context/declaration snapshot, supported/unsupported reason, source relationships, residuals and witness identity. Configuration selection runs in the client before the chosen homogeneous mathematical expression is checked; it does not turn a topology-changing recipe into a case value. Introduce native case or distribution syntax only with an explicit problem-schema version/capability update and regenerated validation evidence. Existing unsupported records stay unsupported until their actual missing operation is implemented (`rule:nrz:problem-data-versions`).

For prototypes/descriptors: rebind expression/symbol/rational slots to the new construction and source facade, compare formal collection structure separately from `Equal` with `SameDefinedDomain`, and use `substitute` followed by fresh complete traversal and physical validation. Coordinate maps can request pointwise boundary evidence or distribution pullback with both spaces; a deferred pullback is propagated as unresolved. Uncertainty-mode, precision and structural-size selectors remain categorical plan metadata. Migrate all nrz-algebra imports and tests before removing that package; the new API is the target, with no silent parser or diagnostic compatibility claim (`alg:nrz:cas-design-client-boundary`).

## Evidence and implementation sequence · `sec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-delivery`

**Decision (Four test layers on the landed base)** · `dec:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-test-layout`

Assume cas-tests-1 has landed on main before implementation starts. Inline unit tests use only module-private values and no harness. Crate tests live under `src/tests/` and carry labels with kind test and area crate. Public conformance tests live under `tests/conformance/`; consumer-boundary integration tests live under `tests/integration/`; both are modules of the single `tests/public.rs` target and carry labels with kind test and area integration. Shared setup/builders, mutation helpers and bounded replay drivers live in `src/testing/` under the test lane's visibility convention. The harness may construct inputs and replay outputs; it must not compute expected mathematics by calling the producer under test. This design does not edit that lane or introduce a second public test target.

**Procedure (Four green implementation commits)** · `alg:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-implementation`

1. `feat(cas): add scoped region judgments and v2 replay` introduces facade/API movement, space/region/arm/boundary/source-form syntax, context frames, guarded conditions, typed statements, region checkers and the sole v2 codec. New operation requests may honestly return unresolved. Include admission, header dependency, residual, transaction and wire-mutation tests in the four landed layers. Acceptance: the existing suite stays green, the independently re-authored v2 golden replays and old-version bytes refuse, and a rational cover plus a local guarded claim replay through v2. Target 30 minutes, using the existing encoder, transaction and condition infrastructure.
2. `feat(cas): elaborate and prove native cases` adds complete source descriptors, capture-safe graph substitution, ordered construction, selection, elimination, merge and lazy common refinement; increment the current policy version and enforce producer/refinement charges before enabling these producers. Acceptance: source-order and inactive-partial examples, unknown cover versus actual hole, compatible overlap, ownership, no sibling leakage, both definedness directions on merge, deterministic v2 bytes and budget refusals. Target 25 minutes; no calculus producer is needed to make this commit useful or green.
3. `feat(cas): certify piecewise boundary derivatives` adds complete finite-boundary certificates, polynomial jets, jumps, classical interiors and point/nonexistence results, plus transparent named case definitions. Acceptance: abs/ramp and J/Q outcomes exactly match (`tab:nrz:cas-piecewise-design-acceptance`), with singleton, wrong-side, wrong-function and ownership tampering rejected. Reuse exact polynomial and derivative kernels; introduce no transcendental limit solver. Target 25 minutes.
4. `feat(cas): check distribution jumps and delta actions` enables the finite polynomial regularity certificate, embedding, finite-exception transport, delta calculus, smooth polynomial multipliers, supported pairings and jump derivatives, then records named shortcuts and both search routes. Acceptance: both routes to `D² T_abs = 2δ_0`, J/Q, the pairings, the multiplier sign and oscillator impulse; named distribution refusals, layer/space tampering and replay/resource checks all pass. Target 25 minutes, with 15 further minutes reserved for the complete gate and fixed-point review.

The 120-minute target assumes the test lane and existing scalar machinery are available, and the exact rational fragment is held fixed. The largest risk is the combined public-model/codec movement in the first commit; establish its round trip before adding producers. Each later commit adds producers only after their admission/checker/codec forms are tested, and runs the relevant layer tests plus required repository checks. If an implementation defect needs a correction round, keep the unmet acceptance explicit; do not mark a missing first-slice operation deferred merely to fit the clock. No consumer migration, general root solving or broad analytic certificate mechanism belongs in these four commits.

Each implementation commit keeps the one current `logical` policy identity and increments its version whenever its active checker table changes. Commit 1 introduces the complete specialized limit vocabulary and enforces applicable admission/replay charges; later producers add their charges before becoming executable. Unimplemented rules remain unavailable, even when their syntax/codec records are already admitted; no accepting placeholder makes an intermediate commit green.

The second lane implements split polynomial integration and integer telescoping at policy version 6, standalone one-sided limits and side series at version 7, and rational affine/finite simple-zero pullbacks plus primitive uniqueness at version 8. Each commit registers its semantic-version-one schemas and complete immutable manifest, with independent endpoint, remainder, zero and Jacobian evidence. General regularity, accumulating boundaries, nonlinear guard solving, full differentials, general distribution products and a Fourier/tempered-space layer require separate design work. The current design supplies honest unresolved contracts for them and does not claim they fit that second lane.
