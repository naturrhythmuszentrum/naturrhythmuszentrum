# Tiles, streams and reusable computation · `spec:nrz:tiles-streams-design`

**Principle (One engine contract for lowered questions)** · `prin:nrz:tiles-streams-contract`

This design specifies how to realize the decided tile, mask, barrier, reuse and flush contracts of (`sec:nrz:lc-grid-decisions`) and (`sec:nrz:lc-stream-decisions`) for any checked lowered question. LC is a worked consumer, not an engine type. The descriptor graph owns exact meanings and proof dependencies; the computational graph owns region versions, kernel applications and heap masks; bindings joins them under (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-two-graphs`). CAS receives only mathematical descriptors, decisions and their pure exact evidence through bindings, never heap data, mask bits or storage handles (`rule:nrz:decision-edge-cas-heap`). Matrix owns storage, bindings owns correspondence, and the plan owner owns graph dependencies and declared continuations, preserving (`rule:vision:layer-enforcement`). All behavior below is an implementation contract; its LC prerequisite standing and LC completion are recorded in (`sec:nrz:lc-closing-register`), particularly (`tab:nrz:lc-closing-tiles-evaluation`). Implementations preserve existing limits, defaults, tariffs and Work pins.

**Rule (Graph objects and three separate identities)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-identities`

An authority node identifies a checked exact judgment; a lowered lambda identifies a checked executable subgraph with formal tile bindings; an application identifies that lambda's actual region, mask and companion versions. Citation edges reuse authority, application edges bind current storage, and retention edges protect its occupations. None can substitute for another. Exact preparation precedes the numerical subgraph or occurs at its decision barrier; no kernel constructs descriptors or calls CAS. Host readings remain heap values without a declared input range or pre-arrival support gate (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-arrived-decisions`). Tables below map labelled obligations, not cell results: original outcomes are graph paths through bindings, layout maps, masks and evidence under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-outcome-view`).

## Exact lambdas and cache authority · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-exact-reuse`

**Definition (Anonymous proof lambda)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-proof-lambda`

Every proof fact is a lemma, including a trivial identity or an inline derivation. A named schema has a revision and checked instances; an anonymous lemma has no display-name identity. It closes its typed parameter telescope, ordered premises, conclusion and derivation over their immutable dependency DAG under (`def:nrz:reuse-lambda`). Its complete canonical statement fixes relation and original target, declarations and context, stratum, ordered quantifiers, premises and residual obligations, representation and semantic/checker/proof revisions. Bound-variable renaming follows (`conv:nrz:reuse-canonical-form`); free declarations, source occurrences, branch ownership and premise order do not disappear. A ground proof cannot be reused as a family theorem. Applying a family lambda checks typed substitutions and every premise in its original scope under (`rule:nrz:reuse-application`).

**Definition (Statement identity and proof identity)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-exact-key`

Use `K_J = Can_v(kind, revisions, parameters, premises, judgment, judgment_dependencies)` and `K_proof = Can_v(K_J, derivation_dependencies, derivation)` from (`def:nrz:reuse-content-keys`). References abbreviate complete immutable graph content; canonical tags, lengths and ordered edges resolve structural equality, with hashes only selecting candidates. A statement lookup selects the first eligible checked derivation of that complete statement in the total order of (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-lookup-order`). A request to check or return a particular witness also keys its complete proof content, so corrupted bytes cannot hit a good proof of the same conclusion (`rule:nrz:reuse-statement-key`). Local authentication adds retaining owner, namespace generation and sealed node identity outside the portable mathematical key. Bindings retains physical declarations and exact source/map correspondence beside the CAS judgment; a bindings-level reuse key includes their complete immutable identities without teaching CAS physical units or heap semantics.

**Rule (Heap values enter only through decided exact scope)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-key-boundary`

No exact key contains float bits, a buffer address, mask population or bits, tile ordinal, arrival time, heap generation, host hint or a cached numerical answer. Equal readings do not establish equal sources. An arrived value can affect exact identity only through the exact cut or stratum it is decided against, including the certified enclosure premises that the barrier constructs and checks for that judgment under (`rule:nrz:decision-exact-certificate`), (`rule:nrz:decision-enclosure-and-cut`). Such rational bounds are mathematical certificate content, not an alternate scalar import path for the heap. Exact source declarations, immutable assumption versions and joint-map premises do belong in the statement when its claim depends on them. Numerical source/companion versions qualify a separate live attachment under (`def:nrz:tiles-streams-attachment`); they are not portable proof fields. A source-independent lemma has no invented source dependency, while an application to a source-scoped question retains that source's actual premises.

**Definition (Constant and identity keys)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-constant-key`

A prepared exact constant keys its judgment kind and revision, original exact expression, declaration/context closure, definedness/domain obligations, exact representation and preparation authority. A normalized result alone cannot identify the original expression. A trivial identity adds its relation, ordered original operands and premise judgments to that key and borrows the prepared constant node; it does not prepare that constant again. The already supported constant mechanism is (`def:nrz:reuse-constant-instance`). An exact root bracket additionally keys the original root expression, sign and squared-endpoint predicates, exact bracket and derivation revision. Machin authority keys its theorem revision, both term counts, exact partial sums and declared bounds under (`def:nrz:reuse-machin-instance`); consumer target/context checks remain on its application. Numerical constant imports use the separate prelude key of (`def:nrz:tiles-streams-prelude-key`).

**Definition (Stratum and cut keys)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-stratum-key`

A stratum keys the original relation/family, ordered supplied/query roles, exact parameter and index domains, parent stratum, declaration/context snapshot, retained joint source/map assumptions, ordered quantifiers and conditional fibres, domain/nonemptiness premises, partition and owned boundaries, representation and proof revisions. A cut additionally keys the original target and coordinate map, exact enclosure domain, predicate, selected option or exact parameter tuple, strict/inclusive convention and every enclosure, separation or equality premise. If an origin certificate is retained, its statement and proof have their own complete keys, including law order and exact mathematical input-family premises. Reusing a cut never substitutes for the origin proof linking the original target to that enclosure. Matching a selected option alone hits none of these objects. Unsupported or exhausted searches may be bounded memos keyed by their complete search snapshot and allowances; they are not negative mathematical lemmas (`def:nrz:reuse-index`).

**Definition (Cover and inclusion keys)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-cover-key`

A cover certificate keys the covered exact domain and original relation, the complete finite or parameterized alternative family, parameter ranges, ordered quantifier scope, union/coverage judgment, disjointness and boundary-ownership premises, context and source/map closure, representation and proof revisions. An inclusion certificate keys both directed endpoints, their same-target/coordinate correspondence, the precise inclusion relation and nonemptiness or feasibility premises required by the transported claim. A child cites the parent proof and adds its inclusion evidence; it does not clone the parent's proof graph. Numerical hull overlap, sample counts and matching layouts supply neither key equality nor inclusion. Family proofs retain their binders; a finite enumeration cannot replace an unbounded cover (`rule:nrz:decision-option-ranges`), (`rule:nrz:decision-membership-locality`).

**Definition (Decided barrier content key)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-outcome-key`

A reusable decided outcome keys the decision schema/revision, complete original question and target, stratum, exact cut or witness judgment, ordered quantifiers and actual proved domain, selected option and exact parameters, original assumption/context closure, enclosure/cover/inclusion premises, representation and proof revision. This is exact authority for that scoped conclusion. It contains neither a tile's result mask nor its current attachment. A uniform family conclusion can serve several applications only within its proved quantified scope; per-index feasible witnesses cannot become one shared-source witness (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-quantifier-order`). An undecided or refused attempt retains its evidence and reason as an attempt node; it never enters the decided-authority index as a theorem of the complement.

**Rule (One retained authority graph and three reuse scopes)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-cache-tiers`

Within one evaluation graph, citation edges and a bounded local index reach already admitted nodes, including staged nodes only within their enclosing transaction. Across full flush, the evaluation's warm owner retains eligible immutable exact authority and lowered templates after all heap attachments have gone. A CAS-wide store can share checked lemmas among graphs using that same authenticated store and generation, following (`def:nrz:reuse-authority-graph`) and (`def:nrz:reuse-portable-instance`); it is not a process-global trust flag or a second proof database. These scopes are routes to shared nodes, not three copied payloads. Lookup follows (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-lookup-order`); its receipt identifies each actual probe and the selected proof. Another CAS store independently imports and checks portable evidence. Store destruction ends local eligibility, while independently owned portable bytes remain replayable.

**Rule (Deterministic lookup and recorded admission)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-lookup-order`

The warm and store routes inherit (`rule:nrz:plan-cache-keys`), (`def:nrz:reuse-replay-state`) and (`rule:nrz:reuse-determinism`). Probe a supplied local reference first, authenticating its owner/generation and complete requested claim; a malformed, foreign or stale reference terminates lookup with its typed refusal. In the absence of a local reference or after an admissible local miss, probe the evaluation's warm index and then its authenticated store index, stopping at the first eligible complete match. Each index orders candidate entries, including hash collisions and ineligible entries actually examined, by increasing recorded admission serial. Entries sharing a batch serial are ordered lexicographically by complete canonical statement and proof bytes, then by retained owner logical identity, namespace generation and sealed node identity; identical tuples denote one coalesced entry. Other retained kinds use their complete canonical kind/content keys in the same tie-break. Hash narrowing fixes its algorithm and parameters in the replay state and only selects the candidate set: bucket iteration, resizing and address order cannot reorder its probes. A particular-witness request additionally requires the complete proof-key match of (`def:nrz:tiles-streams-exact-key`). All comparisons, authentication and unsuccessful probes remain charged under (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-cache-charge`).

**Rule (Serialize cache history at publication)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-admission-order`

Each index owner assigns nonwrapping, increasing admission serials at its serialized publication boundary. Concurrent store admissions acquire a recorded total order there: before making a batch visible, record its request identity, serial and ordered entries, together with the relative order of relevant lookup snapshots, commits, rollbacks, evictions and replacements. A lookup observes one fixed committed index view; transaction-local staged nodes retain their enclosing transaction's declared order. Replay uses that recorded order or an explicitly fixed admission schedule, never lock timing as an unstated input. Existing entries keep their serial on a hit; eviction and readmission acquire a fresh serial, and exhausted counters refuse before identity reuse. The replay state retains next counters, index/proof contents, retained/reserved usage and all history effects consulted by selection or accounting; history completely represented by that state need not be duplicated. Required history outside the bounded resident state uses the explicit evidence-delivery capacity of (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-residence`). Equal requests with equal initial reuse state and history reproduce proof choices, probes, costs and bounded outcomes; different states or histories need not select the same proof or complete under the same allowance.

**Rule (Charged lookup and second preparation)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-cache-charge`

Preparation receives a canonical statement/proof reference or constructs it with bounded, charged work, then looks up authority before invoking any constructor or proof producer. A second preparation of identical eligible exact content in the same retention scope performs one logical lookup and authenticated citation, with no repeated preparation, derivation, full pi validation, declaration rebuild or hidden nested re-preparation. Its hit receipt separates key construction/comparison, probe, authentication and any actual application/copy work under (`rule:nrz:reuse-work`); one lookup is not a claim that arbitrary-length keys or a new application cost one universal Work unit. An already canonical sealed local reference avoids re-encoding the DAG. The existing constant citation charge remains unchanged. A miss pays probing and the complete actual construction, checking and admission. New live attachment, numerical import and portable replay have separate scopes; none is relabelled as the second preparation.

**Rule (Bounded retention and eviction)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-cache-bounds`

Before key construction or admission, reserve keys, comparison scratch, proof closures, indexes, reverse dependencies, staged additions and owner registrations against the existing caller and store ceilings. Shared allocations count once by authenticated owner; an index is charged for its references, not again for another owner's already counted payload (`rule:nrz:plan-resource-ownership`). The tile owner's warm index uses deterministic oldest-admission eviction among entries without an active application/transaction pin, with the admission order and tie-break of (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-lookup-order`), (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-admission-order`); a hit does not alter that order. Removing an index route cannot revoke independently retained proof consumers. Eviction releases a payload only after its last owner releases it, and reports actual recovered capacity. If no eligible removal makes the simultaneous peak fit, report the resource outcome without exceeding a bound. Existing subowner policies, including the initial plan index's no-eviction contract (`rule:nrz:plan-cache-keys`), remain intact. Warm after flush means surviving eligible entries, not unbounded retention or immunity to explicit eviction.

**Rule (Invalidation and transactional publication)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-cache-invalidation`

A changed declaration, exact source assumption, map, partition, representation or authority revision loses eligibility along its dependency closure. Old immutable lemmas remain theorems of their old premises while retained; a new context needs checked application or transport. Current input or companion changes invalidate attachments and evaluated artifacts, not source-free exact lemmas. Local authority lookup authenticates owner and generation even when content matches; rollback advances the reuse namespace while CAS allocation high-water marks prevent discarded logical identities from recurring, and exhaustion never wraps (`rule:nrz:reuse-node-lifetime`). Stage new authority, evidence edges and index references together under (`def:nrz:reuse-transaction`). Refusal removes the unpublished suffix and keeps spent Work; independently committed preparation can survive a later failed application. No cache hit revives a retired numerical attachment.

**Rule (Replay remains an independent obligation)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-replay`

Export retains the exact judgment, hypotheses, complete derivation DAG and required revisions under (`def:nrz:cas-design-witness-format`). A receiving store checks canonical encoding, all premises and primitive revisions and the caller's expected claim under (`alg:nrz:cas-design-replay`), (`rule:nrz:cas-design-expected-claim`); producer handles and digests supply no authority. Only a receiving owner's already authenticated complete proof-key match can omit a repeated local replay. Portable evidence cannot authenticate current heap versions. Historical evidence delivery includes resolvable premises, not dangling digests. Different valid derivations may share a statement key while producing different witness bytes; byte-identity claims pin the proof key and original identities (`rule:nrz:reuse-evidence-bytes`).

## Lowered lambdas and numerical reuse · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-lowered-reuse`

**Definition (Lowered tile lambda)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-lowered-lambda`

A lowered lambda is the checked finite subgraph between semantic barriers, abstracted over formal input/output tile bindings, shape parameters, layout maps, masks and permitted prelude inputs. Its body contains only closed kernel applications and their numerical guards, temporary regions and companion propagation; its boundary retains checked correspondence to the original descriptor subgraph under (`def:nrz:descriptor-binding-lowered-subgraph`). The template has no actual input occupation and owns no computed answer. Application substitutes live versions and reserves concrete output bindings before kernels run. A numerical guard may authorize an admitted numerical law; a new exact conclusion ends this lambda at a decision barrier (`rule:nrz:decision-crossing-authority`).

**Definition (Lowering compatibility key)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-lowering-key`

The key fixes the original descriptor subgraph and ordered roots, schema and lowering revisions, exact context/stratum and guard authority, formal binding meanings and reading/restoration maps, ordered source/companion relations, representation and shape class, layout class, input/output mask roles and coverage law, precision, kernel/arithmetic/error-model revisions and admitted numerical policy. When a numerical endpoint representation is admitted, the key explicitly names ordered stored lower/upper endpoints or the exact dyadic c−r/c+r reading of a centre/radius pair, together with its decoding and checked correspondence to centre, deterministic-total-radius and numerical-only-error readings under (`req:nrz:tiles-streams-matrix-owner`). Different endpoint representations have different attainable endpoint targets and are not interchangeable merely because they enclose the same set. A shape class is a checked parameterized law with explicit extent constraints, strides, component multiplicity and supported gather/broadcast behavior; equality of dimensions alone is insufficient. Actual extents and maps must discharge that law on application. Unrolled extents or specialized coefficients enter the key when the implementation specializes them. Formal slot identities replace concrete tile addresses, region versions, mask bits and arrival ordinals. Captured semantic dependencies remain in the key even when their numeric values happen to agree.

**Rule (Reuse across tiles and continuations)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-lowering-application`

Reuse one lowering across successive tiles, stream segments, branch tiles and refinement subgraphs exactly when its compatibility key matches and the new binding discharges all shape, layout, mask-role and guard parameters. Rebinding validates ownership, actual versions, initialized demand and companion targets without reconstructing the exact descriptor graph. Branch-local authority is a required parameter or captured context, never silently dropped. A changed layout class, precision, kernel revision, reading map, premise or declared continuation requires a different lowering; a changed input version merely requires a new application unless it invalidates a captured guard. Each application computes fresh enclosures from its actual inputs. The lowered template can survive flush; numerical results cannot be cached under its template key (`rule:nrz:computation-result-cache`).

**Rule (Hoist dependency-independent nodes)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-hoisting`

Dependency analysis on original descriptor and guard edges identifies every node independent of the varying index. Such a node lowers into a shared prelude evaluated once per compatible graph binding and version key, with every tile referencing its published region rather than evaluating it again (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-hoisted-constants`), (`rule:nrz:plan-dependency-classes`). Hoisting across branches requires independence from their selection and domain guards; otherwise share only within that authorized stratum. A reciprocal of one measured C is graph-invariant but still depends on C, its source, deterministic bound and numerical-error versions. Invariant arithmetic, constant import and shared source-Jacobian evaluation remain distinct from exact proof reuse. Missing or changed dependencies cause a fresh prelude application, never reuse by equal centers.

**Definition (Evaluated prelude key and lifetime)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-prelude-key`

The prelude key extends its exact node and lowering key with ordered actual input owner/occupation/version selections, source and companion revisions, complete guard attachments and concrete precision/maps under (`def:nrz:descriptor-binding-reuse-key`). A source-free constant has no input versions but still has representation and error-model dependencies. Its value, mask and error companions are ordinary heap regions with binding coverage and reader pins. Full flush releases them; the warm exact constant and lowering template authorize a separately charged fresh numerical import on renewed input. Numerical factors, Jacobians or result caches, if a question supplies them, have their complete artifact dependency keys and share this heap lifetime. They cannot be hidden in the warm exact cache to defeat empty-heap completion.

## Decision barriers and their applications · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-barriers`

**Definition (Barrier interface and demand edges)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-barrier-interface`

A barrier node declares its original target, partition or quantified question, context/stratum, permitted result and refinement schemas, cumulative allowances, flush contract and ordered input-demand edges. Each demand joins a bound symbol to specific live value, filled-mask and companion versions through a checked axis/layout selection. Its readiness predicate states which demanded cells or producer completions are necessary; a tile boundary supplies no implicit condition. A sub-tile decision uses an explicit selection map and demand mask over the published parent tile, for example the cells belonging to one window. A multi-tile decision joins several such edges. Dynamic membership selection is itself a predecessor kernel or decision result with a declared law; it cannot be discovered by reading a lent or unpublished tile. All reads occur after complete handback/publication, including a decision needing only a small subset of a host loan.

**Rule (Freeze and classify actual arrived inputs)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-barrier-evaluation`

On becoming runnable, the barrier freezes its complete demanded version closure under the matrix lease, checks bindings and uses the actual filled set. Native kernels may scan enclosures and propose masks or candidate strata; proposed membership has no semantic authority. The barrier then authenticates existing exact enclosure/cut or witness authority, or constructs and checks the missing pure mathematical certificates. It selects an already declared continuation only after its original-target, domain, boundary and ordered-quantifier obligations hold under (`rule:nrz:decision-enclosure-and-cut`). A general fallback exports exact rational enclosure/cut evidence at this barrier and pays its full cost. Distinct enclosures or premises may cause distinct proof work even when many cells select the same branch. A cached hull or one previous cell's origin proof never certifies another cell by assertion.

**Rule (Cached families still require current membership)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-barrier-cache`

A family fast path requires a checked parameterized origin/membership recipe whose declared premises cover the original input meanings and certified numerical law on the complete family. Its application binds the current version closure and verifies the demanded cells against the recipe's exact stratum, using its proved outward comparison/import contract. The recipe's pure enclosure and cut lemmas are the cached authority; the native check and new attachment are actual barrier work. No unchecked kernel comparison discharges a CAS premise. If that recipe or its exact replay argument is unavailable, use charged per-input certificate construction or publish `UnsupportedCertificate`; a template hit cannot manufacture family authority. Repeated exact cuts/strata use (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-cache-charge`) across cells, tiles and arrivals. Observable origin, cut, lookup and attachment costs delimit the fast path instead of claiming that every arbitrary stream has constant decision cost.

**Definition (Family membership application boundary)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-membership-recipe`

A membership recipe has a closed mathematical lambda proving `origin(x, P, E) and E subset S imply target(x) in R`, with its exact original-target relation, premise family P, enclosure E, cached stratum S, cut `S subset R`, boundary convention and all quantified parameters explicit. Its binding interface accepts only authenticated numerical enclosure origins and a checked current containment result for the same selections. The result is a new scoped application edge plus its live attachment, never the old application's authority reused unchanged. Containment may be checked by an admitted comparison law with certified representable bounds directed into S, or by exact rational export and comparison at the barrier. For an open upper face, equality does not pass; an unrepresentable or overlapping bound falls back or stays undecided. Native checks discharge only the predicates proved by that closed law; any extra symbolic premise needs its own checked evidence. The recipe key combines the complete origin, containment and cut lambda keys and representation/revision, with actual versions outside it. This interface isolates the remaining owner proof obligation without allowing a numerical flag into CAS.

**Definition (Live decision attachment)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-attachment`

The attachment is an evidence edge joining a checked outcome/family application to its actual selected input and output region versions. Its key includes decision/application identity, complete exact authority references, heap owner and occupations, ordered value and mask versions, selected cell/layout maps, required companion identities/versions/targets, source/assumption and reading-map revisions, assurance and arithmetic revisions. The bindings owner retains the semantic part and opaque tickets; matrix authenticates their live storage. This key is not an exact lemma key. Replacing a companion, source or map invalidates the transitive attachment/selected-lowering closure even with unchanged centers under (`rule:nrz:decision-invalidation`), (`rule:nrz:lattice-attachment-lifetime`). Native version checks never ask CAS to read the heap.

**Rule (Publish masks and selected graph edges together)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-barrier-publication`

Stage the checked exact application, outcome masks, selected snapshot, concrete continuation bindings, dependency pins and availability edges as one declared publication group. A mask-only result references the previous tile through a new immutable mask version; a materialized result has a new tile, its own mask and checked layout-map edge to the earlier regions. Fallible validation and reservations finish before the no-callback commit publishes the whole group under (`rule:nrz:decision-selected-publication`), (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-atomic-publication`). No dependent kernel sees a mask without its authority or required companions. Failure leaves the selected outputs unavailable, rolls back unpublished graph additions and retains the attempted costs. Independently published preparation remains reusable only within its explicit transaction scope.

**Rule (Undecided masks and refinement edges)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-undecided`

Partition the participating demand into settled option/domain masks, undecided masks and refusal masks with explicit reasons and coverage. An undecided mask keeps the last valid enclosure, exact pending obligations, attempt history and complete candidate domain when no finite enclosure is justified. It grants no complement membership. The decision may activate its declared tiled refinement subgraph, retaining settled masks and assigning only its unresolved demand to the child. The child uses later strata and checked same-target/inclusion/cover edges; no descendant supplies a premise for its own input lowering (`rule:nrz:decision-stratification`). Attempts, exact Work, native work, bytes and depth share the parent's finite allowance and do not reset on cache misses or new arrivals. Exhaustion follows the declared undecided continuation under (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-stop`).

## Masks, layouts and tile choice · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-memory`

**Definition (Packed heap mask)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-mask-storage`

For a tile capacity C, a mask occupies a separately versioned, zero-initialized contiguous allocation of `ceil(C/64)` unsigned 64-bit words, aligned for that word type. Logical cell j uses bit `j mod 64` of word `floor(j/64)`; unused high bits in the last word are zero. Checked arithmetic reserves `8*ceil(C/64)` payload bytes plus actual allocation capacity and owner metadata. This is native bit storage owned by matrix kernels beside the floating arenas, with no physical scalar meaning and no CAS node per bit. C is the number of logical cells, not the number of float components: the tile layout maps each cell and component to its numerical address. A mask names that same cell-layout identity, capacity and axis association, so changing a component stride cannot silently change bit meaning. No cache-line alignment, unsafe cast or backend bit layout is required.

**Rule (Every tile and companion has matching coverage)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-mask-coverage`

Each input, output, temporary, branch, prelude and refinement tile has a mandatory filled mask. Additional masks select roles such as branch membership, settled output, refusal and undecided. Several roles may reference the same immutable bit allocation when their meanings and cell correspondence permit; roles remain distinct graph edges, not multi-state bits. Companion tiles with different component shapes carry their own matching masks and checked target-cell maps. A participating value cell is readable only when every required companion selection is initialized and published for that target version. Matrix validates mask capacity/layout and target versions before reading values. Kernels iterate set bits or scan words and then load only selected initialized components; vectorized procedures must use safe masked loads or bounded packing and may not load holes speculatively. Empty masks execute no value loads.

**Rule (Mask algebra follows checked graph correspondence)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-mask-algebra`

For masks sharing one validated cell correspondence, intersection selects common demand, union selects combined demand, and complement of A is `F & ~A` within the participating filled set F. Padding bits are cleared after every operation. A decision partition satisfies pairwise disjointness and `F = union(selected masks, undecided masks, refusal masks)` for its declared demand; no unfilled cell enters that equality. Branch joins union only compatible outputs with preserved original identity; overlapping outcomes need a checked reconciliation law, not double counting. With distinct layouts, an explicit checked pullback or pushforward maps masks through the graph's cell association before combination. Bitwise operations on unrelated buffer positions are refused. Kernel edges carry these maps and masks alongside numerical operands, never an exact collection of selected cells.

**Rule (Demand determines multi-input coverage)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-mask-demand`

A pointwise application requires the intersection of its required inputs' filled masks after their checked cell maps. Missing required cells remain unavailable on that demand edge; intersection cannot silently certify the omitted portion of a requested domain. A contraction, reduction or stencil declares its complete operand incidence: an output cell becomes filled only when every required input contribution is available, or when the operation's explicit absent-input law proves a different scoped result. Broadcasting a constant requires its own filled singleton and checked expansion map. Statistical cross-companions retain both target maps and joint sources under (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-companion-reading`). Thus a reduction cannot treat an unfilled term as zero, and masks do not weaken the operation's exact law.

**Rule (Version masks with their qualified regions)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-mask-versions`

A mask version is immutable after publication and identifies its allocation occupation, cell-layout version and qualified target-region bundle. Its bindings and application edges pin all required versions. A changed bit set creates a new mask version; a changed target value or companion requires a new publication/attachment even if the bit pattern is unchanged. Shared mask-only views retain the parent data versions until their last reader releases. An exclusive loan exposes only the engine's initialization-tracking interface for its pending filled mask; the host cannot fabricate initialized bits or publish it independently. Word initialization is safe zero-fill, while value writes are restricted to engine-provided initialized storage or safe setters that track complete cell/companion initialization. No unsafe uninitialized-memory access is introduced.

**Rule (Counts and compaction preserve original identity)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-mask-compaction`

Popcount supplies a checked native population for allocation and reporting, not an exact physical count, a source identity or proof of domain coverage. A compaction kernel scans the branch mask, computes checked prefix counts and writes selected cells and all required companions to new disjoint storage. Its gather-map edge maps each new cell injectively to the original cell/axis/source association; multi-parent compaction also names each retained parent occupation. The map is bounded native layout metadata with a checked family reading relation, not one descriptor per selected cell or an independent source. The result has its own filled mask, with unused capacity clear. Publication validates injectivity, companion correspondence and complete movement before exposing the new versions. Count overflow, map capacity and copy costs are explicit outcomes; the old versions remain protected until every copy and downstream older-region read finishes.

**Rule (Choose tile size under simultaneous bounds)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-tile-choice`

For each admitted layout/shape class, nrz computes feasible capacities using checked extents, component sizes, mask words, companions, layout maps, output-family extent, scratch, prelude and all protected or lent occupations. The choice must fit the simultaneous peak, applicable numerical/error constraints and graph allowances, not merely the input buffer. From feasible candidates nrz selects the capacity nearest the host's optional size hint, breaking equal-distance ties toward the smaller capacity and then the admitted layout's stable identity; without a hint it chooses the greatest feasible capacity in the lowering's declared preferred layout class. The preference is a representation choice, not a deadline or a fixed override. The host learns the actual layout and capacity. No feasible positive capacity yields an explicit resource outcome. A tile boundary never changes a barrier's demand or the original question (`rule:nrz:lc-grid-size`).

**Rule (Copy a branch only when its admitted cost warrants it)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-branch-choice`

The default branch result is a new mask over existing data. Compare it with an admitted compacted-layout continuation using the known remaining consumer graph: estimate native work for scanning/gathering the parent on all those edges versus mask counting, copy, map construction, companion movement and subsequent dense reads. Use one declared cost-model revision and like units; report byte residence separately rather than adding bytes to time. Compact when its complete estimated native cost is lower and its simultaneous old-plus-new peak fits, or when it is the only feasible continuation after its checked copy/release sequence. Otherwise retain the mask view; unknown reuse extent supplies no invented saving. Choice changes representation only, preserves all guards and measured actual costs, and never forces contiguous original indices. If neither realization fits, the runnable leaf waits with the resource outcome under (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-walk-capacity`).

**Rule (Partial and empty tiles are ordinary graph inputs)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-partial-tile`

Handback may publish any initialized subset, including an empty mask; unused capacity creates no suffix obligation. Its bindings preserve original associations for actual cells and no observation for holes. Later independent input can run without waiting for earlier unfilled cells unless its demand edges require them. A partial output mask reflects its operation's actual completed demand, while missing or failed requested outputs remain explicit graph outcomes. Tile resizing or refinement creates new applications/maps and masks; it cannot alter deterministic uncertainty, exact boundaries or tolerance. The same contracts apply to finite axes and open streams (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-partial-tile`).

## Region lifetimes and resource ownership · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-lifetimes`

**Definition (Descriptor-held region pin)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-region-pin`

A binding's opaque region ticket is paired by matrix with a retention edge to the exact allocation occupation, value/mask/companion versions and required layout-map owners. This pin grants lifetime, not writable access or mathematical truth. Pins belong to active descriptor bindings, barrier inputs, application readers and delivery obligations; an exclusive host loan has its own non-cloneable ownership edge. Mask-only continuations and new-layout continuations reading older regions both retain these edges across barriers. A static lowering template has formal slots and no such pin. CAS proof nodes have no heap pins; the bindings/matrix application owns their live association under (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-validation`).

**Rule (Release the last use without erasing history)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-release`

Completion or cancellation of a consuming application releases its input-use edges after its outputs and required evidence are safely published or explicitly unavailable. Retire an occupation only after the last live binding reader, kernel, borrower and delivery obligation releases it. Graph reference counting over the acyclic use graph or an equivalent bounded reachability walk can implement this; release follows edges, not tile age. Historical descriptors retain immutable mathematical snapshots and resolvable evidence but change their live availability to retired, removing their numerical pins. Preserve or export required input/evidence paths before release; failure to deliver them keeps retirement and flush unfinished. Later refinement of retired answers needs bounded restoration or reports unavailable history under (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-retirement`). A reused slot advances generation and cannot satisfy an old ticket (`rule:nrz:matrix-core-resolution`).

**Rule (Account for every resident owner)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-residence`

The execution owner reserves distinct bounds for heap bytes, graph nodes/edges, exact authority, caches, walk storage, loans, attempts and evidence delivery. Heap accounting includes lent capacity, initialized and unused tile storage, all masks and companions, scratch, gather maps, prelude values and every descriptor-protected older occupation. Graph/cache accounting includes keys, proof closures, schema/provenance registries, reverse edges and staging; transient simultaneous peaks count before allocation. Native index metadata and observer capacity are visible beside the heap and cannot hide duration-dependent growth. Shared allocations count once with all retaining edges discoverable. Exported output/history has a separately declared external capacity and evidence contract. Dropping a local reference does not claim bytes recovered while another owner retains them (`rule:nrz:plan-resource-ownership`), (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-bounded-resources`).

## Bounded ready-leaf walk · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-walk`

**Definition (Readiness is graph state)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-readiness`

The execution DAG has bounded active node slots with stable application identities and nonwrapping occupation generations, ordered input-demand edges and reverse consumer adjacency. Each active application retains a count of unsettled producer edges and a readiness state derived from those edges, its selected authority and exclusive-access requirements. An edge is pending, published with the demanded coverage, or terminal with an explicit absence/refusal/inactive outcome. Terminal absence is not a value; it makes a node runnable only if that node's declared absence or flush contract can consume it. Inactive alternatives never await nonexistent numerical output. A barrier's input predicate can inspect published mask metadata to recognize sub-tile readiness; it cannot ignore a still-required producer. Static admission checks all semantic and numerical dependencies for acyclicity under (`rule:nrz:decision-stratification`).

**Rule (A bitmap indexes runnable leaves)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-walk-index`

Store the derived ready state as a hierarchical bitmap over allocated graph-slot capacity C, with V currently occupied slots, fixed word width w and one bounded cyclic traversal cursor. Here C is slot capacity, distinct from the tile-cell capacity of (`def:nrz:tiles-streams-mask-storage`), and `V <= C`. Slot indirection resolves the current application identity/generation; retired slots can be reused only after all incident uses release and their generation advances. Bitmap extent depends on allocated slot capacity C, never cumulative stream population or a monotonically growing historical identifier. There is no task queue, priority, worker allocation, deadline policy or scheduler. The cursor finds the next set leaf in stable slot order, validates its current edge generations, executes its admitted operation if capacity fits, and continues from that position. Clear a leaf's bit when visited or when invalidated; set it only on an actual readiness transition. Eagerly update every changed summary along its path so the next-leaf search observes exact summaries. A synchronous drive call walks until there are no executable leaves or its declared finite traversal allowance is exhausted, then reports quiescence, waits or remaining runnable work. Resuming preserves the cursor and cumulative attempt state. Independent leaves continue when one leaf waits; refinements add only checked finite later-stratum nodes with reserved graph capacity.

**Rule (Arrival and dependency release costs)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-walk-release`

For positive slot capacity C and fixed word width w, the bitmap has `h = max(1, ceil(log_w C))` word levels. Let B be the total number of words across the base layer of `ceil(C/w)` words and successive summaries through the single top word; `B = O(ceil(C/w)+h)`. Reserving and zero-initializing these layers costs `O(B)` storage and native word work, irrespective of V; allocating and initializing the C slot records costs `O(C)`. Initial activation builds V occupied node records and E demand edges in `O(V+E)`, fills the base ready bits in `O(V)`, and constructs summaries bottom-up in `O(B)`, for total `O(C+V+E+B)` work and storage, plus actual mask checks. Zero capacity has no bitmap words or runnable slots and constant bookkeeping. Handback, a published kernel result or a decided barrier visits only the changed producer's outgoing edges, marks each matching generation once and decrements its consumers' unsettled counts. Masked readiness recomputes only those edges' demanded coverage. A release touching d edges, performing m mask-check operations and changing r readiness bits costs `O(d+m+r*h)`, including all summary updates; finding one leaf costs `O(h)` summary operations. Immutable static incidence can be reused across applications, but concrete changed edges are still counted. Version invalidation traverses its affected closure in `O(V_affected+E_affected)` with one visitation mark per activation. Complete invalidation additionally performs mask checks and clears readiness bits with their summaries, costing `O(V_affected+E_affected+r*h+m)` for its actual changed-bit count r and mask-check work m. Bitmap height depends on C even when the affected closure or occupied count V is small. No arrival scans historical retired graphs or an unbounded stream prefix.

**Rule (Capacity wait is an observable graph outcome)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-walk-capacity`

Before running a leaf, reserve its complete incremental peak beside live owners. Failure leaves the leaf and its input pins intact, marks a resource-wait bit and reports owner, required resource, available capacity and blocking dependencies when known. Continue walking other ready leaves without repeated retries of that leaf in the same capacity epoch. A relevant release, explicit host drive or changed admissible binding can trigger one bounded rescan of the waiting set; scanning B waiting nodes costs `O(B)` plus their actual reservation checks and bitmap updates. No engine policy steals a loan, drops input, expands a bound or chooses a different mathematical refinement. Exhausted cumulative Work or an intrinsically oversized demand is reported as such; repeated drive cannot replenish it. The host decides whether to release consumers, return loans, request a different admitted operation, flush or stop. A graph with all capacity tied up in required inputs can remain waiting; the contract promises no deadlock-free completion for an unfit question.

**Rule (Release and continuation activation are atomic)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-walk-activation`

Publication installs its complete version/evidence group before notifying outgoing demand edges. Each notification carries the producer application generation and cannot satisfy a replacement application accidentally. Reserve child nodes, masks, retained input uses and reverse edges before a barrier activates a selected or refinement subgraph; failure reports the exact decided outcome with its continuation unavailable, or the node's declared undecided/refusal outcome when no decision was proved. It never runs an incomplete continuation. Dependency-release and lifetime-release edges are separate: making a consumer ready does not release a producer still read by that consumer. Completed, failed and inactive consumers discharge their remaining use edges explicitly, so terminal failures cannot leave unexplained permanent readiness waits.

## Scoped flush contracts · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-flush`

**Definition (Flush notification on a graph scope)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-flush-scope`

`flush(scope)` creates a generation-stamped graph notification that no more work is expected for the targeted evaluation or declared subgraph. Its edges reach the active barriers and producers in that scope and any later continuation/refinement applications they activate while draining. The notification is idempotent for that generation and consumes bounded graph-walk storage. It closes expectation edges for future contributions; it does not complete already admitted upstream applications, reclaim a loan, synthesize missing values or bypass a dependency. A barrier runs only when its actual producer states and its flush-specific readiness predicate permit it. A full scope includes the evaluation's heap owners and delivery obligations. A partial scope names subgraph roots and their declared owned dependency closure, never a stream identifier or an input as such (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-flush`).

**Definition (Required node flush interface)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-node-flush`

Every node design declares ordinary and flushed input-demand predicates, which producer completions remain mandatory, which unproduced contributions become terminal absence, the exact domain/quantifiers of each possible result, output masks and companions, permitted refinement continuations, remaining allowances, terminal outcomes and release/delivery obligations. Its interface exposes notification, readiness under current producer states, execution over actual published demand, and completion after outputs and releases. These are graph transitions with versioned dependencies, not arbitrary host callbacks inside a kernel or publication. A node lacking this contract cannot enter a flushable graph. The generic engine implements propagation and draining; the node owner proves its absence semantics. Flush neither changes an existing proof's quantifiers nor refills a retry allowance.

**Rule (Input, kernel and output behavior under flush)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-flush-native`

A pending input producer without a loan can close its unproduced contribution edges as absent. An outstanding loan remains pending until successful handback or cancellation returns ownership; a failed genuine handback returns the loan for repair or cancellation under (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-host-loan`) and does not satisfy flush. No notification infers which bytes were filled. A pointwise kernel consumes its published masked demand and produces only valid corresponding output cells. A kernel requiring missing contraction terms or a complete neighborhood publishes the declared unavailable outcome instead of substituting zeros. Prelude and copy nodes run only when their consumers still require them; otherwise their use edges retire. Output/delivery nodes preserve pending consumer loans, result access and evidence export until explicitly released or acknowledged. None of these outcomes declares that future input can never arrive.

**Rule (Local decisions and refinements under flush)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-flush-local`

A local decision consumes the arrived subset of its own demand once upstream producers terminate. It decides each participating cell with its actual evidence, carrying holes as absence and unresolved cells as undecided. If its semantics require a missing coordinate, it returns the declared missing-input outcome with that exact scope, not a guessed branch. Refinement can still execute its finite declared tiled subgraph after notification; its children inherit the flush generation and remaining parent allowances. Absence cannot be repaired by pretending a future host reading has arrived. Once the permitted refinement ends, publish the last valid enclosure and unresolved obligations or the proved selection, then release the original reader pins only after all continuations finish.

**Rule (Quantified decisions close only their declared domain)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-flush-quantified`

An observed-domain aggregate closes the finite actual observation domain under its question's definition and proves only that version. A fixed-window or whole-declared-domain universal claim still needs complete coverage, or a feasible counterexample for its negative; an existential claim still needs a feasible witness, or complete exclusion for its negative. Missing data that prevents the requested proof yields a scoped undecided/missing-input outcome, even if a separate observed-subdomain result can be published under a declared output. All inner physical/source quantifiers retain order and common-witness obligations; an empty physical family has its explicit domain outcome. A whole-future stream claim remains open without an independent proof. These requirements specialize (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-decision-scope`) and prevent flush from turning sample coverage into a whole-domain theorem.

**Rule (Partial flush respects shared graph dependencies)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-flush-partial`

The subgraph boundary declares which expectation edges it owns. Notification closes only those edges; it cannot seal a shared external input or producer on behalf of another consumer. A shared pending producer required by the scope remains a visible external dependency wait until it publishes or supplies an admissible terminal outcome. Shared published regions stay pinned by outside consumers even after all scoped uses release. The scope completes when its own applications, refinements and required delivery finish and its use edges release; its completion reports any surviving shared occupations and does not assert an empty global heap. A scoped flush neither invalidates unaffected live attachments nor edits their historical assumptions.

**Rule (Full completion empties the heap with caches warm)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-flush-completion`

Full completion requires no active scoped producer, executable or waiting application, outstanding borrower, delivery obligation or live numerical reader, followed by retirement of every input, result, scratch, mask, companion, layout-map and numerical prelude occupation. The heap's occupied bytes and occupations are zero; reusable exact authority and lowered templates remain within their warm bounds. Empty retained allocator capacity is reported separately from occupied heap, and any numerical payload intentionally retained makes completion false. Nonempty ownership, failed required evidence export or unfit runnable work returns an unfinished wait/stop with its reason. Completion is observed atomically against new admissions; input admitted during draining is a new activation whose live occupations prevent an empty-heap claim and whose open barriers require their own scope notification. New input after completed flush can reuse warm authority with fresh numerical imports, without reopening the retired versions.

## Host graph operations · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-host`

**Rule (Request, lend, fill and hand back)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-host-loan`

The host requests an input application against a checked question/axis/source binding and optional size hint. nrz chooses capacity/layout, reserves masks and all required companions, installs pending binding and loan edges, and returns a safe exclusive borrow or non-cloneable owned loan. An owned loan detaches exclusive numerical access to its allocation so unrelated allocations can execute; it retains nrz's capacity reservation and can only return to its original owner/generation. The host fills permitted cells and companions through safe initialization tracking. Handback first authenticates the loan's owner, occupation and generation. Foreign, stale or duplicate attempts publish no usable values and leave the rightful live loan, pending binding and reservation unchanged. For an authenticated genuine loan, nrz temporarily receives exclusive access to check layout, initialized filled coverage, native payload validity, companion targets and all fallible publication prerequisites. If payload validation or another publication prerequisite fails, no bundle is published or consumer dependency released: the error returns the same owned loan, or restores the safe exclusive borrow, to the caller for repair or cancellation, preserving the pending producer, reservation and spent costs. A consuming API cannot discard the only capability needed to cancel that live loan. Successful handback atomically transfers exclusive access to nrz and publishes the complete bundle before releasing consumer dependencies. Handback does not check containment in a predeclared sensor range or certify physical or mathematical validity. Cancellation authenticates and returns exclusive ownership without requiring a valid publishable payload, closes the input's unproduced contributions with their scoped absence outcome, and releases the loan edge before storage can retire under (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-release`), (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-input-loan`) and (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-handback`).

**Rule (Skipped, late and corrected readings)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-host-inputs`

The host can skip a reading by supplying no cell or returning it unfilled; this creates no sample or automatic future obligation. A question explicitly requiring that index retains its missing demand until satisfied or handled under its flush contract. Late data and corrections are new input operations with the host's declared original associations and assumption/supersession relations. Published versions are immutable; correction does not overwrite a prior tile or infer monotone refinement. Checked inclusion can transport valid information, while changed assumptions invalidate their actual dependent closure. nrz adds no reordering frontier, loss policy, correction model or backpressure; capacity and operation outcomes leave flow choices with the host (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-host-flow`), (`rule:nrz:lc-stream-invalidation`).

**Definition (Observable operation outcomes)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-host-outcomes`

Every request/drive/handback/flush operation exposes its graph identity and one of completed publication, pending dependency or ownership, pending resource capacity, scoped undecided/domain outcome, or typed refusal/stop. Requested and actual tile sizes, resource dimension and attempted/retained usage accompany capacity outcomes. A certified exact decision may coexist with a materialization refusal for its continuation under (`rule:nrz:decision-materialization-capacity`). These are graph states reached through binding and evidence edges, not a second cell-result authority. Access to a numerical output resolves symbol, original index, current mask and region version and retains a reader edge until released. Host pacing and consumer holding time are reported separately from engine execution and never erased from observed latency.

## Tracing, host sampling and deterministic Work · `sec:nrz:tiles-streams-observability`

**Decision (Tracing is the common observation surface)** · `dec:nrz:tiles-streams-tracing`

**Definition (Host sampling surface and report)** · `def:nrz:host-sampling-surface`

The host supplies an immutable prepared problem, a representative item and its element count, and a closure or object-safe adapter that executes one item. The adapter separates fresh iteration context/reset, the timed operation, and extraction of the operation's own Work receipt. It receives a cooperative sampling deadline and declares bounds on uninterrupted execution and on its entire mandatory finalization tail. Matrix combines adapter and sampler costs into an uninterrupted-interval bound `U` and a bound `C` on the complete finalization tail from every reachable partial state, including receipt preservation, disposal, cleanup and return-report construction. These are aggregate execution bounds under the declared host scheduling conditions; additional scheduling delay is a separate term under (`rule:nrz:host-sampling-deadline`). Wall-clock observations establish total elapsed time and overshoot, not an independent measurement of descheduling. The timed closure consumes the prepared plan and local context by borrow and returns its ordinary output or typed error. Matrix controls repetition and analysis. Prepared input may contain several representatives; sample each named representative separately and retain its shape, operation revision and workload identity. Reuse prepared plans and immutable authority; do not repeat preparation unless preparation is explicitly the host's chosen operation. Each iteration has fresh private execution/ledger owners or a lawful reset of scratch buffers. Never reset a live cumulative engine budget, refund a failed attempt, rewind graph identities or publish sampled results into the host's live graph. Output storage is reused or released after its bounded lifetime. Pending receipt preservation, output disposal and other cleanup contribute sequentially to the complete finalization bound `C`; taking the maximum of their individual bounds is insufficient. A persistent mutable cache or externally visible effect requires an explicit reset/isolation adapter; otherwise that operation is unsupported by this reusable sampling surface. The prepared problem and retained graph state remain unchanged after both warm-up and measurement, including after failure and a second sampling call.

Read the entry Work snapshot immediately before starting the timer and the exit snapshot/ordinary operation receipt immediately after stopping it, before private ledgers are destroyed. Preserve ledger scopes and existing transfer reconciliation under (`rule:nrz:tiles-streams-attribution`); never infer Work from time or from a second execution. An adapter must expose all relevant Work, including private ledgers, or declare the receipt incomplete. Existing charging and creation of ordinary operation evidence remain inside the timed operation. Additional sampler snapshotting, formatting, allocation, reset and output disposal stay outside that interval but count toward total sampling elapsed time. Expiry forbids new iterations; it does not cancel the obligation to preserve spent Work and release live iteration state. The remaining mandatory work and partial-report construction form the finalization tail bounded by `C`, including any required reset or disposal callback. If output disposal or setup belongs to the operation the host wants to plan, include it explicitly in the closure and identify that scope in the report. Timing each iteration separately permits receipt collection outside its measured interval; a sample row sums those iteration durations and records its iteration count. This adapts Criterion's group timing and makes timer/call overhead visible; it does not promise identical live timings to its harness or subtract an assumed overhead. Operations too short for useful resolution require a larger host-selected item.

A sample run may use the host's existing scoped tracing subscriber. nrz installs no global subscriber, mutates no process environment or current directory, parses no process arguments and starts no background benchmark work. Enabled tracing cost is part of the timed operation; record its configuration and compare only compatible observations. Work collection does not require a subscriber. The sampler returns structured data without console output, plots, HTML, baseline directories or any file write. An explicit host export of the nrz report is separate from sampling and never uses Criterion's private output format. Concurrent samplers have independent RNG, state and budgets and do not interfere with a host's Criterion benches.

**Rule (Cooperative total wall budget)** · `rule:nrz:host-sampling-deadline`

A monotonic deadline starts at sampler entry and covers validation, allocation, warm-up, measurement, receipt handling, reset, analysis and bounded cleanup. Warm-up doubles iteration groups only within checked count caps. From its observed per-item mean `m`, form linear sample counts `k_j = j d` for `j = 1..S`, with `d = max(1, ceil(T / (m S(S+1)/2)))` and measurement target `T`; validate every product/conversion and admit the schedule only within the declared caps. This is Criterion's linear schedule, not permission to extend the wall budget to finish it. Check the deadline before every host invocation and throughout reset, bootstrap resampling, sorting and report construction. Cap/check analysis in finite chunks and reserve storage for the complete mandatory finalization tail. At the deadline `D`, stop admitting new iterations or ordinary analysis; an already executing interval may still need to reach its next check. Once expiry is detected, perform only mandatory finalization, preserving receipts, disposing of outputs, releasing state and constructing the bounded return report, without restarting measurement or estimation. Complete estimates require the entire chosen sample schedule and bootstrap count. Do not turn a shorter selected prefix or a partly completed row into a nominal full-confidence estimate. Report actual elapsed time and any overshoot.

No Rust library can forcibly stop an arbitrary closure in its process. This surface therefore promises a cooperative budget with a composed return-time bound: after `D`, residual unchecked execution costs at most `U`, and the entire subsequent mandatory finalization tail costs at most `C`. Return is bounded by `D + U + C + delta`, where `delta` is additional scheduling delay beyond the declared execution assumptions; a known smaller residual can replace `U`. `C` bounds the sum of sequential tail costs across every reachable partial state, not the largest individual cleanup action. Checkpoints inside finalization do not erase the remaining obligations. `D` is the admission deadline, and `U + C` is the declared execution allowance for overshoot, not permission to admit another iteration. Admission is the deadline check immediately before invocation; scheduling delay between that check and execution contributes to `delta`. Unbounded descheduling makes `delta` unbounded, so these declarations cannot guarantee a hard wall-clock return time or reserve processor availability. A host needing hard termination must isolate the sampler in a process it owns; a detached worker thread is not cancellation. Noncooperative operations are outside this in-process contract. Deadline tests exercise these bounds and stop states rather than turning a noisy wall-clock threshold into an engine pin.

**Rule (Estimates, throughput and batch uncertainty)** · `rule:nrz:host-sampling-estimates`

For completed rows `(k_j, t_j)`, compute normalized times `y_j = t_j/k_j`. Retain Criterion's arithmetic mean, median, sample standard deviation, scaled MAD and Tukey labels of `y`; retain its slope `sum(k_j t_j)/sum(k_j²)` and paired bootstrap of the rows. The slope is the primary per-item estimate for the declared linear model; return the mean alongside it so the estimator is unambiguous. For each statistic use uniform resampling with replacement and the percentile interval at `50(1-c)` and `50(1+c)` percent for confidence level `c`, with linear interpolation at rank `(p/100)(R-1)` and bootstrap sample standard deviation as the standard error. Validate positive finite measured times, nonzero counts, finite intermediates and nonzero regression denominators; no NaN, infinity or invented positive timing is a usable result. Outlier classification never prunes rows.

For primary time estimate `q` seconds and interval `[a,b]`, return items/second `1/q` with inverted interval `[1/b,1/a]`; for a declared positive `e` elements/item, return elements/second `e/q` and `[e/b,e/a]`. An absent element meaning yields no element rate; a zero or unresolved time bound yields no finite reciprocal claim. The batch helper accepts a nonnegative integer `N` and returns `N q` with `[N a,N b]`, checking overflow and returning zero for `N=0`. It identifies this as uncertainty in the estimated mean total under the same warmed operation, representative population, machine, tracing and sequential execution conditions. It is not a prediction interval for one future batch, a worst-case bound or a numerical certificate. Estimation uncertainty scales with `N`, not its square root; cold preparation, input/output, contention, throttling, changing cache state and different parallelism need separately measured scopes. Bootstrap coverage is approximate and assumes representative, sufficiently stable observations; a narrow interval does not establish those assumptions. For several representative classes, the host supplies batch counts and receives separate extrapolations; marginal confidence intervals cannot silently become a joint-confidence guarantee for their sum.

**Definition (Ledger incarnations and entered interval forests)** · `def:nrz:tiles-streams-ledger-intervals`

Each ledger incarnation has a stable owner-qualified identity and nonwrapping generation for its complete observed lifetime. Distinct private, cloned or replacement ledgers have distinct identities even if their snapshots or storage addresses coincide. A span's entered interval records entry and exit snapshots on one such ledger. **Rule (Exclusive attribution subtracts same-ledger children)** · `rule:nrz:tiles-streams-attribution`

For each closed interval of (`def:nrz:tiles-streams-ledger-intervals`), inclusive Work is its exit snapshot minus its entry snapshot. Its exclusive Work subtracts only the disjoint inclusive intervals of its immediate children in that ledger's projected interval forest. Sum these exclusive deltas across a span's closed intervals for the span's exclusive receipt; descendants below an immediate child are already included in that child's subtraction. Sum exclusive classified phases and the explicit unclassified remainder to each observed ledger root delta; never clamp a negative result or hide a missing exit. One ledger has one sequential mutation owner; independent concurrent work uses distinct ledger incarnations, so unrelated elapsed overlap is not subtracted. Different-ledger Work remains separate. `nrz.work.transfer` links it to an existing parent reconciliation as a separate accounting relation, counting that debit once without making the foreign-ledger interval a subtractable child or debiting either ledger again. Failed attempts retain their spent delta; rejected charges add no invented consumption. Private or discarded ledgers must be observed before destruction and cannot disappear behind a zero caller delta.

**Example (A multi-tile window and renewed input)** · `ex:nrz:tiles-streams-lc-flush`
