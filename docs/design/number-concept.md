# Number storage, identity and display · `spec:nrz:number-concept`

## The number kinds of this engine · `sec:nrz:number-kinds`

**Principle (A quantity, its representation and its display)** · `prin:nrz:number-separation`

A number has a mathematical meaning, an owned representation and a possible display; none substitutes for the others. This concept proposes display obligations across the engine, leaving concrete types, codecs and implementation to a later design. Exact descriptors remain exact everywhere, while numerical executions agree within their tracked error rather than necessarily sharing bits (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-cas-meaning`), (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-reproducibility`), (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-reproducibility-scope`). Rendering introduces only its declared display rounding; it cannot undo rounding already performed by numerical computation or turn a stored center into an exact physical result.

**Definition (Exact descriptor numbers)** · `def:nrz:number-descriptors`

CAS owns exact graph expressions, rational constants, symbols, domains and checked relations; bindings owns their physical declarations and exact reading maps. An irrational expression or an unresolved symbolic quantity need not have a finite decimal literal or a computed value. Its exact form is the retained expression with original premises, not a decimal approximation. Units, kind, affine role, measure and count domain come from the bindings descriptor, with exact scales such as π/180 for degrees and 2π for turns (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-cas-meaning`), (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-optional-values`), (`dec:nrz:descriptor-unit-authority`). Exactness of a descriptor does not assert that a measurement has zero uncertainty.

**Definition (Floating centers and companions)** · `def:nrz:number-heap`

Matrix owns finite binary64 heap entries and their region versions, with binary32 governed by the same separation; scalar supplies numerical primitives. A finite stored float has an exact dyadic value determined by its bits, exportable as a rational or a complete terminating decimal. That exact form describes the stored coordinate only. Bindings supplies its quantity interpretation and units; error, radius and statistical companions retain their own bindings, target versions and assurance. The total radius and numerical error have distinct coverage under (`thm:nrz:descriptor-binding-kernel-enclosure`); covariance is not a deterministic enclosure. The heap remains floating storage under (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-float-heap`), (`def:nrz:descriptor-binding-companion-symbols`), (`def:nrz:descriptor-binding-source-symbols`); this concept does not introduce arbitrary-precision heap arithmetic.

**Definition (Certified enclosures)** · `def:nrz:number-enclosures`

An enclosure identifies its original target, premises and lower and upper endpoints in a common coordinate system. CAS enclosure endpoints have exact rational forms under (`def:nrz:cas-enclosures-interval`), (`rule:nrz:cas-enclosures-target`), (`req:nrz:cas-enclosures-outward`). A numerical center-radius result instead retains its finite stored center and radius and their coverage theorem; an endpoint display derives exact bounds from those representations and the checked reading map. Endpoint units match the enclosed quantity; a radius for an affine point is a difference in that measure. Neither endpoint is asserted equal to the target, and a statistical confidence interval retains its statistical interpretation rather than acquiring deterministic containment.

**Definition (Complex fibres and angles)** · `def:nrz:number-complex`

A complex value retains its CAS target, component relations and bindings reading, possibly over a component pair or a real block (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-family-representation`), (`ex:nrz:descriptor-binding-complex-block`). A fibre retains the feasible correlated set and its original parameter and branch identities, not just a chosen center. Amplitude carries the complex quantity's units and phase is a distinct angle difference; the real and imaginary coordinates require compatible quantity meanings. Exact π-measure angles remain symbolic exact expressions with checked unit conversion. Their numerical realizations and angular enclosures carry evaluation error separately (`dec:nrz:descriptor-unit-authority`), (`rule:nrz:lc-relation-time-queries`), (`rule:nrz:lc-resonance-phasor-domains`).

**Definition (Decisions and counts)** · `def:nrz:number-discrete`

A certified decision is an exact outcome with its original question, partition, premises and evidence, or an explicit unresolved outcome; its option is not a rounded number (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-certified-feedback`), (`thm:nrz:decision-reproducibility`). Physical counts retain exact whole-number values, units of the counted kind and count-domain checks. Structural extents and branch indices retain their distinct metadata roles (`def:vision:counts`), (`rule:vision:counts-and-metadata`). Exact count storage remains outside floating arenas; this concept does not settle the foundations implementation (`prin:nrz:descriptor-binding-float-heap`).

**Definition (Measurements and documentation figures)** · `def:nrz:number-measurements`

Acquisition owns raw observations and their units; analysis owns estimator outputs, revisions and source-row identity. Integer timer observations are exact as recorded, while a binary64 estimate has an exact stored dyadic form without being an exact elapsed-time law. Measurement variability, estimator uncertainty and numerical rounding remain separate (`prin:nrz:compute-statistical-basis`), (`cav:nrz:compute-reproducibility`), (`dec:nrz:compute-design-schema`). Problem-data parameters, observables and uncertainty components retain the records in (`def:nrz:problem-data-records`): source numeric strings survive exact admission; metadata uses `integer_range` or `category`, including precision-bit categories 24 and 53. Uncertainty modes are radius, standard, relative-radius, relative-standard, variance and covariance, with their target identities and separate assurance. Observable `value_kind` is `exact`, `decimal-12` or `decimal-4`; the existing decimal checks count significant digits after leading zeros, and `exact` refuses scientific notation. These checks do not establish rounding direction, equality to the executable expression or authority for an equality mark. A documentation figure is a view of an identified exact reference, bound, observation, estimate or declared hypothetical example; docgen owns its rendered text, not the truth of its quantity. Reproducible rendering requires retained sources and provenance (`prin:vision:documentation-is-reproducible`), (`[DOCGEN-sec:docgen:concepts]`).

## Storage and identity · `sec:nrz:number-identity`

**Requirement (Retain an authoritative exact form)** · `req:nrz:number-exact-form`

The producing owner retains enough information to recover the exact represented value: integer or rational data, complete finite decimal, finite-float bits with format, or a symbolic expression and its checked context. A shortest round-trip decimal can recover binary64 bits through its specified decoder, but treating that decimal itself as the exact rational value changes the value. Before decimal rounding, expand the bits exactly or operate on their dyadic rational; merely adding digits to an already rounded decimal recovers nothing. Irrational symbolic values require exact identities or certified refinement to decide rounding, with unresolved or resource-limited outcomes when the boundary cannot be decided. No unbounded evaluation or mandatory decimal materialization is implied (`rule:nrz:cas-enclosures-budgets`).

**Requirement (Link each display to its quantity)** · `req:nrz:number-display-identity`

A contracted display identifies a source statement label or dataset field, its quantity meaning and units, and the display-contract revision. Live values additionally retain the original symbol/declaration, reading map, region and companion versions needed to identify the publication. Dataset figures retain dataset/schema identity, record key, field path and producer revision or source attachment; labels locate the assertion but alone do not identify a particular measured instance. Derived figures retain input identities and the declared derivation. Exact, rounded and enclosure forms can then be checked against the same quantity without parsing a caption to guess provenance (`def:nrz:descriptor-binding-symbol-binding`), (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-invalidation`), (`def:nrz:compute-design-wire`).

**Principle (Equal text is never identity)** · `prin:nrz:number-text-not-identity`

Distinct quantities, measurements, branches and values can display the same decimal. Conversely, one quantity can have several unit conversions or notation choices. Schema equality, source identity, value identity and equality of mathematical values remain separate; checked relations authorize conversion or equality, while string comparison authorizes neither (`def:nrz:quantity-schema-key`), (`rule:nrz:descriptor-binding-checked-overlap`). A rounded zero cannot identify an exact zero, merge two observations or select a certified continuation.

## Display contracts · `sec:nrz:number-display`

**Definition (Per-value display contract)** · `def:nrz:number-contract`

A display contract declares the quantity and coordinate measure, an absolute grid or an explicitly distinct significant-figure policy, rounding mode, notation policy, zero handling and equality-marking authority. Its declared scope distinguishes a center, a bound, an estimate and an exact reference. Reusable field contracts may supply these declarations to many values, but each display retains the resolved contract. Magnitude sensitivity M is positive and has inverse quantity units; the grid spacing Δ = 1/M has the quantity's units. For affine points the contract also declares the grid origin in the chosen measure. Unit or origin conversion precedes grid rounding through a checked exact map or a certified enclosure; the same physical grid must be transformed with that map.

**Assumption (Exact grid coordinates)** · `assum:nrz:number-grid`

For the following rounding argument let x and the declared origin o be exact rational coordinates in one measure, let Δ be a positive rational spacing in that measure, and assume the required integer arithmetic completes within its resource policy. Set t = (x − o)/Δ. The default nearest mode resolves exact half ties away from zero in t; when o is zero this is the usual away-from-zero value rule. A nonzero origin makes that tie reference explicit. This display default makes every exact half step increase the magnitude of the grid coordinate t, symmetrically about the declared origin and independently of integer parity; it supports a direct quotient/remainder rule. It is not a claim of statistical unbiasedness and does not change the engine arithmetic tie mode. A contract may instead declare ties to even on the grid index k. Symbolic inputs use the boundary-decision obligation in (`req:nrz:number-exact-form`) instead of being assumed rational.

**Argument (One exact grid snap)** · `arg:nrz:number-grid-rounding`

Under (`assum:nrz:number-grid`), the default nearest rounding chooses k = sign(t) floor(|t| + 1/2); an explicitly declared ties-to-even mode instead chooses the even adjacent integer at an exact half tie. Both return q = o + kΔ. Thus |x − q| ≤ Δ/2, including exact ties, by the defining nearest-integer inequality. Lower rounding chooses k = floor(t), which gives q ≤ x because Δ is positive. Upper rounding chooses k = ceil(t), which gives q ≥ x by the same multiplication and translation. Quotients, remainders and half comparisons use integers on exact rational numerators and denominators, without binary floating arithmetic. Negative operands require mathematical floor and ceil, not truncation toward zero. These are display errors relative to x, not substitutes for its numerical or physical error.

**Rule (Digits must preserve the snapped value)** · `rule:nrz:number-digits`

For a zero-origin decimal grid with Δ = 10^(−d), nonnegative integer d gives exactly d fractional places in fixed notation; this is the restricted setting in which d = ⌈log₁₀ M⌉ describes the digits after expressing M in inverse display units. A coarser power-of-ten grid needs zero fractional places and explicit grid metadata. For a general grid the formula is insufficient: Δ = 1/4 needs two fractional places although ⌈log₁₀ 4⌉ = 1, and Δ = 1/3 has no finite exact decimal. The contract must provide enough digits to express a terminating snapped value exactly, allow an exact rational or symbolic rendering, or report an unsupported decimal contract. It must not silently round a nearest-grid result again to a different value. Choosing a terminating decimal grid is the natural finite-decimal summary specialization.

**Rule (Outward endpoints throughout rendering)** · `rule:nrz:number-outward`

For certified endpoints l ≤ y ≤ u, use lower rounding for l and upper rounding for u; the argument in (`arg:nrz:number-grid-rounding`) yields displayed L ≤ l ≤ y ≤ u ≤ U. If a separate decimal digit projection is declared for endpoints, it too uses floor on the lower endpoint and ceil on the upper endpoint, so L′ ≤ L and U ≤ U′ preserve containment. Record this additional outward projection as part of the contract rather than claiming its result is still the original grid point. Bounds derived from center-radius data include all required conversion and evaluation error before this projection. A checked decreasing derivation maps the source upper endpoint to the derived lower endpoint and the source lower endpoint to the derived upper endpoint before outward projection; reciprocal rates on a positive time interval follow this ordering. Reject a purported lower/upper pair that is out of order rather than repairing it by swapping. Never round endpoints to nearest or present a display enclosure as the exact original evidence (`def:nrz:number-enclosures`).

**Definition (Significant figures as a separate contract)** · `def:nrz:number-significant`

A significant-figure policy declares a positive count p and applies only when an absolute grid does not express the intended range. For nonzero exact x in declared coordinates, determine the integer e by 10^e ≤ |x| < 10^(e+1), then use Δ = 10^(e−p+1) with the declared rounding mode. Determine the magnitude by exact comparisons, not a floating logarithm; a carry may change the displayed exponent without another value rounding. Zero has no such exponent and uses the zero rule in (`rule:nrz:number-notation`). Bounds still obey (`rule:nrz:number-outward`). Significant figures do not measure uncertainty, and this adaptive grid must not be reported as a fixed absolute sensitivity.

**Rule (Notation follows value rounding)** · `rule:nrz:number-notation`

Fixed, scientific or both are notation choices for the already-rounded value. The contract selects one explicitly, declares thresholds evaluated on that value, or selects scientific notation only when it has strictly fewer digit characters than the fixed candidate, counting mantissa and exponent digits and retaining required zeros; ties stay fixed. Signs, separators and exponent markers are not digits. Scientific exponent normalization must preserve value and any declared precision metadata. Complex displays and zero do not switch automatically. A measured or rounded zero has no sign and retains the fractional places required by (`rule:nrz:number-digits`); for example, Δ = 0.001 in fixed notation displays zero as `0.000`. When no fractional places are required it displays as `0`, while its contract retains grid and uncertainty information; stored signed-zero bits may remain in source identity. An exact zero assertion and an estimate rounded to zero keep different equality marks (`rule:nrz:number-equality`).

**Requirement (Declare and check the contract)** · `req:nrz:number-contract-check`

Validation checks source resolution, units and origin, exact input decoding, positive spacing or valid significant count, rounding direction, snapped value, endpoint containment, notation equivalence and equality evidence. It retains the producer's full representation alongside the rendered value and contract; a pretty string alone cannot pass. Exact decision outcomes, count-domain membership, branch indices and other structural integers are never rounded to decide semantics (`def:nrz:number-discrete`). An abbreviated count display, if offered, is explicitly approximate and retains its exact count, with no use as a decision or structural input. Contract checking is a later implementation obligation, not an assertion that every existing docgen field already meets it.

## Complex and angular values · `sec:nrz:number-angular`

**Requirement (Polar display rounding)** · `req:nrz:number-polar`

For a nonzero complex point z with a certified phase chart, use z = r exp(2πiτ), r = |z| and phase τ in turns. The default principal chart is −1/2 < τ ≤ 1/2 in turns. Round r on its radial grid and τ on its declared angular grid, retaining the chart, wrapping convention and any unwrapped cycle index. Re-wrap the rounded phase into the declared chart; in the default chart replace the excluded endpoint −1/2 by +1/2. If the unwrapped rounded phase is τ̂ + n turns and wrapping adds an integer m to τ̂, replace the cycle index n by n − m, preserving that unwrapped value; the default endpoint replacement therefore decreases n by one. Angular sensitivity has inverse-turn units; one turn is exactly 2π radians, so an exact angle aπ radians corresponds to a/2 turns under the checked measure relation (`dec:nrz:descriptor-unit-authority`). Component-by-component rounding of the original z is not the complex display contract. Conversion of Cartesian input to polar form may require square roots and phase evaluation: exact integer grid snapping does not make those functions rational. Exact special values or certified enclosures must decide the bins; an unresolved phase stays unresolved (`dec:nrz:cas-trig-design-phase-charts`).

**Assumption (Rectangular presentation of a polar point)** · `assum:nrz:number-polar-spacing`

For the following presentation argument assume a single nonzero rounded polar point (r̂, τ̂), positive radial spacing Δr, angular spacing Δτ with 0 < Δτ ≤ 1/2 turn, and a certified conversion back to rectangular coordinates. Require 1/Δτ to be a positive integer and the angular grid origin to be a point in the declared chart; the grid repeats each turn, including across the seam. Radial grid points are nonnegative and separated by Δr. This is a point-display assumption, not a statement that a complex fibre is a disk or that its rectangular components are independent. If radial rounding produces zero, retain the original domain status and handle the display as zero without inventing a zero phase.

**Argument (Rectangular digits resolve both spacings)** · `arg:nrz:number-polar-spacing`

Under (`assum:nrz:number-polar-spacing`), adjacent angular grid points at radius r̂, including the seam pair, have minimum chord distance s = 2r̂ sin(πΔτ); the local tangential spacing is approximately 2πr̂Δτ for small Δτ. Set g = min(Δr, s). Every distinct polar grid point is at least g from the chosen point: a different radius gives a distance at least Δr by the reverse triangle inequality, and the same radius gives a chord at least s. Choose a positive rectangular decimal quantum h ≤ g/2, using a proved positive lower bound for s when it is not exact, without treating the small-angle approximation as an exact bound. Rectangular text derives from the already-rounded polar point and declares its additional coordinate rounding; nearest rounding of each derived coordinate contributes at most h/2 per coordinate, hence at most h/√2 in Euclidean distance. Since h/√2 ≤ g/(2√2) < g/2, the displayed point is strictly closer to the chosen polar grid point than to any other: the triangle inequality bounds each other distance below by g − h/√2 > h/√2. Thus “resolves” means preserving the unique nearest polar grid point in Euclidean distance. This final notation projection does not replace the polar decision, and its equality marker includes that additional discrepancy. Exact rectangular output, where available, avoids this projection; a bounded unresolved conversion cannot claim the stated digit contract.

**Rule (Domains and fibres survive display)** · `rule:nrz:number-phase-domain`

At proved zero the phase is undefined; a zero-containing enclosure without proof of zero or a unique chart retains its undecided outcome. Display rounding to zero does not prove either condition. Do not use polar point rounding to shrink a certified complex enclosure or a correlated LC fibre: retain its full feasible set, outward bounds, cuts and branch identity. Wrapping text at a turn boundary cannot merge distinct unwrapped solutions or choose a continuation (`rule:nrz:lc-resonance-phasor-domains`), (`rule:nrz:lc-relation-time-queries`), (`rule:nrz:lc-relation-overlap-tail`). Certified set presentation needs its own containment checks beyond the point argument in (`arg:nrz:number-polar-spacing`).

## Equality marking · `sec:nrz:number-equality`

**Rule (The evaluator owns equality)** · `rule:nrz:number-equality`

The producing evaluator or checked derivation supplies an equality status with the quantity identity and evidence. Documentation may say “=” only when that status establishes that the displayed expression is exactly the named target in the declared coordinates, including any notation projection. Otherwise a finite estimate or rounded point uses “≈”; string agreement, a no-change rounding flag or equal binary64 bits alone cannot certify equality to a physical target. An exact display of the stored center may use “=” when the subject is explicitly that center, without transferring the assertion to the represented quantity. Enclosures use containment or inequalities, and unavailable or undecided values retain their outcome text rather than an approximate numeric placeholder. Exact symbolic identities may use “=” without any decimal evaluation (`req:nrz:number-exact-form`), (`prin:nrz:number-text-not-identity`).

## Where it applies first · `sec:nrz:number-applications`

**Requirement (Documentation datasets and tables)** · `req:nrz:number-docgen`

Apply the concept first to nrz-docgen dataset fields, derived table cells and labelled numerical figures: retain exact input representations, source keys, units, contracts and equality statuses through deterministic derivation and rendering. Problem-data fields include parameters, observables and uncertainty components under (`def:nrz:problem-data-records`), with the existing `value_kind` checks distinguished from the fuller display obligations in (`def:nrz:number-measurements`), (`open:nrz:number-docgen-check`). A hypothetical example identifies its assumptions; a measurement identifies its retained observations. Templates select presentation while the producing derivation owns quantity truth. Rendered tables cite labelled definitions for their meanings and preserve provenance under (`prin:vision:documentation-is-reproducible`), (`[DOCGEN-sec:docgen:concepts]`). The later design must specify mechanical checking of figures and the docgen fixed point (`open:nrz:number-docgen-check`).

**Requirement (Compute rendered display contracts)** · `req:nrz:number-compute`

The number design must specify contracts for compute’s rendered display context under (`dec:nrz:compute-design-pages`), (`rule:nrz:compute-design-rendering`). If it selects terminating decimal grids for scalar summaries, those displays must satisfy (`def:nrz:number-contract`), (`rule:nrz:number-digits`), (`rule:nrz:number-outward`); the compute design does not already specify such grids. Preserve raw integer observations and the exact stored analysis values behind each summary, with timer units, numerator identity, estimator revision and statistical interpretation. Reanalysis and rendering of captured observations remain distinct from live acquisition (`dec:nrz:compute-design-schema`), (`def:nrz:compute-design-wire`), (`rule:nrz:compute-design-rendering`). This concept does not change the acquisition codec or infer deterministic timing from exact display arithmetic; outward projection contains the unprojected statistical interval and makes no new coverage claim. Bootstrap standard errors and confidence intervals retain their distinct estimator meanings under (`def:nrz:compute-design-results`).

**Requirement (LC outputs and register evidence)** · `req:nrz:number-lc`

LC amplitude, phase, element and inverse-time outputs attach displays to their original bindings, units, branch or fibre and companions. Register evidence retains exact decision outcomes and source observations alongside any displayed bounds or measured ratios. Formatting cannot turn component evidence into integrated acceptance, change a resource count or decide a measured threshold from rounded text (`req:nrz:lc-integrated-evidence-register`), (`rule:nrz:lc-closing-completion`), (`req:nrz:lc-overhead-acceptance-protocol`). The domain distinctions of (`rule:nrz:number-phase-domain`) apply even when rounded output appears identical.

**Caveat (Debug and trace text is outside the contract)** · `cav:nrz:number-debug`

Debug and trace renderings are diagnostic views with no display-contract, equality or stable-identity authority. They may expose implementation-oriented numeric formatting, but cannot serve as published quantity evidence or be parsed to reconstruct identity. Promoting a diagnostic figure into documentation requires its authoritative source and an explicit checked display contract under (`req:nrz:number-display-identity`), (`req:nrz:number-contract-check`); the presence of a number in a trace does not establish one.

## Open questions for the number design · `sec:nrz:number-open`

**Open Question (Shared type or owner-specific contracts)** · `open:nrz:number-contract-owner`

Should one shared display-contract type serve CAS, bindings, numerical owners, compute and docgen, or should each owner expose its own contract with a common checked interchange meaning? Specify revisioning, ownership, bounded exact arithmetic and refusal outcomes without moving physical meaning into scalar or heap storage. Resolve the API and wire representation after the separation in (`prin:nrz:number-separation`).

**Open Question (Checking rendered figures)** · `open:nrz:number-docgen-check`

How does docgen bind every contracted rendered figure to its exact quantity and detect stale, hand-edited or mislabelled text? Specify field identifiers, derivation/equality evidence, template integration, unit conversion, label resolution and checks for both notation forms, while preserving deterministic datasets and rendering. Decide how `value_kind` values `decimal-12` and `decimal-4` become significant-figure contracts with rounding, zero and carry policies, and whether `exact` grants “=” only when accompanied by the target-equality evidence required by (`rule:nrz:number-equality`). The existing digit-count and numeric-syntax checks in (`def:nrz:number-measurements`) alone supply no such evidence. Establish independent boundary examples for signed ties, general grids, outward endpoints, carry transitions and zero without using the formatter as its own oracle.

**Open Question (Display grid versus tracked error)** · `open:nrz:number-error-grid`

May a grid be finer than the value's own error radius, and what presentation then prevents those digits from implying unsupported accuracy? The design must distinguish exact stored-center inspection from a claimed physical precision, numerical error from input uncertainty, and absolute from relative policies near zero. Decide whether to refuse, annotate or coarsen such displays, how any coarsening becomes an explicit contract, and whether to publish a combined display/evaluation bound. No choice may shrink the underlying enclosure or silently reinterpret covariance as deterministic coverage (`def:nrz:number-heap`), (`rule:nrz:number-outward`).

**Open Question (Certified polar conversion and set displays)** · `open:nrz:number-polar-certification`

Specify bounded phase and modulus refinement, exact tie proofs, branch-cut wrapping, zero outcomes, coordinate-projection budgets and the representation of correlated fibres or wrapped interval unions. Decide which angular grids are admitted and how unsupported exact comparisons are reported. The point-display arguments in (`arg:nrz:number-polar-spacing`) and (`req:nrz:number-polar`) do not supply a general certified set formatter or phase evaluator.
