# Descriptor heaps, computation plans and batches · `spec:nrz:descriptor-heap-computation`

## Contract and ownership · `sec:nrz:computation-contract-and-ownership`

This specification defines the storage and binding contracts for computations over checked quantities. Logical matrix maps, entry order and independently shaped companions remain owned by the shape specification (`spec:nrz:matrix-shapes`); units, kinds, measures, affine roles and assurance remain governed by (`req:vision:algebraic-unit-checks`), (`def:vision:intrinsic-uncertainty-companions`). The heap makes those contracts explicit at the boundary of plain numerical storage. It specifies no hardware target, residency or hardware alignment policy, and neither its correctness nor its ordering depends on parallel execution. These are implementation obligations, not capabilities of the provisional dense solver (`dec:nrz:computation-model-implementation-boundary`).

**Definition (Scalar arena)** · `def:nrz:scalar-arena`

A scalar arena owns contiguous storage of one supported backend scalar type, with an arena identity, generation, length and allocation lifetime. A region is a checked range of scalar elements in that arena; offsets and strides count elements of its declared type, not bytes or physical counts. Distinct precision types use distinct arenas. Scalar primitives remain dimension neutral and matrix leaves contain plain backend scalars (`dec:vision:storage-and-scalar-wrapping`), (`dec:vision:precision-selection`). Discrete physical counts require their separately checked representation and cannot enter a floating arena by reinterpretation (`def:vision:counts`). Descriptor metadata and source identities are outside these numerical buffers.

**Definition (Heap descriptor)** · `def:nrz:heap-descriptor`

A heap descriptor is the matrix owner's checked record for a value or a companion. It associates a versioned value identity with a region and its interpretation. Immutable shape, quantity and coordinate schemas can be shared across descriptors; a schema is not a value identity. A descriptor can describe a homogeneous leaf, a checked block assembly of leaves, or a view with an explicit logical entry map. A heterogeneous operator reaches a scalar kernel through its recorded normalization, without pretending that its physical entries have one common unit (`def:nrz:shape-normalization`), (`rule:vision:heterogeneous-assembly`).

**Table (Descriptor contents)** · `tab:nrz:heap-descriptor-contents`

|Part|Required content|Reason|
|----|----------------|------|
|Identity|Heap identity, slot generation and independent value identity/version|Distinguish slot reuse, mutation and equal-shaped values.|
|Storage|Arena identity/generation, region offset and length, scalar type/precision, layout and element strides or an explicit checked entry map|Resolve safe views without reinterpretation or unchecked indexing.|
|Meaning|References to checked shape and quantity schemas, ordered axes, measure/origin maps, normalization scales and structure evidence with its scope|Interpret the same stored scalar only under its declared physical contract.|
|Companions|References to independently described measurement, computational and other admitted companions, their targets and versions, source dependencies, assurance and coverage|Keep bounds, covariance and value identities attached through operations.|
|Provenance|Binding and operation identity, declaration versions and transformations needed to interpret the result|Make the evidence and invalidation dependencies inspectable.|

The shape and logical entry order are those of (`def:nrz:matrix-shape`), (`conv:nrz:shape-entry-order`), independent of physical layout. Companion descriptors follow (`def:nrz:companion-shape`), (`def:nrz:covariance-shape`), (`def:nrz:numerical-error-shapes`): each has its own storage, shape and quantity meaning, and names every target value version. A scalar norm radius, an entrywise bound and a covariance cannot be substituted for one another. Cross-value covariance names both targets and its source declarations; shared schemas or overlapping views never establish independence. Companion dependencies form an inspectable relation, not implicit ownership cycles; the heap owns records and validates their targets.

**Definition (Descriptor reference)** · `def:nrz:descriptor-reference`

A public descriptor reference identifies a heap, slot and generation. Resolving it checks that the heap and occupied generation still exist; a reference from another heap or an earlier occupation is refused. A value version is separate: mutation within a surviving slot must invalidate evidence even when the reference remains resolvable. Arena references have an analogous generation check, so reusing numerical storage cannot revive an old descriptor. Generation and value-version counters never wrap into a previously valid identity; an exhausted slot or identity is retired and replaced. These lifetime rules are engine choices, not claims about a graphics API.

**Decision (Heap ownership and sharing)** · `dec:nrz:heap-ownership-and-sharing`

The matrix layer owns arenas, descriptors, safe numerical views and evidence invalidation. Physics owns physical and computational petgraph values and uses public matrix handles and operation contracts (`rule:vision:backend-boundaries`), (`dec:vision:physical-and-computational-graphs`). Bindings owns quantity checking without depending on the heap; algebra remains a generic expression core. A computation plan and immutable checked schemas can be shared. Each binding owns its slot assignments, value versions, declared source relationships, selected conversions, outputs and operation records; explicitly shared constants retain the same value identity instead of being copied into apparently independent inputs.

During evaluation, input descriptors, declaration snapshots and evidence dependencies are frozen. Resolving a handle grants only a safe borrow whose lifetime is bounded by the owner; handles never contain persistent Rust references into a growable arena. Arena growth, compaction, descriptor replacement and mutation cannot occur through a conflicting live borrow. An implementation may relocate storage between evaluations only by updating validated indirection and invalidating stale physical views and caches. Immutable schemas may be interned, but equality never merges physical sources or values. Read-only overlapping input views are permitted; writable access follows (`rule:nrz:descriptor-layout-validation`). The metadata/buffer separation takes its motivation from (`ex:exemplars:direct3d12-descriptor-heaps`), (`ex:exemplars:apache-arrow-columnar-format`), while ownership and validity follow the engine's evidence contract.

## Layout and lifetime · `sec:nrz:computation-layout-and-lifetime`

**Rule (Descriptor layout validation)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-layout-validation`

Before constructing a numerical view, validate the descriptor and arena generations, precision agreement, region bounds and logical-to-physical mapping. For an affine layout the element address is `offset + sum(k_d * stride_d)` over the declared extents; validate every product, sum, extreme address and length conversion without integer overflow. Signed strides require checked signed arithmetic and both lower and upper bounds. Zero extents admit no entry access and remain subject to each operation's empty-input contract. An explicit structured map has equivalent range obligations and preserves the logical expansion required by (`rule:nrz:structured-companion-coordinates`). A view of a view composes checked maps rather than assuming contiguity.

Read-only broadcasts or repeated indices may alias. An output mapping must be injective over its written entries and disjoint from other simultaneous writes and live inputs, unless the operation explicitly admits an in-place transformation with a proved access contract. If safe borrowing or non-overlap cannot be established, use separately owned scratch storage and a checked copy-back, or refuse; no unsafe cast or unchecked alias assumption is available. A backend adapter receives compatible safe slices or backend views only after validation and can pack a strided input when needed. Layout compatibility proves access safety, not dimensional compatibility, a covariance model or a numerical certificate.

**Rule (Descriptor lifetime and mutation)** · `rule:nrz:descriptor-lifetime-and-mutation`

A value change advances its version and transforms or invalidates every dependent companion, structural claim, binding check and cached result. Changing a declaration, measure/origin map, normalization, source model or assurance has the same dependency obligation even if center bytes do not change. Shared aliases name the underlying value version and entry map; mutation through any alias invalidates all affected evidence, conservatively the entire overlapping value when finer coverage is unavailable. A copied center is a new value with explicitly preserved source dependence, not a fresh independent measurement. A view or permutation carries the transformed target and source maps; retiring a descriptor invalidates references to that occupation.

Precision conversion produces a new typed representation and accounts for conversion error under (`rule:vision:precision-conversions`); it does not change an arena's interpretation in place. Measure conversion transforms centers and each companion by its own rule, preserving affine origins and difference measures (`rule:vision:measure-conversions`). Raw mutable access consumes the checked evidence or returns through a revalidation boundary. Read-only raw-center access identifies the owner and the evidence it does not itself carry. A failed mutation or validation cannot publish a partially checked descriptor as a valid result.

## Plans and bindings · `sec:nrz:computation-plans-and-bindings`

**Definition (Computation plan)** · `def:nrz:computation-plan`

A computation plan is an immutable operation graph with stable node identities, named input/output slots and checked slot schemas. Edges reference slots, not copied numerical values or borrowed arena addresses. Each operation declares its input and output contracts, original expressions where applicable, supported domain and uncertainty modes, shape relations, policy, dependencies and any admitted structural alternatives. An input slot may be shared by several nodes and several bindings. A slot reference within the plan resolves through a binding to a heap descriptor reference; it is not interchangeable with a heap reference.

**Rule (Declared plan variants and feedback)** · `rule:nrz:plan-declared-variants`

The physical graph describes relations, while this graph describes their computation (`dec:vision:physical-and-computational-graphs`). Coupled constraints become an explicit solve or an admitted iterative node with a stopping and failure contract; they do not become an accidental dependency cycle. Declared branch variants have stable node identities and interfaces. A parameterized assembly can derive extents from declared structural parameters and checked shape constraints. Arbitrary new operations, undeclared symbols or a different physical model require a new plan. A graph whose structure depends on a numerical decision declares exact CAS option regions/ranges, downstream templates and an undecided continuation before computation (`def:nrz:decision-graph-case`). Unbounded and parametric alternatives use explicitly bound finite schemas and checked range/uniqueness premises, never an undeclared arm created by an integer result (`rule:nrz:decision-option-ranges`). Each feedback edge enters a later lowering stratum with no path to its own inputs (`rule:nrz:decision-stratification`). The separation of model and state takes from (`ex:exemplars:drake`), and explicit assembly from (`ex:exemplars:modelica`), without importing either runtime surface.

**Definition (Plan binding)** · `def:nrz:plan-binding`

A plan binding maps all required input, output and workspace slots to concrete heap descriptor references, declaration snapshots and value versions. It supplies the selected structural variant or shape parameters, compatible coordinate conversions, source declarations and the policy choices permitted by the plan. Outputs may begin as reserved regions with checked schemas but are unavailable as values until their producing operation publishes a successful version. A binding record retains the original symbol-to-slot association and diagnostics; resolving an algebraic name by matching text alone cannot authorize an undeclared input. The binding's identity remains distinct from every value and source identity. A decision-dependent choice remains pending until a live certified membership selects a declared instance, or its undecided continuation applies. Exact-only descriptors and selected result schemas retain the storage-neutral binding contract (`def:nrz:descriptor-binding-symbol-binding`); a pending output is not an exact value without storage.

**Rule (Plan validation)** · `rule:nrz:plan-validation`

Plan construction checks every original expression and operation before any normalization, including operands under zeros, canceled terms and inactive branch alternatives (`rule:vision:check-before-normalization`). It validates symbols, constants, functions, dimensions, kinds, affine roles, dependencies and typed zeros through the bindings checker, and ordered matrix and companion relationships through the shape contract (`req:vision:expression-and-symbol-contract`), (`rule:nrz:shape-data-time-evidence`). It rejects undeclared symbols, incompatible interfaces, unsupported operations or uncertainty contracts, illegal dependency cycles and incoherent branch outputs. Generic shape relations remain explicit obligations to discharge when concrete extents are bound; plan checking cannot assume them true for every parameter value.

The plan retains immutable checked declarations, original operations and diagnostic paths alongside any optional normal form. Rewriting or substituting an expression requires checking the resulting original tree at the client boundary; algebra does not acquire physical or heap semantics. Plan checks can be reused only against the declaration and schema versions for which they hold. A scalar block-entry check alone is insufficient for matrix composition, source pairing or covariance contraction (`rule:nrz:shape-entry-contract`). Plan validity establishes semantic admissibility; value-dependent numerical domains, nonsingularity, positive definiteness, finite enclosures and budget acceptance remain operation obligations.

**Rule (Binding validation)** · `rule:nrz:binding-validation`

Before evaluation, the binding validator resolves handles, checks generations and value/declaration versions, discharges shape parameters and variant constraints, validates storage mappings and writable aliasing, and checks actual quantity schemas and conversions against slot contracts. It validates the target identities, versions, extents and source declarations of companions, including batch-wide consistency of shared sources. It rejects unsupported uncertainty modes explicitly and never interprets a missing uncertainty declaration as physical exactness. Frozen compatible descriptor/declaration blocks can be checked once and their evidence referenced by all corresponding rows; different versions or contracts require fresh checks.

The table is checked as a family of bindings, without repeating units and kind algebra for each stored scalar. This does not waive required numerical scans, finiteness checks, discrete-domain checks or value-dependent checks before a kernel or certificate. Per-entry quantity checks remain necessary when a newly assembled heterogeneous schema contains distinct entries. Diagnostics identify the batch point, plan node or slot, descriptor reference, original expression location where available, and expected and actual contracts. A shared table schema error prevents evaluation of that table; a bad reference or declaration in one row yields a point refusal and blocks its dependent nodes without discarding valid rows (`rule:nrz:batch-outcomes`).

**Rule (Computation cache validity)** · `rule:nrz:computation-cache-validity`

Every cached artifact records the dependencies of the claim it makes. A factor candidate depends on the operator identity/version, precision, shape, actual coordinate maps, structure scope, algorithm and relevant policy. An inverse witness and its bound additionally retain their validation evidence and coordinate norm. A changed right-hand side can reuse an eligible operator factor, but requires its own candidate, residual, perturbation calculation and budget decision (`def:vision:certified-solve-contract`), (`rule:vision:decomposition-objects`). A previous point's solve certificate never becomes the next point's certificate merely because the factor is reusable.

**Rule (Complete result-cache dependencies)** · `rule:nrz:computation-result-cache`

A whole-result cache also depends on all actual input versions, symbol/declaration snapshots, measures and origins, normalization scales, source/cross-covariance dependencies, companions and their assurance, requested observables and budgets, and recovery policy. Evidence changes invalidate the affected record even when the center is unchanged. A selected continuation additionally retains the decision attachment, exact partition/option parameters, proof context, policy and all enclosure-companion versions; the numerical-to-descriptor dependency closure is mandatory (`rule:nrz:decision-invalidation`). Cache reuse records the originating artifact and its validated dependencies in the new operation record. Shape equality, pointer equality, a reused batch ordinal or an unchanged slot generation alone cannot establish any of these cache keys. These invalidation choices follow the engine's contracts and are not attributed to the external design exemplars.

**Definition (Operation record)** · `def:nrz:operation-record`

Each operation record identifies its plan, binding, point and node, resolved input value versions, output descriptors if published, actual precision, algorithms, coordinate transformations and recovery attempts. A successful numerical record references its outcome and intrinsic companions, with residual, conditioning, bound and budget evidence appropriate to that operation (`def:vision:operation-result-and-failure`). For a solve it follows (`def:vision:certified-solve-contract`), (`rule:nrz:shape-certified-solve`); a factor reconstruction or solve certificate cannot certify a different operation. Every claim identifies covered uncertainty components and assumptions, and distinguishes deterministic bounds from statistical propagation.

A record is successful, refused at binding, numerically failed, blocked by a named dependency, or inactive in the selected branch. Numerical failure retains available diagnostic evidence without publishing a successful result; a certified bound that exceeds the budget can remain a bound in the failure record without becoming budget success. A node that has not executed has no invented numerical outcome. A valid decision operation records its decided option or named undecided reason, evidence and attempts (`def:nrz:decision-node`). Undecided is a declared control outcome, distinct from a proved mathematical domain failure and from a binding refusal; its consumers follow their declared continuation. Invalidated selected outputs are unavailable for current execution while historical records retain their original snapshots. An operation's refused deterministic guarantee does not erase independently valid statistical evidence. The point record names all requested outputs and their states; downstream nodes consume only outputs admitted by their contract, never a failed candidate by accident.

## Batches, sources and replay · `sec:nrz:computation-batches-and-replay`

**Definition (Computation batch)** · `def:nrz:computation-batch`

A batch is an ordered family of bindings of one plan. A binding table holds a stable point key and ordinal per row, shared constant descriptors, and columns for varying slot assignments, structural parameters and declared choices. Equal schemas may be stored once; values can occupy separate regions or checked slices of shared arenas. A batch can express a Cartesian sweep, sampled design, explicit case list or structural family admitted by the graph. Its batch index is organizational metadata independent of every physical matrix axis and scalar quantity (`dec:nrz:shape-descriptor-ownership`). Representing points together never asserts statistical independence.

**Definition (Batch axis)** · `def:nrz:batch-axis`

A batch axis names a varying declaration or selection and records its role, ordered levels or realized samples, and the mapping from each point to its coordinate on that axis. A quantity axis identifies a plan input and its quantity schema, measure/origin and uncertainty declaration; a structural or categorical axis identifies an explicitly permitted parameter or variant and is not a physical count. Sample coordinates can be a table with no Cartesian product; repeated levels and repeated bindings are legitimate distinct points. The binding table records the actual row order and point-to-axis map, so an axis description never guesses missing combinations. Experimental-design vocabulary motivates these representations (`ex:exemplars:box-hunter-experimental-design`); it supplies neither a solver nor an independence assumption.

**Rule (Batch variation)** · `rule:nrz:batch-variation`

Values, compatible declared measures, source/uncertainty declarations within supported modes, budgets within the allowed policy, and graph-admitted structural parameters may vary by point. Concrete schemas and conversion maps are checked per distinct version. A shared source must have one consistent declaration/version and explicit point-dependent transformations; changing its variance under the same identity without a joint model is a contradiction, not point-local freedom. Distinct sources with unknown correlation require that uncertainty to remain unknown. New dimensions, kinds or affine roles outside a slot schema, undeclared functions or operations, an unsupported assurance mode, or an unplanned precision reinterpretation are refused.

The plan's operation contracts, symbol interfaces, dependency rules and admissible policy/variant set remain fixed. Structural variation is permitted only where a declared parameterized assembly or variant determines valid extents and operations; ragged regions then have per-point extents and checked offsets, and outputs preserve their own schemas. Branches expose compatible declared interfaces or an explicit tagged result whose consumers handle each admitted alternative. Flattening an incompatible family into one untyped rectangle is forbidden. A change outside these contracts requires another plan. Precision selection chooses a complete typed computation, and an admitted retry records its distinct representation and evidence (`dec:vision:precision-selection`), (`rule:vision:precision-conversions`).

**Rule (Batch outcomes)** · `rule:nrz:batch-outcomes`

Evaluation retains a row for every input point and a record for every applicable plan node in stable order. A point's binding refusal or numerical failure blocks only its dependents; independent nodes and valid other points remain evaluable. Inactive alternatives are recorded as inactive. A shared invalid input affects each actual dependent point and names that common cause; a malformed global table or joint-source declaration can refuse the family because no consistent binding interpretation exists. No fail-fast collection may hide already available successes or suppress the required failure states. Decision nodes retain the complete per-point outcome table and partition active execution into compatible strata, including an undecided bucket with no asserted mathematical membership (`def:nrz:decision-outcome-table`), (`rule:nrz:decision-batch-strata`). Shared lowered regions require the full dependency key; equal option labels cannot transfer another point's certificate. Cross-point aggregate nodes explicitly depend on their selected inputs and refuse or report an explicitly requested partial aggregate when any required input is unavailable.

**Rule (Batch subset identities and aggregation)** · `rule:nrz:batch-subset-identities`

Successful subsets preserve the original point keys and row ordinals. Companion targets and covariance submatrices use explicit selection maps; dropping a failed point never silently renumbers the remaining source relationships or fills an unavailable block with zero. Optional aggregation states its membership, weights, source model, uncertainty coverage and missing-point policy. Repeated runs of the same measured source do not acquire independent error merely because their records have distinct point identifiers.

**Rule (Batch source covariance)** · `rule:nrz:batch-source-covariance`

For admitted covariance propagation, the family retains a joint relation over point and logical-entry identities. It can be represented densely, as explicitly identified blocks, or by checked sensitivity/source factors with a declared source covariance, provided all represented cross-point entries can be recovered with their targets and dimensions. Per-value logical entry order follows (`conv:nrz:shape-entry-order`); the batch specifies the separate point-to-entry product map, including ragged segment offsets where required. Covariance itself remains the tensor contract of (`def:nrz:covariance-shape`), with propagation and provenance under (`rule:nrz:shape-sensitivity-propagation`), rather than an ordinary matrix operator with quotient units.

**Rule (Shared-source cross-point covariance)** · `rule:nrz:batch-shared-source-covariance`

If `y_p = f_p(s)`, retained sensitivities to a common source vector give cross blocks `U_pq = J_p U_s J_qᵀ` under the propagation assumptions. This is exact for an affine map with declared finite second moments; a nonlinear linearization retains its approximation and coverage limits. Source identifiers, coordinate transformations, joint covariance and versions remain available across every point. Zero cross blocks require a declared and justified independence or zero-covariance model; absence, unsupported propagation and missing data remain explicit. Reusing one center with a common source is correlated even if different descriptors own the copies. The statistical contract follows (`ex:exemplars:jcgm-100-gum`), (`ex:exemplars:jcgm-102-multivariate-uncertainty`); no covariance supplies a deterministic enclosure on an unbounded input by itself.

**Convention (Batch order and replay)** · `conv:nrz:batch-order-and-replay`

The plan records a deterministic node order with a stable tie-break for independent nodes, and the batch records its exact point order. Results, diagnostics, branch states and recovery attempts follow these identities rather than map iteration, allocation addresses or completion timing. Engine-controlled cross-point reductions use a declared fixed input order and the selected validated arithmetic; a mathematically permutation-invariant formula alone does not guarantee floating-point replay. These requirements specify an observable order without choosing a work scheduler.

Replay records plan/declaration versions, actual input representations and precision, coordinate maps, source model, policies, structural choices and the arithmetic/backend environment. A sampled design stores the realized samples or pins the generator algorithm/version, seed and point-key mapping needed to regenerate them; a seed alone is insufficient. Reusing the same source in a simulation and generating independent random draws are different declared experiments. Deterministic ordering does not promise bitwise identity across different backend or compiler environments, nor does it strengthen their numerical guarantees (`dec:vision:reductions-and-residuals`), (`dec:vision:independent-verification-arithmetic`). The engine chooses these order and replay rules independently of its exemplars.

## Worked shared-source resistor batch · `sec:nrz:computation-resistor-batch`

**Assumption (Resistor batch source and exact gains)** · `assum:nrz:resistor-batch-source`

The unchanged resistor-network reference supplies the six exact one-ohm resistors, node order and source-current sign convention (`prob:exemplars:resistor-network`). Use the illustrative shared source `s0` from (`assum:nrz:resistor-source-uncertainty`), with mean `1 V`, standard uncertainty `1/100 V` and finite second moments. The three bindings use exact dimensionless gains `a = (1, 2, 3)` and sources `s_p = a_p s0`. These are transformations of one source, not three independent voltage measurements. The gains and their exactness are this example's additional scenario; the problem data is unchanged. There is no deterministic physical radius for `s0` in this assumption.

**Example (Resistor source batch)** · `ex:nrz:resistor-source-batch`

Let the physical solution be `x = (v0, v1, v2, v3, iV)ᵀ` with domain coordinates `(V, V, V, V, A)` and equation coordinates `(A, A, A, A, V)`, under the established shape example (`ex:nrz:resistor-shape-covariance`). The physical operator has a `4 × 4` conductance block in siemens, a `4 × 1` dimensionless incidence block, a `1 × 4` dimensionless source constraint block and a typed zero `1 × 1` resistance block in ohms. Both nonzero and zero entries obey their row-over-column descriptors. Normalize variable coordinates with scales `(1 V, 1 V, 1 V, 1 V, 1 A)` and equations with `(1 A, 1 A, 1 A, 1 A, 1 V)`; the resulting dimensionless operator and source coordinates are `M = [[1,-1,0,0,1],[-1,3,-1,0,0],[0,-1,3,-1,0],[0,0,-1,2,0],[1,0,0,0,0]]` and `b_p = (0,0,0,0,s_p/(1 V))ᵀ`.

A possible heap layout uses an immutable normalized operator region of 25 elements, a source-center element and a separate variance element, and separate center arenas or disjoint regions for gains, right-hand sides and outputs. All numerical regions have explicitly selected precision; the exact rational values below are mathematical oracles, not a claim that one backend scalar type stores every rational exactly. Physical block schemas, normalization maps and source definitions are immutable shared metadata. The scalar variance descriptor has squared voltage dimension and a statistical kind; it is neither a voltage nor a numerical radius.

**Table (Resistor batch descriptors)** · `tab:nrz:resistor-batch-descriptors`

|Slot or relation|Logical contract|Example region or sharing|
|----------------|----------------|-------------------------|
|Physical operator blocks|The four quotient blocks and typed zeros above, with one fixed source-free operator identity|Shared checked schemas and normalized operator mapping for every point.|
|Normalized operator|Dimensionless `5 × 5` map in declared coordinates|25 plain scalars, row-major element offset `5*i + j`; logical vectorization uses `i + 5*j`.|
|Source and variance|Scalar voltage difference source and scalar voltage-squared variance with one source identity/version|One center region and one companion region reused by all bindings.|
|Gain|Scalar exact ratio, distinct from a run index|Three ordered center entries for `(1,2,3)`; no uncertain gain source.|
|Right-hand side|Per-point `5 × 1` equation vector and its source sensitivity|Point `p` has a disjoint five-element center region at offset `5*p`; covariance comes from the same source.|
|Solution|Per-point `5 × 1` variable vector, normalized center and physical restoration map|Point `p` has its own output region and value version, with associated operation record.|
|Sensitivity|Per-point `5 × 1` map from the one voltage source to the output differences|Shared base sensitivity schema and exact gain transformation; the current component has units `A/V`.|
|Within-point covariance|`5 × 5` pairings of output difference entries|Own target version and mixed `V²`, `V A` and `A²` descriptors, recoverable from the source factor.|
|Joint covariance|`15 × 15` relation over three points and five entries|Explicit point-major map `k = 5*p + i`, or a `15 × 1` sensitivity factor and scalar source variance.|
|Numerical solve bound|Scalar normwise radius in normalized coordinates with its restoration map|Distinct per-operation descriptor, supplied only by an actual certificate.|

Here `p = 0, 1, 2` is the ordinal for gains `1, 2, 3`, and `i, j = 0, ..., 4`. The point-major product map orders the joint covariance; it does not replace the column vectorization of the physical operator. A joint covariance descriptor names all three output versions and their common source version. An implementation need not materialize 225 scalars when the source factor represents exactly the same relation, but it cannot retain just three diagonal blocks and claim full coverage.

The plan imports the checked exact resistor model and source declaration, forms `s_p = a_p s0` and the normalized right-hand side, solves with the shared operator, restores physical outputs, propagates the common-source covariance and optionally forms the mean of the three outputs. Each stage has a stable node and output slot. An operator factor or inverse witness can be shared under (`rule:nrz:computation-cache-validity`); the solve candidate, residual and bound belong to the actual right-hand side and point. The common-source factor for the aggregate is a declared cross-point dependency, not an extra physical matrix column disguised as a batch axis.

Plan checking establishes the typed block zeros and quotient dimensions, gain multiplication, shape relations, original expressions, normalization and permitted statistical/conditional numerical coverage once for the immutable schemas. Binding checking validates each point's gain region, source identity/version, source transformation, slots, extents, precision, mappings, companion targets and actual conversions. Shared-source consistency is checked for the whole table. Actual numerical solves still check finite arithmetic, inverse conditions, perturbation margins and their own budgets. Standard uncertainty is never used as `b_radius` in a deterministic physical-input certificate. A numerical certificate may cover the separately declared represented center system, with conversion and rounding error, while the statistical relation covers the random physical source; their assumptions remain separate.

For the exact physical model the response is `x_p = a_p s0 J`, where `J = (1, 5/13, 2/13, 1/13, -8/(13 Ω))ᵀ`. Equivalently, in normalized coordinates with source coordinate one, `w = (1, 5/13, 2/13, 1/13, -8/13)ᵀ` satisfies `M w = (0,0,0,0,1)ᵀ`. Thus the mean-source solutions and all row residuals can be verified with rational arithmetic.

**Table (Exact per-point means)** · `tab:nrz:resistor-batch-means`

|Gain|v0 in V|v1 in V|v2 in V|v3 in V|iV in A|
|----|-------|-------|-------|-------|-------|
|1|1|5/13|2/13|1/13|−8/13|
|2|2|10/13|4/13|2/13|−16/13|
|3|3|15/13|6/13|3/13|−24/13|

The shared variance is `u²(s0) = 1/10000 V²`. Write `U0 = J u²(s0) Jᵀ`; the cross-point block is exactly `U_pq = a_p a_q U0`. The block multipliers in point order are `[[1,2,3],[2,4,6],[3,6,9]]`. The full normalized covariance is a positive semidefinite rank-one outer product: with `z = (wᵀ, 2*wᵀ, 3*wᵀ)ᵀ`, it is `z zᵀ/10000`. Restoring physical coordinates yields the declared mixed entry dimensions, without requiring an inverse covariance or pretending it is positive definite.

For example, `cov(v1 at gain 1, iV at gain 3) = 1*3*(5/13)*(-8/13)/10000 V A = -3/42250 V A`. The mean output of the three bindings is `2 s0 J`, with covariance `4 U0`. Keeping only within-point covariance would instead give `(1 + 4 + 9)/9 U0 = (14/9) U0`, an unjustified change of the source model. No Gaussian assumption is needed for these affine finite-second-moment identities. They establish no deterministic bound on the random source and no floating-point solve certificate; those require their separate evidence.

An illustrative invalid-binding variant changes only the gain-2 row to reference a retired source descriptor generation. Its binding is refused with the stale reference identified, its dependent solve and propagation nodes are blocked, and it has no numerical outcome. Valid gain-1 and gain-3 rows remain evaluable with their original ordinals and common-source relationship; any retained covariance subset explicitly selects those entries. The three-point mean is blocked because a required input is absent. This is a proposed failure representation, not an observed runtime result or a change to the exact three-point example above.

## Implementation evidence · `sec:nrz:computation-implementation-evidence`

**Requirement (Computation implementation evidence)** · `req:nrz:computation-implementation-evidence`

Implementation must demonstrate safe lifetime and access checks for foreign/stale handles, slot and arena reuse, exhausted versions, relocation, signed and strided maps, empty and ragged regions, integer overflow, overlapping outputs and copy-back. It must demonstrate alias-aware invalidation after center, descriptor, measure, precision, source and companion changes, preserving exact logical entry maps and independent companion shapes. Tests compare equivalent compatible layouts without asserting unmeasured allocation or throughput benefits.

Plan and binding evidence must reject hidden invalid original operations, missing or changed declarations, incompatible kinds/roles/axes, wrongly typed zeros, unsupported uncertainty modes and unplanned structural changes. It must distinguish reusable operator factors from point-specific residuals, budgets and certificates, and retain successful, refused, failed, blocked and inactive records without losing other points. Exact arithmetic or independent enclosures must check the resistor means and shared-source cross blocks, aggregation, covariance selection and conflicting source declarations, alongside a point-local binding refusal and a global schema refusal. A declared variant or parameterized assembly must exercise structural variation as well as ordinary value sweeps.

Replay evidence must preserve point/node order, sample realization, policies, versions and source provenance in a pinned environment, with reductions respecting their declared order. Compare Cartesian, sampled and listed bindings against individually bound evaluations, and verify that absent correlations never become zero blocks. The scalar, bindings, matrix and physics responsibilities remain those of (`tab:vision:package-structure`); implementation enters the existing bites through (`dec:nrz:computation-model-implementation-boundary`). Hardware targeting, parallel scheduling, a full statistical engine and new numerical domains require their own decisions and are not established by this specification.
