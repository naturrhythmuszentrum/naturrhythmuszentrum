# nrz-bindings · `guide:bindings:overview`

The bindings package interprets `nrz-cas` original occurrences through data-time quantity descriptors. It depends on `nrz-cas` and no numerical engine package. Descriptors describe values independently of storage. The matrix core owns numerical regions and binds immutable bindings evidence to their concrete versions (see the matrix-core design).

**Definition (Quantity descriptors)** · `def:bindings:quantity-descriptors`

A descriptor combines a seven-axis SI dimension, an explicit semantic kind, an exact measure and an affine point or difference role. Dimensions use budgeted CAS rational arithmetic, with signed 64-bit numerators and unsigned 64-bit denominators as an explicit dimension admission boundary; an integral-only policy can reject fractional results. Kind identity is declared, never inferred from equal dimension vectors. Named kinds bind invariant dimensions in a registry. Nested kind relationships have a depth limit of 128.

**Rule (Exact measure conversion)** · `rule:bindings:measure-conversion`

A measure maps coordinates to coherent coordinates by a checked positive finite CAS expression and a rational offset. `Scale::Exact` supplies a rational literal; `Scale::Expression` supplies an owned `ExactConstant`. `Measure::new` requires a `PositiveConstant` witness reconstructed from that exact value. Reporting names and unchecked `Scale::Symbolic` text provide no evidence; the latter still returns `UnsupportedMeasureScale`. Zero, negative and unproved-positive expressions return `InvalidScale`. `Scale::pi` uses the typed CAS circle constant, without a numerical approximation.

The closed constant grammar admits rational literals, pi, e, negation, arithmetic and integer powers. The CAS checks every original operand for finite real definedness before normalization, including divisors hidden by zero or cancellation; free symbols and unsupported operators refuse. Strict signs, exact rational subresults and same-sign sums establish positivity conservatively. A mixed expression such as `pi-1` remains unproved even though a stronger theorem could establish it. Existing CAS polynomial normalization and an independent coefficient equality check produce immutable exact graph values. Equality uses normalized structure, not names or a complete transcendental identity decision; rational literals retain their existing arithmetic ledgers.

Conversion checks dimension, kind and role before composing scales in the CAS. For example `(pi/180)/(2*pi)` reduces to the exact Rational `1/360`. Measure origins remain rational; a point conversion's shift may be irrational because it divides the origin difference by the target scale. Differences have exact zero shifts and zero measure offsets. `Conversion::apply_exact` accepts source-free exact coordinates; `apply` additionally requires its exact result to be rational. Neither API accepts stored heap values. Companion products, variances, formal sensitivity quotients and original coordinate checking retain the exact scale graph.

**Rule (Registered angle and frequency relations)** · `rule:bindings:angle-frequency-relations`

`Registry::with_angle_frequency` explicitly installs `rad`, `degree`, `turn`, `Hz`, `rad/s` and `s^-1`. Degrees have exact scale `pi/180` and turns `2*pi`; all six are differences with zero offset. `Kind::frequency`, `Kind::angular_frequency` and `Kind::rate` declare three distinct meanings at the same inverse-time dimension. The empty registry remains empty, and `register_measure` only registers a checked descriptor: a reporting key such as `turn` cannot create a physical relation.

`Registry::frequency_relation` checks the distinct source and target kinds, difference roles and the registered full-cycle definition. Its private `FrequencyRelation` retains both actual coordinate descriptors, the typed turn angle, direction and exact CAS conversion. Forward coordinates multiply by the full-cycle angle and reverse coordinates divide by it; both endpoint measure scales participate. Thus `1000/pi Hz` becomes exactly `2000 rad/s`, and the inverse restores the exact original. `apply_exact` additionally checks the captured source schema, while ordinary same-kind conversion continues to refuse hertz versus angular frequency and derivative rate. The catalog's original frequency-times-angle multiplication rules support checked equations with retained source declarations; inverse cyclic interpretation uses the explicit full-cycle relation. Generic angular-frequency divided by angle retains the derivative-rate kind.

**Rule (Semantic operations)** · `rule:bindings:semantic-operations`

Addition and subtraction require compatible kinds and the affine role table. Point minus point is a difference; point plus difference and difference plus point are points; point plus point, difference minus point and multiplicative point operations are refused. Products and powers require explicit kind rules beyond the standard continuous ratio and uncertainty relationships. Rules are ordered, checked against dimension arithmetic and rejected on duplicate registration. Multiplication does not consult the commuted registration: a registered rule for count multiplied by ratio applies to `n*2`, while `2*n` uses count scaling unless its own ordered rule is registered. Registry declarations are assumptions about physical meaning, not proofs of them.

**Definition (Count domains)** · `def:bindings:count-domains`

A count has a declared thing and an integer domain, remains distinct from a continuous ratio and from amount of substance, and uses scale one with zero offset. Imports must be integers in the domain. Signed and nonnegative domains have conservative closure rules; bounded domains refuse unproved arithmetic. Known whole-number scaling checks domain closure. Dividing counts requires `count_ratio` and preserves divisor definedness as an original operation. No count is silently rounded or promoted to a continuous value.

**Definition (Uncertainty descriptors)** · `def:bindings:uncertainty-descriptors`

Radius, standard uncertainty, relative radius, relative standard uncertainty, variance and covariance have distinct kinds and their own measures. Absolute companions use difference dimensions and scales; variance squares them, and covariance takes the product of the two values' difference dimensions and scales. Relative descriptors require a compatible declared nonzero difference reference; the general descriptor constructor refuses a raw relative kind, and construction refuses a relative kind nested within any other uncertainty kind. Explicit relative conversion checks that reference, while ordinary division by a ratio coefficient preserves the uncertainty kind. Descriptor-only multiplication of standard uncertainties does not infer a variance, because value identity is unavailable at that layer. A standard uncertainty of a count is continuous, while the value remains discrete. These declarations provide neither numerical uncertainty propagation nor a proof of an assurance claim.

**Rule (Original expression checks)** · `rule:bindings:original-expression-checks`

`Context` owns one `Cas` store and an immutable semantic context. Mathematical declaration keys and function sorts are supplied before parsing, including forward references. `Context::parse` passes text to `source::parse_original` against `Cas::context_header`. `Context` accepts inputs, typed exact constants, metadata, derived observables, independent uncertainty companions, uncertainty inputs bound to named values, and fixed function signatures. It uses complete `Cas::walk_original` walks and `Cas::describe_syntax` to check every original operand before any coordinate normalization, including operands beneath zero, cancellation and zero powers. Bare constants are ratios; even zero never supplies a missing physical descriptor. Typed constants provide physical zeros and checked whole-count imports. Metadata cannot become a numerical operand. Derived declarations resolve forward references with cycle checks and bounded expansion; `check_all` also validates unused declarations. Equations check both sides, and homogeneous block checks validate each scalar entry without applying scalar commutativity to matrix operations. Equation diagnostic paths begin with 0 for the left side or 1 for the right; block paths begin with the entry index.

`Context::with_real_symbols(registry, limits, keys)` supplies real scalar mathematical declarations for bindings-only callers, using each key as its display name and delegating to `Context::new`. It retains the standard functions and the supplied physical checking limits. The existing `Limits` and `LimitsOverride` types are re-exported for explicit bindings-only client policies. Callers add physical declarations normally, then freeze the context; missing mathematical keys still refuse parsing and freezing. Duplicate keys and builtin collisions retain the ordinary constructor refusals. Explicit function sorts remain available through `Context::new`.

**Convention (Function signatures)** · `conv:bindings:function-signatures`

sin, cos and tan take angle differences in radians and return ratios; asin, acos and atan reverse that relationship; exp and log take and return ratios. sqrt checks a rational power, while `count_ratio` checks an explicit count relation and retains a quotient coordinate expression. Other applications need an ordered fixed signature; their arguments and result receive exact conversions to and from the signature's declared measures. Numerical domains of logarithms, inverse functions and roots remain obligations of the original expression, not conclusions of dimensional checking.

**Definition (Checking evidence)** · `def:bindings:checking-evidence`

A successful check owns its original expression, postorder node descriptors, exact conversion maps and the declarations it used, including source and assurance claims. A requested output descriptor additionally records conversion from coherent coordinates to that output measure. `Context::prove_equal` submits a `Task::Equal` request for checked coherent coordinates and an explicit original target. The simplifier returns a proved, conditional, refused or unfinished outcome; conditional equality retains its residual domain obligations. A coordinate equality carries no physical proof; a zero coordinate retains its descriptor in the checked result. Normalization cannot be passed back as a substitute for original operations. Refusals carry a typed reason, operand path, optional byte span, declaration chain, expected descriptor or operation constraint, and the descriptors successfully derived at the failing operation. Entering an observable body appends operand index 0; its node evidence and declaration failures use that same path and the body's span. Uncertainty inputs establish declared value, optional second-value and reference symbol identities. Literal coefficients and declared typed ratio constants preserve the relationship under supported scaling; scaling by another ratio quantity requires propagation. Sums carrying the same companion evidence and supported powers preserve identity, while multiplying standard uncertainties gives a variance only for the same original uncertainty symbol. A derived alias carries its body's existing companion and evidence; the alias name does not establish a new companion value identity. Equal dimensions alone establish no identity. Relative quotients must use the declared reference symbol as the divisor. For ratio-valued uncertainties, division by a ratio is scaling unless the divisor is the bound value or the companion explicitly names it as its reference. Relative inputs require the relationship-bearing declaration; ordinary inputs, constants, derived declarations and fixed function results cannot introduce an unbound relative kind.

An uncertainty declaration for a derived value may explicitly propagate from companions of that value or symbols in its resolved dependency closure. A covariance declaration may use companions from either value's closure. The checker verifies the body's declared descriptor, requires every uncertainty operand to carry a checked companion inside those closures, and records `CompanionEvidence::Propagated` with the contributing uncertainty symbols and their relationships. Original operands remain contributors even under zero factors or cancellation. `CompanionEvidence::Established` distinguishes the relationships supplied by inputs and preserved by the supported identity operations. A propagated companion binds its declared target for later variance and relative checks; relative conversion uses a bound absolute companion and the declared compatible nonzero reference. Propagation evidence establishes admissible dependencies and physical kinds, not correctness of a numerical propagation formula, independence assumptions, correlation values or assurance claims. Numerical nonzero proofs remain outside this contract.

**Rule (Frozen declaration evidence)** · `rule:bindings:frozen-declaration-evidence`

`Context::freeze` checks every numerical declaration through `check_all`, including unused bodies, original operands hidden under zero or cancellation, and companion relationships. It consumes the builder into `CheckedDeclarations`, which retains the actual CAS store, original occurrences, registry, function signatures, metadata, declared provenance and immutable checking results. Read-only accessors expose that inventory and permit original-syntax inspection through `cas`; no mutable semantic context escapes. A snapshot establishes descriptor validity, not numerical assurance or source independence.

`SnapshotId` combines the CAS context identity with a monotonic semantic revision. The context exclusively owns its initial operation registry; each successful declaration or function addition advances the revision covering its rules, descriptors and evidence. Failed additions leave it unchanged, and exhaustion refuses before mutation. Parsing and checking do not change the semantic revision. Every `CheckedExpression` captures this ID. `validate_checked` distinguishes foreign contexts from stale revisions, and `prove_equal` refuses both before constructing a new request. Equal schemas in another context never identify the same snapshot. IDs and cloned checks alone do not retain graph storage: clients keep the frozen owner while inspecting originals or witnesses. This adds a reusable evidence boundary without changing existing parse, declaration, checking or docgen call signatures (see the matrix-core design).

**Rule (Layout-free companion pairing)** · `rule:bindings:companion-pairing`

`PairingWitness::uncertainty` derives componentwise radius and standard schemas, variance schemas and ordered covariance/cross-covariance entry schemas through the existing scalar uncertainty rules. Covariance dimensions add both target dimensions, its measure multiplies both difference scales, and its kind retains both target kinds. All companion offsets are zero. `NamedReference` requires a nonempty declaration key, a difference descriptor and an explicit nonzero assumption. Relative radius and relative standard schemas retain that reference; a missing, mismatched or extra reference refuses. `validate_targets` and `validate_reference` check exact captured schemas and reference declarations. They do not resolve concrete value identities, indices, extents, sources or versions (see the matrix-core design).

`PairingWitness::sensitivity` derives an output-over-input dimension and difference-scale quotient, retaining both original target schemas, including point roles and origins. It creates a formal relation rather than a scalar named kind or a registry multiplication rule. `check_entry` checks a supplied scalar coefficient through the existing registry before returning its exact coordinate map; uncertainty entries must have the derived uncertainty kind. Discrete count targets and unnamed relative sensitivity coordinates refuse; a separately declared continuous quantity may have its own sensitivity. A schema alone does not establish a derivative formula. Native symbolic derivative witnesses are supplied by the checked sensitivity recipes described below (see the matrix-core design).

`contract_covariance` checks both input sides of one descriptor term in `J_left U J_right^T`, accepting covariance or a variance with its repeated target. It derives the paired output covariance schema without inferring independence or performing a numerical contraction. `contract_radius` accepts only an absolute deterministic radius. Wrong modes and target-side mismatches refuse even for equal dimensions; changed measures, affine origins or roles require explicitly rederived pairings. Matrix owns the ordered maps, actual derivative evidence, coverage, source model and numerical propagation.

**Definition (Parameter evaluation radius)** · `def:bindings:parameter-evaluation-radius`

`EvaluationDescriptor` targets a checked companion or sensitivity parameter itself. It retains the full pairing and supplies `EvaluationKind::Radius`, the parameter's own dimension and its zero-offset difference measure. Thus covariance evaluation error has the product dimension of that covariance, with both target sides retained; it cannot become a bound on either physical value. An evaluated relative parameter retains its named reference through explicit evaluation-descriptor semantics. No nested relative scalar kind is admitted, and no conversion into an ordinary scalar uncertainty descriptor is exposed. The matrix owner binds this schema to the parameter region and version and certifies the numerical bound (see the matrix-core design).

**Rule (Checked native sensitivities)** · `rule:bindings:native-sensitivities`

`Context::check_sensitivity` consumes a builder after checking every declaration and every supplied original operand. `SensitivityRecipe::Square` accepts a square of one declared continuous input; `Reciprocal` accepts its negative first power or a literal-one quotient. Arbitrary equivalent formulas and user-supplied derivatives are not executable recipes. Rows retain the caller's original order and columns retain distinct declared input keys, including requested independent coordinates with formal zero partials. Derived symbols, discrete counts, metadata, missing operands and duplicate coordinates refuse. Original physical checking still rejects unregistered kind operations, point multiplication and hidden invalid operands before any symbolic transformation.

The adapter submits the checked coherent-coordinate expressions through the public CAS Jacobian request on an explicit context domain. It inspects the replayed theorem's context, layer, domain, coordinate order, source column and result shape, then converts the CAS row enumeration to logical column order. Every partial is additionally proved equal to the fixed native coefficient recipe. `PairingWitness::sensitivity` supplies the output-over-input descriptor, including formal derivative zeros; an original untyped physical zero receives no new exemption. Input measure scales participate in the chain rule, and outputs use coherent coordinates. Square evaluates its derivative as `(2*c)*s` and reciprocal as `-s/(c*c)`, where `c=s*x` and `s` is the input's exact measure scale.

`CheckedSensitivity` privately owns the frozen declarations, original checks, graph witnesses, ordered descriptors and `SensitivityKernel` entries. It exposes read-only evidence and unresolved `SensitivityObligation` values, with no numerical evaluation API or arbitrary checked-state constructor. Reciprocal proofs use explicit symbolic nonzero hypotheses; their qualified provenance remains in the witnesses, and numerical nonzero/separated-divisor obligations remain even when a declaration already claims nonzero. Only complete discharged CAS results can enter the schedule; conditional, unfinished, unsupported and exhausted outcomes remain typed refusals with their outcome retained. A symbolic premise is never a numerical certificate, and the numerical owner must enclose coefficient evaluation before using a sensitivity (see the matrix-core design).

For larger closed recipes, `Context::with_cas_limits` permits explicit per-store CAS ceilings without changing global defaults or the ordinary constructor. Each derivative and equality request still requires explicit `LimitsOverride` work and replay allowances, capped by that store. Physical checks retain their existing ledger. Sensitivity graph construction resolves the symbolic policy into a separate ledger shared across the lowering phase; each service request has its own generation and replay ledger. `JacobianEvidence::work` reports derivative-phase construction work, per-request generation/replay peaks and completed request count; it grants no additional work or numerical assurance.

The closed-recipe conformance profile explicitly allows 16,000,000 construction/generation work units and 16,000,000 replay work units, with all other CAS ceilings unchanged. Measured square and reciprocal request peaks were respectively 1,002,411 / 4,517,336 and 762,132 / 4,317,129 generation/replay units. Tests measure each recipe afresh, require at least twofold profile headroom, succeed at the exact observed replay limit and refuse one unit below it. This profile is an explicit client choice for the closed fixtures, not a promise that arbitrary larger schedules fit; size or work exhaustion remains a typed refusal.

**Rule (Positive-root sensitivity)** · `rule:bindings:positive-root-sensitivity`

`Context::check_root_sensitivity` accepts original `sqrt(input)` calls and distinct declared output-placeholder keys. It checks the root kind and the reverse squared-root relation, constructs every original operation of `t²-x`, checks that relation physically, and requests its polynomial partials in root-placeholder order followed by independent-input order. Every returned partial is checked against its fixed coefficient, with input and placeholder measure scales included. The named root call itself never enters the unsupported CAS derivative grammar. Implicit partials use one explicit `Task::Differentiate` request per scalar cell; `JacobianEntry::witness_index` associates each cell with its exact proof in `JacobianEvidence::witnesses`. The adapter checks every source, coordinate and domain before retaining that association.

The final `SensitivityKernel::PositiveRoot` follows the fixed positive-root implication `2*t*dt=dx`, giving `s/(2*t)` for a declared input scale `s` and coherent positive output `t`. `SensitivityObligation::PositiveRoot` requires the numerical owner to enclose that exact root and establish strict positivity and a smooth neighborhood. A zero operating input cannot discharge this obligation even though polynomial partials exist there. `originals` retains the original root calls, `jacobian` retains the checked polynomial relations and replayed partials, and `auxiliary_symbols` identifies the output placeholders. These placeholders are not independent input columns or new statistical sources. Bindings supplies neither an implicit-function theorem from CAS nor a numerical domain certificate (see the matrix-core design).

**Rule (Solve sensitivity)** · `rule:bindings:solve-sensitivity`

`Context::check_solve_sensitivity` validates a `SolveSymbols` inventory for a positive square order and a positive number of right-hand sides. Operator, right-hand-side and solution-placeholder entries use logical column order and distinct declared continuous keys. Bindings builds and checks each original scalar relation `sum_k A_ik X_kj - B_ij` with a first-product initialization and an increasing-index left fold. It then differentiates those relations in `(vec(X), vec(A), vec(B))` order and checks every coefficient against the fixed product-rule table. Original entry kinds and roles remain authoritative before any zero or cancellation; matched extents alone cannot admit a physical contraction.

Final sensitivity columns use `(vec(A), vec(B))` and output rows use coherent `vec(X)`. Each `SensitivityKernel::Solve` selects the appropriate entry from a differential solve. In increasing input-column order, the numerical owner encloses `delta_B-delta_A*X_0`, including the selected input's exact measure scale, and solves with the same verified nominal operator. `Invertible` and `NominalSolution` obligations require a common invertibility certificate and an enclosure of the exact nominal `X_0`. A rounded candidate is insufficient. The 4-by-12 partial fixture measured 3,098,125 construction, 5,464,087 generation and 3,064,645 replay work units over 96 requests. Generation is its largest phase; its boundary test uses bounded fresh-run bisection to distinguish the required proof work from optional response encoding, then checks exact-limit success and one-below exhaustion. This preserves a common operator across all right-hand sides; it does not assert source independence, covariance validity or numerical solve accuracy. Aliased symbol inventories must first be expressed through the matrix owner's explicit structural maps (see the matrix-core design).

**Rule (Exact descriptor constant exports)** · `rule:bindings:descriptor-constant-exports`

`CheckedDeclarations::conversion_map` checks both descriptor schemas and constructs a private `CheckedMap` for exact measure conversion. `normalization_map` instead resolves a strictly positive exact difference reference from `Declaration::Constant`, and requires a compatible named exact point origin for a point value. Runtime inputs, derived observables and nonpositive references refuse; counts and unnamed relative values cannot enter normalization. Difference maps have zero shifts, and `inverse` restores the same reference and origin. Reference and origin identity remains distinct even when their exact coordinates coincide.

A `MapIdentity` retains the frozen snapshot, source and target descriptors, map purpose/direction and exact named reference/origin definitions. `DescriptorConstant` retains that identity and its scale or shift role. Its bounded `export` method produces an integer or one literal fraction only when the exact coefficient is rational, with explicit output-byte and arithmetic/work refusals. `exact` exposes the retained CAS constant. Non-rational coefficients return `UnitError::UnsupportedExactConstantExport`; the same named refusal protects rational-only matrix schema coefficients, native sensitivity schedules and the version-two docgen schema. A numerical decoder encloses that spelling once per map, precision and arithmetic revision. There is no map evaluator accepting stored coordinates, no scalar or matrix dependency, and no numerical value sent to CAS. Exact scales and offsets, numerical conversion error and physical source uncertainty remain separate (see the matrix-core design).

`ReferenceDefinition` and `OriginDefinition` also carry immutable source-free schema definitions without adding declarations or changing a frozen snapshot. `ReferenceDefinition::unit` defines one unit of an exact difference measure; `new` permits an explicit checked positive finite exact coordinate, including a CAS constant expression, and refuses unchecked text or unproved positivity. `OriginDefinition::new` defines the zero coordinate of a point measure. Reuse these objects when adapting matrix reference and point-origin definitions: cloning preserves identity, while independently constructing even identical labels and coordinates creates distinct identities. `reference_definition` and `origin_definition` resolve existing frozen constants instead and retain their exact snapshot authority.

`CheckedDeclarations::schema_normalization_map` exports a scalar map from those definitions with explicit arithmetic limits. For named definitions it returns exactly the same map as `normalization_map`. `quotient_normalization_map` accepts an entry's bindings `PairingWitness` plus its ordered row and column reference definitions, checks both original descriptors, and exports the coherent physical map with scale `column/row`. Both exact coordinates and measure scales participate. An optional compatible point origin is admitted only with ratio columns and affects centers; exporting the same quotient without an origin supplies its zero-shift difference map. The inverse restores the same definitions, and its shift supplies the coherent exact origin for a matrix RHS correction. Matrix retains correction placement, signs and operator multiplication; bindings performs none of those numerical operations.

`MapIdentity::source` and `target` expose `MapCoordinates`: `descriptor` returns a scalar descriptor when present, and `quotient` returns the formal ordered pairing otherwise. Formal coordinates are coherent and retain their explicit point or difference role; they never acquire an invented scalar kind. Map identity includes both reference definitions, the origin and the frozen owner. A definition resolved in another snapshot refuses, even at identical coordinates. Schema coefficient arithmetic uses one caller-limited ledger; existing descriptor validation retains its own bounded checks, and exact string export retains its separate byte/work budget. No matrix dependency, heap data or numerical evaluation is introduced.

**Rule (Homogeneous symbol admission)** · `rule:bindings:homogeneous-symbol-admission`

`HomogeneousRecipe::check` checks every original concrete entry under an explicit entry limit, retaining physical kind, coherent measure and difference role. Points, formal quotients and unequal kinds refuse even at equal dimensions; empty rectangles use ratio meaning. This is a closed copy recipe predicate, not a requirement on floating storage.

`BindingSymbols::declare` prepares a homogeneous matrix-valued CAS symbol and optional deterministic-bound and numerical-error symbols before numerical allocation. Each `QuantitySymbol` retains its mathematical sort, physical descriptor, distinct `SymbolKind`, actual CAS node and graph owner. Both companions have the target's difference units, with separate `CompanionKind` values and checked ordered edges to the exact value symbol. These edges state obligations; they prove neither the supplied bound nor a numerical enclosure.

The graph uses CAS free declarations and the closed `SymbolRelation` kinds `Copy`, `DeterministicBound` and `NumericalError`. Each association retains the new symbol followed by its exact source or target, even across independently admitted declaration headers. CAS checks distinct prior Free endpoints, closed scopes, Boolean result sort, exact result-header ownership, Real matrix sorts and companion extent agreement; bindings checks physical kinds, measures and relation inventory. These edges provide no equation, normalization, solving or numerical authority. Ordinary header-local function applications would accumulate and recompare every earlier declaration, so each bundle instead has a bounded independent header and fixed-width symbol names. The read-only `QuantitySymbol::cas` accessor permits inspection of the actual graph edges.

`copy_result` derives fresh result and companion symbols before execution. `HomogeneousRecipe::check_copy` checks the input and result meanings, companion kinds and targets, and exact source identity. A copied selection retains the source symbol; the numerical owner must separately validate its ordered region selection, extents, live versions and complete companion inventory. Swapped companion roles, a foreign companion and a result derived from another source refuse. `check_copy` returns a sealed `CopyWitness` retaining both symbol inventories. `SymbolBinding<T>::check` joins that meaning to opaque numerical-region tickets and refuses missing, extra or repeated region owners. The numerical client must validate every ticket under its live execution lease. The symbols and tickets alone grant no numerical access, representation proof or source independence.

Graph admission consumes one caller-supplied CAS `Budget` across all construction phases and uses its existing limits and charges. The fixed companion inventory has at most three symbols; homogeneous entry admission checks its full iterator length before and during traversal. Independent numerical imports require `check_import`; a derived result must be bound through its checked publication. Public tests in `tests/conformance/admission.rs` cover the exact entry and cumulative CAS work boundaries, with success at N and refusal at N minus one. This subset does not implement general shaped descriptors, covariance/source graphs or total ownership across a client's heap.

**Caveat (Validation boundary)** · `cav:bindings:validation-boundary`

Numerical quantity types belong above the matrix heap; bindings remains a storage-independent descriptor and symbolic evidence layer. It does not integrate numerical matrices, evaluate expressions, prove function domains, prove source or assurance claims, infer semantic kinds from dimensions, or migrate legacy problem data. Exact arithmetic and bounded original-tree expansion can refuse inputs. Original walks and coordinate construction share a finite check ledger, with separate occurrence and expression-size ceilings; conversions and inlined observables can exceed those limits even when the physical operations are valid. Coordinate drafts check depth and unfolded size at each original operation, then enter the CAS in one journaled batch whose work depends on those drafts and their actual lookups. Walks charge reached records and their own frames and events, so unrelated retained sources do not consume a later check's work allowance. Observable checks reuse the physical walk's root location and evidence. Each check memoizes successful derived declarations against the immutable declaration snapshot: shared bodies are physically checked, walked and coordinate-built once. Later occurrences reuse the checked result and its coordinate reference, paying for evidence copying and ordered depth checks at their own paths and definition chains. Failed expansions are never cached. Coordinate parents still count the full unfolded size of each operand, and the memo is discarded after each check. Nested roots retain their exact coordinate syntax without requiring a rational normal form. Such failures have the distinct `Failure::Coordinates` reason, separate from physical refusals and original traversal limits. General affine combinations such as `0.5*a + 0.5*b` are refused even when their weights sum to one; `a + (b - a)/2` is supported. The same-symbol variance rule remains syntactic: `(2*u)^2` is supported, while `(2*u)*(2*u)` is refused because the multiplication rule requires bare original symbols. A count domain is part of kind equality. Negation drops declared nonzero evidence, so `sigma/(-reference)` is refused as a relative uncertainty. An unbound plain-input standard uncertainty `sigma/x` with a non-ratio value x is refused for missing companion identity. For an unbound ratio-valued standard uncertainty, `sigma_eff/eff` remains standard uncertainty scaling; a relative quotient requires a bound value or explicit companion reference. The dependency closure is structural: a dependency under a zero factor, as in `z = x + 0*w`, still counts, consistent with original operands remaining contributors. Propagation is admitted for derived values and covariance pairs only; an input value cannot combine its own bound components with a non-literal ratio factor, for example `sqrt(u_a^2 + (eff*u_b)^2)` with both components bound to `x`. The zero power of a named difference is unsupported without an explicit kind rule. Unproved-positive measure scales, unproved count-domain closure and undeclared kind operations have explicit error boundaries.

`Conversion::export_scale` exports signed or zero rational structural coefficients as bounded exact literals for numerical owners, and refuses a non-rational coefficient by name. The quantity stage must enclose pi-dependent constants once per exact map, precision and arithmetic revision, retain their evaluation error, and share eligible invariant evaluations. This stage adds exact descriptor meaning and explicit refusal; it supplies no numerical pi kernel or heap materialization.
