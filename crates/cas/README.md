# nrz-cas · `guide:cas:overview`

## Purpose · `sec:cas:purpose`

`nrz-cas` owns the exact mathematical structure and checked judgments behind the engine's descriptor graph. It keeps symbols, immutable sorts, original expressions, contexts and relations independent of any floating computation. Bindings uses that structure to express physical meanings; matrix executes numerical procedures in a separate graph of region versions and kernel applications. CAS makes exact definitions and proofs reproducible across machines without requiring floating results to have identical bits.

## Place in the engine · `sec:cas:engine`

The dependency direction is matrix → bindings → CAS. CAS depends on no other engine crate; bindings and docgen are its direct workspace clients. It owns exact graph construction, rational and source-free constant algebra, original occurrence coverage, a closed proof kernel, bounded producers and portable expected-judgment replay. Physical kinds, units and representation choices belong to the bindings client; numerical storage, enclosures and kernel execution belong below matrix's numerical boundary.

A descriptor is intended to retain meaning as CAS nodes and checked relations, including axes and representations. The implementation already has scalar sorts, mathematical shape syntax and `SymbolRelation` associations for copy results and deterministic/numerical-error companions. The full graph-backed physical vocabulary belongs to the descriptor-graph step in `docs/design/descriptor-binding.md`. An admitted relation states structure; it is not automatically an equation, a theorem or permission to access a heap.

No heap value enters CAS. Bindings exports a source-free exact constant spelling, scalar encloses it in the requested format, and matrix retains the import error. Exact π and e expressions are available descriptors, but non-rational numerical export still refuses. Exact descriptor identity and checked judgments are machine-independent; process-local handles and allocation order are not portable identities.

## In the LC target · `sec:cas:lc-target`

For `L=25 mH=1/40 H` and `C=10 µF=1/100000 F`, the characteristic equation is `s²+1/(LC)=0`. On the Real stratum `L>0, C>0`, its two Complex roots are `±i/√(LC)=±2000i s⁻¹`. The angular-frequency relation gives `ω₀=2000 rad/s`; the distinct cyclic-frequency relation gives `f₀=1000/π Hz`. CAS supplies exact root selection, membership, factor identity and completeness evidence. Bindings must supply the physical interpretation and retain the original denominator and positivity premises.

The implemented mixed family `z²-r=0` supports the negative-radicand piece needed here, with a positive Real square-root witness and explicit transport from the base stratum. Its independently replayed tests establish that algebraic capability. They do not establish automatic discovery for the newly assembled two-parameter LC problem: the adapter must prove the exact original LC judgment and supply the required positivity transport. The cumulative symbolic policy and exact LC evidence step in `docs/design/lc-resonance.md` owns that integration. Its checked spectrum step adds the numerical spectral theorem; its quantities, pi evaluation and data-time binding step adds certified π evaluation. A symbolic root proof certifies neither a floating eigenvalue enclosure nor a phase branch or trajectory.

## Main concepts and types · `sec:cas:concepts`

`Cas` is an owned exact graph; its `Handle` and `Reference` values retain store identity. `Rational` and `ExactConstant` support bounded exact arithmetic, with `PositiveConstant` witnessing admitted strict positivity. For example, the LC coefficient product remains an exact rational before any numerical import:

```rust
use nrz_cas::{Rational, limits::{Budget, Limits}};

let mut budget = Budget::new(Limits::default())?;
let l = Rational::parse("1/40", &mut budget)?;
let c = Rational::parse("1/100000", &mut budget)?;
let expected = Rational::parse("1/4000000", &mut budget)?;
assert_eq!(l.mul(&c, &mut budget)?, expected);
# Ok::<(), nrz_cas::Fault>(())
```

The public modules organize the graph-to-proof flow:

- `syntax` supplies child-before-parent drafts, operators, sorts and scopes; `Cas::admit` validates them atomically.
- `domain` supplies declaration headers, contexts, exact regions and spaces. Declarations and assumptions remain distinct.
- `source` preserves each original operand occurrence, including inactive or cancellable operands. A complete walk yields a sealed `TraversalReceipt`; the client still checks physical validity.
- `request` pairs originals and their receipts in `CoveredInput`, then names a `Task`, limits and explicit premises. Outcomes distinguish proved, conditional, partial, refused, unsupported and exhausted work.
- `proof` exposes typed judgments, certificates and sealed `VerifiedWitness` values. `check_proof` reconstructs every rule application; `replay` checks portable bytes against an independently supplied expected judgment.
- `solving` supplies exact problems, coefficient domains, root selections, solution sets and checked coverage/presentation. Its capability registry separates represented syntax from executable checkers and producers.
- `limits` supplies finite ceilings and monotone `Budget` ledgers. Store ceilings cap request allowances.

Detailed source grammar and coverage contracts are in `src/source.rs`; proof/domain/replay contracts are in `src/proof.rs`, `src/domain.rs` and `src/wire_answer.rs`. Solving contracts live with the corresponding coefficient, family, tower, elementary and Real modules under `src/solving/`. `src/request.rs` describes search and calculus outcomes; the design arguments are in `docs/design/cas-design.md`, `docs/design/cas-piecewise-design.md` and `docs/design/cas-solving-design.md`.

## Checked selections and reading maps · `sec:cas:readings`

`readings::EntrySelection` checks a finite literal matrix selection against its source rectangle. Entry, ordered list, row, column and block requests retain their exact index sequence; lists may explicitly require column or row order. Bounds, duplicate entries, malformed shapes and violated order contracts have distinct named refusals. Logical shaped coordinates use column order, independently of the row-major storage of an explicit `Operator::Matrix` constructor.

`Cas::entry_set_relation` compares two admitted selection handles over one exact base. Its intersection follows the left selection's order and reports each shared base entry with both original positions. Both containments, set equality and disjointness are separate from `same_order`; equal sets in different orders remain distinct readings. `Cas::entry_coverage(base,selections,budget)` reports uncovered entries and multiply covered entries with their multiplicities in logical column order. Repeated list members count repeatedly; an empty base is covered exactly once by an empty list.

The entry judgments refuse nonselection operands as `SelectionExpected`, different symbols as `SelectionBase`, a reused symbol under a changed declaration snapshot as `SelectionDeclaration`, and different retained definitions of one declaration as `SelectionDefinition`. Foreign handles retain `ForeignHandle`. Neither equal extents nor matching display names authorize comparison, and no numerical value enters these judgments.

Entry judgments publish no graph records. For operand lengths `n,m` and maximum base-key byte length `k`, relation Work is `4+k+n*(n+1)+m*(m+1)+n*m+max(n,m)+min(n,m)`: selection validation, base checks, entry comparisons, order comparison and result reservation. Its staged and output capacity is `16*min(n,m)` bytes. For base size `b` and selection lengths `n_j`, coverage Work is `1+4*b+sum(4+k_j+n_j*(n_j+2))`; the simultaneous count and result arrays reserve `32*b` staged bytes and `24*b` output bytes. The caller's cumulative ledger and store ceilings both apply. Public boundary tests pin Work and both payload ceilings at `N` success and `N−1` refusal, without changing existing operations or their pins.

`readings::AffineMap` stores rational `y=T*z+d` coefficients in row-major order and preserves the coordinate order of both vectors. `compose` computes the ordered matrix contraction and both translation terms; `project` selects output coordinates without sorting. The Complex block expansion has rows `(1,0)`, `(0,1)`, `(0,-1)`, `(1,0)`, retaining the repeated coordinate and sign. Matrix dimensions, all original coefficients, temporary payload and arithmetic are checked against the caller's cumulative ledger.

`Cas::select_entries` retains the exact source expression and declaration snapshot. `Cas::define_reading` constructs a transparent `Operator::AffineReading` with ordered operands `(declared symbol, exact source)` and the complete affine map. A Real scalar has one coordinate, a Complex scalar has `(real,imaginary)`, and a Real matrix has its logical column coordinates. The returned definition is the reading identity; the bare `Free` declaration alone supplies no equation. Physical adapters must retain the complete definition, selected source and context rather than resolving a reading by its display name.

`Cas::prove_reading_equality(context,left,right,budget)` reconstructs both complete reading chains over the same exact free-coordinate symbol and its ordered entries. It composes selections, rational affine definitions, Real/Imag parts, conjugation and transpose, then checks every coefficient and offset. Different free families refuse as `ReadingUnrelated`; disagreeing maps refuse as `ReadingMismatch`; an unsupported or omitted definition refuses as `ReadingDefinition` or loses the common family. Even a zero map must traverse its original source. The checker proves coefficient identity on the whole family; it does not infer structural relations from a center or from arbitrary asserted equations. An unstructured block does not gain repeated-coordinate constraints from a component reading.

This is the ordered affine fragment of the existing `poly.jets-equal` rule, with the same logical weight 4, primitive registry and cost policy. Its closed grammar is total on the declared coordinates, so the resulting scoped `Equality` has the `DefinedAndEqual` contract and no hidden domain residuals. Ordinary ordered congruence can lift the resulting equation through another reading. The supported map coefficients are exact rationals; general nonlinear readings and non-rational coefficient maps remain outside this fragment.

`export_witness` retains both endpoints' complete original syntax closure: symbols and declaration snapshots, selected copies and order, every map and defining equation, the common free-coordinate declaration and the full context/domain. Ordinary `replay` reconstructs and checks it under an independent ledger against an independently built expected `Judgment`. A different repeated copy needs its own expected claim and freshly checked family proof, even when its composed coefficients agree. Changing a sign, order, extent, declaration, domain or defining equation cannot reuse the old claim. Partial-overlap clients project both readings to the corresponding common entries before asking for equality; comparisons of their unrelated components still refuse.

`Cas::reading_dependencies(reading,budget)` and `Cas::witness_dependencies(witness,budget)` return a sealed `DependencyInventory`. Its immutable `DependencyContents` exposes every declaration in each retained header snapshot, every original selection and its ordered entries, every defining affine equation, and all retained context, domain, predicate, family, guarded-obligation, statement and proof records. The canonical record table is the same table used by witness export, with its existing schemas and no additional wire format. Public record indices refer only to that table and never authorize graph lookups. Inventory equality compares portable contents; fresh replay and a different local allocation order produce identical inventories. Witness extraction checks the retained statement, hypotheses and canonical bytes against the authenticated proof root; altered caches refuse as `DependencyWitness`, and foreign evidence retains `ForeignHandle`.

The structural inventory and its entry-read union answer different questions. Every original selection and equation remains in the closure, including unused coefficients and entries. To find reads, CAS composes the demanded output map through the retained affine definitions, selections, Real/Imag parts, conjugation and transpose, and records its exact nonzero input support at every named base. Thus the inventory of `v=x[0]` retains the whole definition of `x` while reading only its first entry. A whole state reading reads both entries. A scalar has one entry `(0,0)`; both Real coordinates of a Complex scalar refer to that entry. Unsupported semantic sources refuse as `ReadingDefinition`, so an incomplete inventory never authorizes disjointness. Complete declaration snapshots include declarations that have no demanded entries; a declaration alone is not an entry read.

`Cas::dependency_selection_intersection` accepts an admitted selection write, and `Cas::dependency_entry_intersection` accepts a base handle and an explicit ordered list of distinct entries. Both return touched entries in logical column order. They authenticate the inventory's private store owner and every write handle before comparing the retained base identities. Another declared base is disjoint; a reused symbol with a changed snapshot or definition retains the named entry-base refusals. Duplicate and out-of-range raw writes refuse even on an unrelated base, while an empty write is valid. The inventory exposes no mutation or caller-supplied dependency constructor.

Inventory extraction reuses canonical table generation, exact map composition, coefficient validation and the existing rational arithmetic charges. Each additional view copy prepays its retained payload, each index lookup and membership scan prepays its compared entries, and canonical sorting prepays the square of its element count. A conservative meter includes canonical records, lookup descriptors, typed views, copied declarations/maps and growing index vectors; retained data reduces the temporary allowance of every nested encoding or composition. The same cumulative reservation is checked against staged and output payload ceilings. Write intersection charges selection validation or `n*(n+1)` raw-list validation, base lookup and key comparisons, `r*n` entry comparisons and `min(r,n)` output reservation; its result capacity is `8*min(r,n)` bytes. All four public operations have `N`/`N−1` Work, staged-payload and output-payload tests and preserve the graph on refusal. Existing proof, export and replay Work pins remain unchanged.

The region-bindings step in `docs/design/lc-resonance.md` can use entry coverage before publication, intersection positions to project overlap judgments, and dependency write intersections to invalidate every affected reading and proof. It must retain each exact base definition and inventory together with its own numerical entry versions. Heap values, versions, lifetime, allocating sponsorship and whole-base fallback remain outside CAS; a failed or unavailable fine inventory supplies no disjointness claim.

The worked public tests prove and replay the Complex block corner equation, both state components, partial overlaps, three pairwise readings through one family, conjugate-block transpose and ordered congruence. Value construction, selection composition, map composition/projection, graph admission, proof construction, export and independent replay have exact cumulative Work boundaries; temporary maps, retained graph payload and witness bytes have matching boundary checks. Construction charges ordered entries and encoded coefficient payload before copying; composition charges each contraction term and the existing rational arithmetic ledger, retaining live coefficients while nested arithmetic uses the remaining temporary allowance. Existing CAS resource pins remain unchanged.

## Guarantees and refusals · `sec:cas:guarantees`

Construction preserves exact syntax, sorts, scopes and original occurrences, and rolls back a failed publication without refunding spent work. Sharing mathematical syntax never merges distinct original uses. A coverage receipt proves that all operands were visited; it proves neither their physical validity nor their mathematical definedness. Operands beneath zero multiplication and cancellation still participate in checks.

The proof kernel is closed and versioned. Callers may propose untrusted certificates and derived recipes, but cannot register an arbitrary trusted callback. A witness retains its complete context, statement, space, region, qualified hypotheses and residual obligations. Cancellation of `x/x` keeps `x != 0`; a conditional result cannot hide that premise. A selected root, a region annotation or a constructor alone supplies no existence or completeness theorem.

Portable `NRZCAS04` replay re-admits syntax, rechecks every primitive, verifies canonical bytes and compares the complete expected claim. `Cas::replay_answer` checks one presentation closure and obtains its matching reachable coverage without replaying shared applications twice. Both returned witnesses publish atomically. A different valid theorem still fails the caller's expected target. Old wire versions, changed premises, missing dependencies, foreign handles, malformed scopes and resource exhaustion refuse explicitly.

Search producers are bounded proposals, with separate cumulative generation and checking ledgers. Exact `N` success and `N−1` refusal are part of their contract. Failed exploration is not a theorem of impossibility; an unfinished search does not prove there is no better route. Logical search cost is distinct from measured runtime and from mathematical witness authority.

## Current scope and limits · `sec:cas:scope`

Implemented services include bounded equality/simplification, polynomial expansion, scalar derivatives and ordered Jacobians, rational square-free layers, native piecewise and finite boundary/split/germ calculus, and the documented distribution fragment. Solving includes complete symbolic Complex cubics with radical presentation, Real cubics with radicals or counted Real cells, and explicit Real-base/Complex-fibre pieces. Original declarations, degree drops, strict divisors and exact contexts remain part of each claim.

General higher-degree radical discovery, arbitrary nonlinear systems, general analytic branches and the represented `root.isolate` and `solve.local-branch` schemas remain unavailable. The available Real cell checkers do not make those separate schemas executable. A requested Real radical presentation can refuse while preserving independently checked coverage. No symbolic service evaluates physical quantities, floating spectra or numerical π constants.

The design permits several symbols to read the same floats through checked related bindings: for example a Complex block and a Real corner, or the state `(v,i)` and its components. CAS now proves the exact affine reading relations and replays their complete scoped witnesses; it never allocates those floats. The migrated homogeneous binding lifecycle still enforces one symbol owner per migrated region, and general shared readings and representation graphs remain later binding work. The versioned bindings and meaning-free kernel step owns heap/kernel migration; existing CAS matrix syntax alone does not implement that lowering.

## Testing · `sec:cas:testing`

Independent integer, rational, factor and sign oracles check exact results. Fresh receiving stores rebuild the expected original judgment before witness replay; mutation tests change source, sort, scope, domain, premise, wire bytes and coverage. Budget tests pin exact work, payload and witness boundaries, including cumulative complete-answer replay. Public lifecycles cover construction, original walks, solving or calculus, export and independent replay. Static inspection is not execution evidence; the verification gate also runs strict Clippy, workspace tests, rustdoc and documentation checks.

Place each test in the outermost layer that can express its assertions. A unit test stays inline under `#[cfg(test)] mod tests` only when it needs a value private to that module. It builds its own setup and never imports the harness. A crate test needs crate-internal access and lives in `src/tests/`, with one file per concern and a `#[cfg(test)] mod tests;` declaration in `src/lib.rs`. An item visible as `pub(crate)` is not module-private. Public tests live under `tests/`.

Public tests use only the crate's public API. `tests/conformance/` checks documented laws, refusals, boundaries, resource limits and determinism. `tests/integration/` exercises complete client computations and proof lifecycles across capabilities, such as constructing a graph, obtaining coverage, searching or differentiating, exporting a witness and replaying it in a fresh store. Classify a test by its principal question or worked flow; setup calls and replay helpers alone do not determine its kind.

Both directories are modules of the single `tests/public.rs` target, with one file per concern. `cargo test -p nrz-cas` includes them without feature flags; `cargo test -p nrz-cas --test public conformance::` and `cargo test -p nrz-cas --test public integration::` select the two public kinds. Both kinds retain the inventory area `integration`, with one existing acute-accent label as each test's final documentation line and a hyphenated function-name slug.

The shared public harness lives in `src/testing/`, included through the private `src/testing/public.rs` entrypoint by source path. Its `test_api` alias refers to the ordinary public library, and it includes no crate-only fixture. Each harness file documents which test binaries compile it. Helpers are absent from ordinary library builds and rustdoc, and the access route adds no feature or dependency. The default, graph, proof and derivation budget profiles remain distinct; test-specific refusal limits stay explicit. Shared setup preserves admission batches, original occurrences, independent oracles and fresh-ledger boundaries. The square-free fixture accepts only a discharged `Proved` result.

The library test binary privately includes `src/testing/mod.rs`, which composes shared public basics and crate-only frontier and frozen-search fixtures. `src/testing/common.rs` is compiled in both binaries. Crate-only fixture files are never reachable from the public entrypoint.

The linter derives each inventory area from the test's location: inline unit tests carry `test:unit`, crate tests under `src/tests/` carry `test:crate`, and public conformance and integration tests carry `test:integration`. Every label is the final documentation line and keeps its acute-accent delimiters.
